## Summary

The client's combo is a named, fixed bundle of goods sold for one price, and its shape is decided by what the client does with it. **The wire is exploded**: adding a combo writes one cart line per component, each tagged `promotionCode: "combo"` with the combo's id (`components/j-combo/format/format.js:169-190`), the cart endpoint re-groups those lines for display (`pagesF/cart/cart.js:273-315`), and the order surfaces show nothing combo-shaped at all — `grep combo` over the order pages returns one unrelated image name. Every per-component quantity is multiplied out on the way in (`format.js:108,174,202`), and the group is atomic in every operation: one stepper, one tick that sends every line (`cart.js:510-521`), and deleting the group deletes every line (`cart.js:522-553`). `fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “A cart line carries the shopper's tick”) already treats that as real cart structure.

So the plan's headline is a modelling decision, and it is the one that makes the inventory's "stock question" dissolve rather than need an answer: **a bundle is a composition plus a discount, not a variant.** Modelling it as a sellable variant would force it to have its own price, weight, tax, stock, reservation, fulfilment and review surface — six things the client's own exploded wire proves it does not have, and six things core would each have to be taught. Modelling it as composition-plus-discount leaves every one of them exactly where core already puts it: the components are ordinary line items, so stock is theirs, reservation is theirs through the existing primitive, weight is the sum `Stock::Package` already computes (`6.1-weight-bracket-shipping-calculator.md:37`), fulfilment and returns are theirs, and a bundle simply has no review surface — which is what the client shows, because its review calls are variant-keyed and the product page renders the components' reviews.

What the bundle owns is the composition, the price rule, and the availability verdict the client renders as one boolean.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **A bundle is not sellable.** It has no price of its own, no stock, no weight, no shipment and no reviews. It is a row that says which variants, in what quantity, form a set, and what that set costs.
- **The lines stay exploded, and the bundle's identity rides a gem-owned join row.** A core column on `spree_line_items` was refused: the fork's rule is that a gem ships its own migrations and patches no core model, and a join table costs nothing at upstream-merge time where a new column on a core table is permanent.
- **The saving is an order-level discount, not a promotion.** The client's own copy says the combo price "与满减满赠等活动不同享" (`pagesA/goods-details/normal/normal.wxml:55`) yet tags every line `promotionCode: "combo"` — the tag is how the cart re-groups, not how the price is computed. Promoting it would submit the bundle to the promotion engine's best-action-wins reconciliation, which clears rows it did not choose; a discount the gem owns is not subject to that, which is the same reasoning the points plan used when it refused a promotion action (`fork-decisions.md` 2026-09-17).
- **Availability is `min` over components, expressed as the client's own unit.** The client compares against a *combo* quantity and shows 套餐商品不足, 无法购买 below a threshold (`miniprogram/static/utils/comboLimit.js:99-101,121-125`), which only works if the server returns `min(floor(component_available / component_quantity))` rather than a sum or a boolean.
- **A component out of stock does not hide the bundle.** The client dims that component and stamps it 已抢光 while the card, its button and its price stand (`pagesA/goods-details/normal/normal.wxml:66-67`, `components/j-combo/goods/goods.wxml:4`), and the only fallback is the 单品购买 tab at single prices. There is therefore no automatic substitution to build, and no partial sale: the group is atomic.
- **One seller per bundle.** A cross-seller bundle would meet `Carts::SplitBySeller`, which partitions on `line_item.seller_id` and re-totals each child order from its own lines (`6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md:372,400-401`) — the bundle's single price has no home in that fan-out, and teaching the splitter to carry one touches the three seller-specific lines the marketplace plan deliberately keeps minimal (`:767`). The gem validates the composition at save time instead.
- **The activity counters are not this plan's.** `limitStockAllQuantity` and `limitStockDayQuantity` are an *activity-scoped pool* the client renders as 活动库存不足 (`comboLimit.js:102-103`), which is the same "独立库存池" the mapping files under 秒杀 (`endpoints-spree-mapping.md:51`). They belong with `6.1-flash-sales.md`, not with composition; the per-customer purchase caps (`limitQuantity`, `limitDayQuantity`) travel with them.
- **`frozen` is not a hold.** Every combo path carries `frozen` and `frozenQuantity`, and they are 冰冻 — a chilled-drink option with its own picker (`components/buy-format/frozen/frozen.wxml`), carried per component through the cart writes. Recorded here because the field name reads like a reservation and a later reader would otherwise reserve stock against it.
- **`goods/getBuyNotCombo` and `goods/getByGroup` are not combo endpoints.** Recorded so the inventory's count stops being re-derived; the first is the ordinary single-goods buy info, the second a paged goods list.

The four rulings behind those decisions, dated because each constrains the others:

- **A bundle is a composition and a discount, not a sellable variant (ruled 2026-09-18).** Everything the inventory calls "the stock question" dissolves under it — stock, weight, reservation, fulfilment, returns and reviews stay with the components and need nothing from core. **Accepted trade-off:** a bundle cannot be priced independently of its components' prices, so a stale component price moves the bundle's price with it; storing the rule rather than the number is what keeps that visible.
- **The saving is an order-level discount of the existing `manual` kind (ruled 2026-09-18).** It exists, it is not subject to the promotion engine's reconciliation, and the plan therefore needs **no core change at all** — rare in this set, where the points plan had to open `Discount::KINDS` for its own kind. **Accepted trade-off:** the kind is shared with merchant-entered adjustments, so the gem marks its own rows rather than identifying them by kind.
- **One seller per bundle (ruled 2026-09-18).** The composition is validated at save time and a cross-seller one is refused, because `Carts::SplitBySeller` re-totals each child order from its own lines and a bundle's single price has no home in that fan-out. **Accepted trade-off:** no cross-store bundles, which the client's own seller-scoped combo data never asks for.
- **The discount is allocated to the component lines proportionally, and the rounding remainder lands on the last line by position (ruled 2026-09-18).** A return of one component therefore reverses its share and the rest keep the bundle price. **Accepted trade-off:** the allocation is a derived figure that must be recomputed identically on every path that touches the group, so it is computed in one place and never re-derived at render time.

## Design Details

### Models

```ruby
create_table :spree_product_bundles do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :seller, index: true            # one seller per bundle, validated at save time
  t.string   :title, null: false
  t.string   :slug, null: false
  t.string   :status, null: false              # draft / active / archived
  t.integer  :position, null: false, default: 0
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

```ruby
create_table :spree_bundle_components do |t|
  t.references :bundle, index: true
  t.references :variant, index: true           # the component, which is an offer carrying its seller
  t.integer  :quantity, null: false, default: 1
  t.timestamps
end

create_table :spree_bundle_line_items do |t|
  t.references :bundle, index: true
  t.references :line_item, index: true, null: false
  t.timestamps
end
```

- `Spree::ProductBundle` inherits `Spree.base_class`, includes `Spree::SingleStoreResource` and `Spree::Metadata`, uses `has_status` and `acts_as_paranoid`, and publishes lifecycle events. `BundleComponent` is unique on `(bundle_id, variant_id)`; the whole composition must belong to one seller and validation refuses a save that would cross one.
- `BundleLineItem` is the grouping key the cart renders — one row per component line, written when the combo is added, removed when the group is deleted. It is deliberately a gem table rather than a column on `spree_line_items`.
- The pieces are ordinary `Spree::Variant`s, so a bundle's save-time validation must also refuse a component that is itself a bundle's part twice over and a variant that is not the bundle's seller's offer.

### Price

The client is handed three numbers and derives none: `comboPrice`, `goodsPrice` (the components' sum) and `minus` (`miniprogram/pagesA/set-meal/list/list.js:58-61`). The gem computes all three from the components' current prices and the bundle's own discount rule, and stores the rule rather than the numbers:

- `goodsPrice` is the sum of component price times quantity, at the resolved seller's prices.
- `minus` is the saving. It is either a fixed amount or a percentage of `goodsPrice`, as two preferences on the bundle, so a price change upstream does not leave a stale figure behind.
- The **order-level discount row** carries `minus × bundle quantity` and is recomputed by the gem whenever the cart's bundle lines change — added, quantity changed, group deleted, or a component line removed on its own. The recomputation is idempotent and keyed on the bundle and the order, so a recalculation cannot stack two rows.

The discount is allocated to the component lines **proportionally to their goods amount**, with the rounding remainder on the last line by position, so the allocation is deterministic and reproducible. The client never shows a per-line bundle price, so the allocation is not a display concern — it exists so that returning one component reverses its share rather than unwinding the whole saving, and it is computed in one place rather than re-derived wherever a line is read.

### Availability

```ruby
# availability is the scarcest component, expressed in bundles
components.map { |c| available_for(c.variant) / c.quantity }.min || 0
```

- `available_for` is core's `Spree::Stock::Quantifier` for the seller's stock location (`spree/core/app/models/spree/stock/quantifier.rb:29-38`), so the bundle inherits backorderable handling, reservation subtraction and the external-provider seam with no new arithmetic.
- The client's threshold (`goodsMinComboQuantity`) is a quantity, not a boolean, so the verdict is a number and the *action* is derived: below one bundle it is unbuyable, below the threshold it is 套餐商品不足, above it the activity counters take over — and those are not this plan's.
- **Reservation needs nothing from this plan.** A combo in the cart is N component line items, and core's reservation primitive is line-item-scoped and exactly-one-owner (`spree/core/app/models/spree/stock_reservation.rb:19,22,129-131`), so it holds each component exactly as it would if the customer had added them one at a time. This is the concrete payoff of not making the bundle a variant: there is no bundle-level stock to reserve against, because there is no bundle-level stock.
- A bundle is never partially sold. The group's atomicity is the gem's to enforce on write — a cart holding some of a bundle's components and not others is a state the gem resolves rather than a state it allows.

### Cart and order

- **Cart**: the lines are the components, each tagged by the join row; the grouped read the client renders is the cart API's, owned by `6.1-store-api-miniprogram-gaps.md`, and this gem supplies the association it groups by. The selection semantics are already ruled — one bulk tick per group, sending every line (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “A cart line carries the shopper's tick”)) — so the cart's tick sends all of a bundle's lines or none.
- **Order**: nothing changes. The components are the order's lines, the discount is an adjustment on the order, and the order surfaces need no bundle vocabulary at all, which is exactly what the client renders.
- **Fulfilment**: per component, which is what the exploded lines already give. A bundle may therefore split across stock locations, and the plan says so rather than promising one shipment: `Stock::Package` and order routing decide, and the customer sees the components they bought.
- **Weight**: the sum of the components plus tare, which is core's own computation (`6.1-weight-bracket-shipping-calculator.md:37`). A bundle has no weight of its own because it has no package of its own.

### Reviews

A bundle gets **no review surface**, and the plan states it rather than leaving a gap: `6.1-product-reviews.md` reviews the offer — the variant — and the client's review calls are variant-keyed throughout, so the product page a customer reaches from a combo shows the components' reviews. The alternative, reviewing the bundle, would need a review subject that is not a variant, which that plan's first Key Decision forbids.

### Admin and lifecycle

The operator creates a bundle from components in the dashboard (a plugin-registered resource, per the gem anatomy), sees the computed `goodsPrice` and `minus` before saving, and can archive one without touching the components. Activation publishes a lifecycle event; the archive path leaves existing cart groups and orders alone, because the components are ordinary lines that outlive the bundle's status.

## Migration Path

1. The gem, its three tables and the three-place wiring — behind the CI fix the architecture plan already requires (`6.1-miniprogram-extension-architecture.md:170,174`).
2. The model: bundles, components, the seller validation, the statuses and the events.

4. The two reads, with the availability number and the single-object featured form.
5. The cart integration: writing and removing the join rows on add, and resolving a partially-held group.
6. The admin resource and the dashboard plugin.
7. Regenerate types, Zod, OpenAPI and SDK tests.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not make a bundle sellable.** No bundle variant, no bundle price column that a line item can be created against, no bundle stock item.
- **Do not add a column to `spree_line_items`** for the grouping; the join table is the seam.
- **Do not let a bundle cross sellers**, and do not split one to make a cross-seller bundle work; refuse at save time.
- **Do not submit the bundle's saving to the promotion engine.** A promotion row is cleared by a recalculation that does not choose it.
- **Do not read `frozen` as a hold.** It is 冰冻.
- **Do not invent an activity stock pool here.** The counters belong to `6.1-flash-sales.md`.
- **Do not give a bundle a review surface**, and do not leave the omission unstated.
- **Do not promise one shipment.** Components ship from wherever core's routing puts them.
- **Do not compute a price the server does not own.** `comboPrice`, `goodsPrice` and `minus` are all the server's, and the client derives none of them.

## Open Questions

The four questions this plan opened — the modelling, the discount's carrier, cross-seller compositions, and the allocation that makes a return unwindable — were settled on 2026-09-18 and now sit in **Key Decisions** with their trade-offs.

Nothing is deferred. The two adjacent questions this plan deliberately does *not* own — the activity stock pool and the per-customer purchase caps — are `6.1-flash-sales.md`'s, per the hand-off above, and `6.1-seller-scoped-pricing.md` has answered how a bundle's component prices are scoped to the resolved seller: they are that seller's own offer variants, each carrying its own base price. All three are named here so a reader does not mistake the silence for an oversight.
