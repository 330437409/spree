## Summary

The feature is a **brand-authenticity knowledge base**, and the client's own words say whose: the hub is 酒水鉴别学院 with the subtitle 鉴别科普·小二为您保真护航, the product-page entry is 鉴别助手, and the detail page's heading is 官方鉴别方式 with a disclaimer that the steps are **the brand's officially recommended method** and the customer should consult the brand's official website if in doubt (`college.wxml:4-5`, `details.wxml:13-14`). Nothing is user-generated: there is no write endpoint other than two votes, no author field, no reply, no report. A 问题 is an **operator-authored FAQ entry** — a `title` and a `discription` (the source misspells it) grouped under a brand category — not a customer question.

So the content is CMS-shaped, and the client is also clear about what it hangs on: **a brand**. `getDiscernBrand` answers two levels — a `brandCategory` rail and the brands under it, each with `brandName` and `logUrl` — and tapping one opens a guide. Which raises the question this plan's whole shape turns on, and Spree 6.0 has already answered it:

> **Spree 6.0 ships no brand-specific construct** — no `Spree::Brand` model, no brand attribute/column, no `:brands` tag context, no category `kind` column … a store that wants to group products by brand models it as a regular Category or Collection like any other grouping (`6.0-replace-taxons-with-categories.md:106-112`), with the constraint stated flatly at `:728`: "**Don't add a `Spree::Brand`, a brand column, a `:brands` tag context, or a category `kind` column**".

That is not an obstacle here, it is the answer: **the brand the content hangs on is a `Spree::Category`**, and `getBrandGoods` — which takes the *content's* id and answers goods scoped to the seller — becomes the category's products. It also settles a loose end in the client: it carries **two brand notions that are never joined** (the filter facet's `brandIdSet` and the college's `brandCategory`), and serving both from one Category is what joins them on our side rather than pretending the client already did.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **A brand is a Category, and this gem adds no brand model** (ruled 2026-09-18). Spree 6.0's ruling is explicit and its reasoning — a brand is a grouping like any other — is exactly this case. The gem owns the **content**, not the brand.
- **The gem is `spree_discern`** (ruled 2026-09-18), at `spree/extensions/spree_discern/`. It takes the client's own word (鉴别): clearer beside this plan's `discern_*` tables than `spree_product_authentication`, and promising less than `spree_knowledge_base` would.
- **The content model is two tables, not one with a kind.** A guide (an ordered set of steps, brand-attributed, with its share copy) and an FAQ entry (a title and an answer) have different fields, different vote targets and different pages; collapsing them would make every field nullable for one of the two.
- **The name avoids `Question`.** `6.1-product-reviews.md` already creates `spree_review_questions` for the order-level service question on the review form, and a second model called "question" would be a trap for every later reader. This gem's rows are **guides** and **FAQs**.
- **The vote is a row per customer per target, with a boolean.** The client hides the control after a vote and never shows a tally (`details.js:73-77`, `question.js:68-71`), but it keeps **no record** — the button returns on the next load — so write-once is this plan's server-side rule rather than a behaviour the client enforces. The payload is two-valued (有帮助 / 没帮助), not an upvote. A counter alone cannot answer "did this customer already vote", and the reviews plan has already declined helpful votes for reviews in writing (`6.1-product-reviews.md:369-371`), so **the vote on content is this gem's and nothing else's**.
- **Helpfulness grants nothing, and is not gamification.** The exclusion names its domains by example — dice, prize draws, rankings, scan-cashback, the home-page CMS (`6.1-miniprogram-extension-architecture.md:24`) — and a feedback counter is not among them: it grants nothing, and the client only toasts 感谢您的反馈. It is a feedback counter over content, and the tally — if one is ever shown — is this gem's own number rather than another gem's total, so the event-hook rule (`:26`) does not apply.
- **The content is store-wide, the goods are seller-scoped.** Two of the eight calls send `siteId` (`getBrandGoods`, `getDiscernInfoByKeyword`), three send nothing at all and three send only an id; `getBrandGoods`'s `siteId` scopes **whose offers are sellable** — the catalogue is the operator's and store-wide (`:164`) — while the content itself carries no seller.
- **The disclaimer is operator copy, not a client string.** "鉴别步骤为品牌官方推荐的方式 … 若购买对应的商品, 具体以下单为准" is legal-adjacent text that a merchant will want to edit, and the client renders it statically today. It becomes a field.
- **Search is over the content's own name**, which is all `getDiscernInfoByKeyword` does: a type-ahead answering `{id, name}` that the client highlights and routes to a detail page. It is not a search over questions or brands, and the plan does not widen it.

The mapping grades all eight **C** and recommends the CMS treatment — "新域：Page/Section/Article/Banner，或外接 CMS" (`:78-85`) — while filing them in the **catalogue** domain rather than the excluded CMS one. The recommendation is about implementation shape and this plan takes it: the content is its own tables with an operator surface, not a page builder, and **the home-page CMS exclusion (`:24`) does not cover them**.

## Design Details

### Models

```ruby
create_table :spree_discern_guides do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :category, index: true          # the brand, per 6.0's ruling
  t.string   :name, null: false
  t.string   :slug, null: false
  t.string   :share_title                       # the share image is an attachment, not a URL column
  t.text     :disclaimer                        # the operator's own wording
  t.string   :status, null: false               # draft / published
  t.integer  :position, null: false, default: 0
  if t.respond_to?(:jsonb)                      # PostgreSQL jsonb, plain json elsewhere
    t.jsonb :metadata
  else
    t.json :metadata
  end
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end

create_table :spree_discern_steps do |t|         # the ordered 鉴别方式
  t.references :guide, index: true
  t.string   :title, null: false
  t.text     :content, null: false              # rich text, sanitized on read
  t.integer  :position, null: false, default: 0
end

create_table :spree_discern_faqs do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :category, index: true          # grouped by brand category
  t.string   :title, null: false               # the question
  t.text     :answer, null: false              # the answer
  t.string   :status, null: false              # draft / published — the FAQ is authored too
  t.integer  :position, null: false, default: 0
  if t.respond_to?(:jsonb)
    t.jsonb :metadata
  else
    t.json :metadata
  end
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end

create_table :spree_discern_hubs do |t|          # one per store: the academy's own copy
  t.references :store, index: { unique: true }
  t.string   :share_title                       # getShareInfo's shareTitle / sharePic
  if t.respond_to?(:jsonb)
    t.jsonb :metadata
  else
    t.json :metadata
  end
  t.timestamps
end

create_table :spree_discern_votes do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :customer, index: true
  t.string   :votable_type, null: false
  t.bigint   :votable_id, null: false
  t.boolean  :helpful, null: false
  t.timestamps
end

add_index :spree_discern_votes, [:store_id, :customer_id, :votable_type, :votable_id],
          unique: true, name: 'index_spree_discern_votes_on_voter_and_target'
```

- `Spree::DiscernGuide`, `Spree::DiscernFaq` and `Spree::DiscernHub` inherit `Spree.base_class`, include `Spree::SingleStoreResource` and `Spree::Metadata`, use `has_status` and `acts_as_paranoid`, and publish lifecycle events; the guide carries the share image as `has_one_attached`, the way a category carries its own. Steps are position-ordered children; the guide's rich text goes through the shipped sanitizer rather than storing HTML it wrote itself.
- **The hub is a per-store singleton** — the academy's share copy is the one thing in the read that belongs to no brand, and giving it its own row is cheaper than an unowned field. It is what makes `GET /api/v3/store/discern_hub` a resource rather than a constant.
- **The vote is unique on `(store_id, customer_id, votable_type, votable_id)`** with the matching index above, because the second vote the server must refuse is the one the client cannot remember — it hides the control after a vote but resets it on reload (below).
- **Brands come from the category tree, not from a column**: `brandCategory` is a parent category and a brand is its child, with the logo the category's image — the same rows the product filter already reads, which is how the facet and the college come to share an identity.

### Reads and writes

- The hub read answers the rail (categories with their brands), the FAQ groups and the share copy — three small reads or one composed payload, and the plan takes **three**, because they are three questions the client asks at three moments.
- The guide read answers the steps in order, brand-attributed, with the disclaimer.
- `getBrandGoods` is the category's products through the ordinary catalogue read, with the request's seller scoping which offers are sellable — **not a new endpoint**: the response is the product collection the storefront already has.
- The two votes are one resource with two paths — `POST` and `DELETE` on the customer's own vote — because the client's two buttons are one judgement, and a second vote is impossible.

### Admin

The operator authors guides and FAQs, and orders the steps. The gem ships a dashboard plugin for both, with the rich-text step editor, because "operator-authored content nobody can edit" is the failure mode of a CMS-shaped feature.

| Method and path | Replaces |

| `GET /api/v3/store/discern_guides` | `getDiscernBrand` (the rail), `getDiscernInfoByKeyword` (a name filter) |
| `GET /api/v3/store/discern_guides/:id` | `getDiscernDetail` |

Four resources for eight endpoints, and `getBrandGoods` is not among them: it is the products of a category, which the storefront already reads.

## Migration Path

1. The gem, its five tables and the three-place wiring — behind the CI fix the architecture plan requires.
2. Guides and steps, with the admin plugin, because the content has to exist before the reads matter.
3. FAQs — with the FAQ surface beside the guides' — and the vote resource.
4. The reads: the rail, the guide, the FAQ groups, the hub.
5. **The pointer**: the product serializer's `discernId` is `6.1-store-api-miniprogram-gaps.md`'s field, and this step is the conversation with it — a product links to a guide either through its brand category or through an explicit pointer, and the plan's answer is `Category` first with the pointer as the escape hatch.
6. Regenerate types, Zod, OpenAPI and SDK tests.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not add a brand model, a brand column or a category `kind`.** Spree 6.0's ruling is explicit, and a brand here is a Category.
- **Do not call anything `Question`.** `spree_review_questions` exists and means something else.
- **Do not build votes as a counter alone.** The client behaves as if a customer votes once; the record is what makes that true.
- **Do not treat the vote as gamification**, and do not grant anything for it.
- **Do not widen the search** beyond the content's name: the client's type-ahead answers `{id, name}` and nothing else.
- **Do not put a seller on the content.** The catalogue is store-wide; `siteId` scopes which offers are sellable.
- **Do not let the disclaimer or the "brand official website" hint stay a client string** — they are operator copy.
- **Do not claim the product-pointer field or the filter facet.** They are the gaps plan's, and this plan states what they should point at rather than serving them.

## Open Questions

Two of this plan's four questions were settled on 2026-09-18 and now sit in **Key Decisions**: the content hangs on a `Spree::Category` — Spree 6.0's own answer for a brand, and the only thing that joins the client's two brand ids — and **the gem is `spree_discern`**.

Two remain. **One vote resource over two targets, or two** — this draft uses one polymorphic table and one route, and two tables would let each carry its own semantics later at the cost of two of everything today. And **whether the FAQ groups by category or by brand** — the client's navigator sends `brandCategoryId` while the page property is `categoryId`, which this draft reads as the rail's **parent** category; it is confirmed against the first real dataset rather than at implementation time.
