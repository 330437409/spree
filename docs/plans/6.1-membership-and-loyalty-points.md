## Summary

The mini program sells a membership: 40 endpoints for VIP and SVIP plans, levels, rights, growth value ("酒神值"), birthdays, annual gifts and member days, and 12 more for a points balance, a points ledger and a points mall. Spree's nearest concept is `Spree::CustomerGroup` — a flat, store-scoped label with admin CRUD, a Store-side read on the customer serializer and a promotion rule that can target it — which is the tier itself — `decisions.md` (upstream, 2026-09-04: Catalog audiences are alternatives, not layers, “a company buyer is never also priced by their customer gro”) names it for "a retail loyalty tier" — and is missing only the rank, the threshold, the term and the rights. Nothing in the repository models points, a points balance or a points ledger; the only "loyalty" vocabulary in core is the navigation group and permission group that `6.0-6.1-loyalty-gift-cards-store-credits.md` shipped, and that plan explicitly hands the rewards programme to a plan of its own (`:140`). This is that plan, and it covers tiers and points together because they share one balance row, one ledger and one locking discipline.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **The tier is a `Spree::CustomerGroup`.** `decisions.md` (upstream, 2026-09-04: Catalog audiences are alternatives, not layers, “a company buyer is never also priced by their customer gro”) names the group for exactly this — "Customer group assignments remain for buyers not purchasing for a company at all — **a retail loyalty tier**, a staff discount" — and the group already owns the member price through a `CatalogAssignment`. What a group does not carry is a rank, a qualification threshold, a term with an expiry, and the rights attached to it; those are additions on top of the group, never a second audience model beside it. An earlier revision of this plan forbade the reuse and built parallel tier tables, which contradicted both that ruling and this plan's own constraint that a customer must not sit in two tier groups at once.
- **One account row, two units, one ledger.** Points and growth value are the same shape: an integer balance, an append-only history, an expiry and a reason. They live in one account row per customer per store with two balances, and one ledger whose entries carry a `unit`. Two ledgers would be two locking disciplines for the same customer, which is how a customer ends up with points they cannot spend after a failed write to the other table.
- **The ledger snapshots the balance after each entry**, exactly as `Spree::StoreCreditEvent` does. A balance is then never recomputed from history in a request, and a discrepancy is visible rather than silent.
- **Member pricing is a catalog-owned price list — both layers, because they are one mechanism.** The catalog answers *who* (a `CatalogAssignment` to a `CustomerGroup`) and its owned `Spree::PriceList` answers *what price*: `PriceList belongs_to :catalog` (`spree/core/app/models/spree/price_list.rb:19`) and `Catalog has_one :price_list` (`spree/core/app/models/spree/catalog.rb:44`). Three facts decide it. A percentage is refused on a standalone list — `percentage_requires_catalog` rejects `price_adjustment_percentage` and `price_adjustment_tiers` without a catalog (`price_list.rb:471-478`) — so "member price is 10% off" is only expressible on an owned list. The walk consults catalog lists before rule-matched ones (`spree/core/app/models/spree/pricing_provider/internal.rb:48`), so a member price beats a store-wide list instead of losing to it. And the standalone alternative is the audience-rule route core retired: `Spree::PriceRules::CustomerGroupRule#superseded?` is true and hidden from the picker (`price_rules/customer_group_rule.rb:3-7`). `6.0-volume-pricing.md:34`'s "price lists = B2B" is about *rule-matched* lists; a catalog assignment is the door that lets a consumer-facing audience use the same pricing engine, which is what `decisions.md` (upstream, 2026-09-04: Catalog audiences are alternatives, not layers, “a company buyer is never also priced by their customer gro”) sanctions for "a retail loyalty tier".
- **A tier's price is `automatic` or `fixed`, and the choice is about where the boundary lives.** `Catalog#pricing_strategy` already reads those two words off the list (`catalog.rb:216-220`). An `automatic` list carries a percentage and derives every price from base on read, so a variant with no row still gets the member price (`internal.rb:126-135`) — the catalog's **assortment** is what limits it ("Owned by a catalog, the assortment draws that line", `price_list.rb:466-470`). A `fixed` list prices only the variants it holds rows for, so the **rows** draw the line and everything else falls through to base. A uniform rate across the store is `automatic`; a rate that varies by seller is `fixed`.
- **The operator funds the member discount, and commission follows what the customer actually paid.** Both decided 2026-09-17 (Open Questions 7 and 8). The commission base is core's own position rather than a change to it: `Spree::Commissions::CalculateLine.base_for` reads `subject.taxable_basis`, which is `[amount + taxable_adjustment_total, 0].max` (`spree/core/app/models/spree/line_item.rb:246-270`) — the discounted, never-negative figure — so the fee follows the actual selling price for a member price and a promotional price alike, and a value band weighs the sale on that same number (`spree/core/app/models/spree/commissions/context.rb:54-68`). What core documents the opposite of is only *who bears* the reduction: `SellerTransfers::Create#earned_amount` is `order.total` less commission (`spree/core/app/workflows/spree/seller_transfers/create.rb:106-119`), so **any reduction the customer sees comes out of the seller automatically**. One party funds it and it is the operator, so the tier catalog is the operator's, store-wide and `automatic`, the seller is paid as though the discount had not happened, and funding it needs an explicit credit back to the seller rather than merely a member price. See the seller-ledger constraint.
- **The catalog fallback is exclusive, and the plan accepts it.** `Catalog.for_context` consults customer groups only when the company axis returns nothing (`spree/core/app/models/spree/catalog.rb:153-175`). A retail member who also buys for a company gets the company's catalog and no member price. That is a deliberate, logged decision and member pricing must not assume layering.
- **A membership is issued on settlement, once, by an idempotent workflow.** A pending offline payment has not paid for the term. The settlement is a scenario order's rather than an `Spree::Order`'s — see "The order integration" below and `6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md`.
- **A membership and a balance belong to the customer, and span every seller** (decided 2026-09-17, `docs/plans/decisions.md`). A mini-program site is a seller, so the member centre reads `siteId`, but the entitlement is not divided by seller: `spree_memberships` and `spree_point_accounts` key on `(store_id, customer_id)` with no seller column, because per-seller balances would fragment a customer's points into balances that cannot be spent together. What *is* seller-scoped is the purchase — the cart, order and payment that produced the entitlement belong to the seller that sold. Every ledger entry therefore records the seller and order that earned or spent it, so a redemption at one seller can be settled against points earned at another. The client's `isSvipSite` flag is a field of the site object it already holds rather than a question for this domain, and `getSvipSiteList`, the endpoint that would have read it, is dead in the client and excluded (2026-09-17).
- **Points are not money and are not store credit.** `6.0-6.1-loyalty-gift-cards-store-credits.md:12` draws the line between a liability the store owes and margin given up; points are neither, and store-credit types and categories are being removed in 6.1, so nothing here is modelled on them.
- **Rights are typed records, not a JSON blob.** A right that cannot be filtered, listed in the admin picker, or enforced in one place is not a right. Each kind is a subclass addressed by `api_type`. **The kinds themselves are not listed here** — they are in `6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md`, which owns the rights catalogue, and the five names an earlier draft of this plan carried were a guess at them rather than a reading of the client.
- **A purchasable membership is a gift-card-flagged product, and the shared flag is a predicate rather than a behaviour switch** (decided 2026-09-17, Open Question 3). The client makes the two purchases structurally identical — `vip/addOrder` then `vip/payByWxMp` sits beside `gift-card/addOrder` then `gift-card/payByWxMp`, and neither goes through the cart (`pagesB/components/member/pay-suspension/pay-suspension.js:165-175`, `pagesE/gift-card/details/details.js:370-395`) — and what is bought is literally a card: "购买后的会员卡礼品将暂存至【我的-常用功能-我的卡包】中，您可选择将会员卡赠给好友" (`pagesB/components/member/pay-suspension/pay-suspension.wxml:41`). So `spree_products.gift_card` marks the membership too, and no second non-physical flag is added. What must **not** travel with the flag is the prepaid-money behaviour: a membership has no face value to spend, so it is not a `Spree::GiftCard`, and three of the five switches that flag throws are wrong for it. See the constraint on the shared flag.
- **A membership is issued dormant, and the term begins at activation or at a claim.** The client's card wallet holds cards "待激活/赠送" (`pagesB/cards/mine/mine.wxml:20-21`); gifting opens a claim window after which "会员卡退回账户中" (`pagesB/cards/presentation/presentation.wxml:19`); and the record page maps `activation` plus donation `success` → 已赠送, `invalid`/`recycled` → 已失效, `activation` → 已激活, `expired` → 已过期 (`pagesB/cards/record/record.js:49`). Paying therefore issues a dormant entitlement. "A membership is issued on settlement" still holds, but issuance is not the start of the term — activation is.
- **The card is an instrument and the membership is an entitlement, so they are two tables, and the card's lifecycle belongs to this gem** (decided 2026-09-17). A card is bought, held, given away, claimed and voided before anyone is entitled to anything; a membership is a period a named customer holds. Nothing about a card is a membership and nothing about a membership is transferable, so one row cannot honestly be both. Three facts from the client force the split rather than merely permitting it. A customer holds **several** dormant cards at once — the wallet counts them ("您有{{cardCount}}张酒小二会员卡待激活/赠送", `pagesB/cards/mine/mine.wxml:20`) and the same level can be bought twice as two gifts — which "one membership per customer per level" forbids. A card carries facts a membership cannot: whether it may be given away at all (`item.giftTransfer`, `pagesB/cards/mine/mine.wxml:36`), and the deadline to activate or give it ("请于{{item._validUntil}}前赠送/激活", `:32`). And a card that was given away stays in the giver's record as 已赠送 (`pagesB/cards/record/record.js:49`), so the card keeps its buyer while the entitlement goes to the claimer. The four transitions — **activation, gifting, claiming and voiding** — are owned here, against `spree_membership_cards` and `spree_membership_card_transfers`, and not delegated to `6.1-gift-card-purchase-and-transfer.md`. What the two plans still share is one situation, not one table: both a membership card and a gift card are bought dormant and activated later.
- **A claim and an activation are the same transition entered from two doors.** The client sends one call for both and distinguishes them with `activateType` — `"my"` from the wallet, `"relay"` from the received gift (`pagesB/cards/mine/mine.js:115-120`, `pagesB/receive-gifts/receive-gifts.js:100-104`). The underlying work is identical: the card leaves `dormant` and a membership period starts for whoever activated it. What differs is only who that is and whether a transfer had to be closed first, which is why the two are separate resources rather than one verb with a flag. The client also overloads its `cardId` parameter across both — the wallet passes a card's id, the receive page passes the **donation's** id it was handed in the share link (`miniprogram/static/behaviors/locationLinkBehavior.js:200-202`, `pagesB/cards/presentation/presentation.js:60`) — and separating the resources removes that ambiguity instead of reproducing it.
- **A membership card is addressed by an opaque token, never by its id.** Gifting hands the recipient a link carrying the transfer's own id (`pagesB/cards/presentation/presentation.js:60`, `path: "/pages/index?type=receiveMemberCard&typeId=…"`), and the receive page reads that record before signing in, only sending an unauthenticated visitor to login when they **activate** (`pagesB/receive-gifts/receive-gifts.js:74,97-100`). A public read makes its address a bearer capability, so the token is opaque and never derived from the card, while the card's own id stays behind `customers/me`. The card's id is not a secret: the wallet, the activate call and the cancel call all pass it in clear (`pagesB/cards/mine/mine.js:95,118,160`). See `6.1-marketplace-message-relay.md` for the same rule.
- **A points redemption is a discount on the order, not a tender** (decided 2026-09-17, Open Question 5). The client sends a boolean `useIntegral` on `calc/buyCalc`, `calc/cartCalc`, `order/buySubmit` and `order/cartSubmit`, and the server answers with `integralTotal` (available), `integralNum` (points that would be spent) and `integralMinus` (the money off), plus an `integral` entry inside the order's ordinary `minusList` — the same array as `coupon` and `first_order` (`pagesF/static/behaviors/settlementBehavior.js:43`, `pagesF/static/template/order-discount.wxml:42-61`). It is opt-in, refused inside some activities through `seckillIntegralFlag`, and the client performs no arithmetic: the money off is the server's number. That is `Spree::Discount` with `kind: 'loyalty'`, the value `6.0-6.1-split-adjustments.md:194,307` lists but the shipped model does not accept. The points mall is a different mechanism and stays one — see the Key Decision's sibling below.
- **The points mall is a separate order paid in points, and must not be folded into the redemption.** `integral-mall/addIntegralMallOrder` creates a standalone order paid wholly in points (`convertType: 'integral'`, with no payment call at all) or in points plus cash (`point_amount`), and there is no address or shipping step anywhere under `pagesD/integral/`. So the redemption reduces an ordinary order and the mall replaces one; the mall is `spree_point_products` in `6.1-points-and-growth-value.md`, and what a redemption produces is a scenario order in `6.1-scenario-purchases.md`. Neither is a `Discount`.

## Design Details

### Core, and only core

One column, and one decision that is now taken:

- `spree_customers.birthday` (date, nullable, no default). It is profile data, the only field here that belongs on a core model, and a one-column migration is smaller and less surprising than a decorator. Everything else reads it.
- The points redemption is a `Spree::Discount` with `kind: 'loyalty'`, which is the value `6.0-6.1-split-adjustments.md:194,307` lists but the shipped model does not accept. The client settles it: a balance is a tender that needs a payment password ("支付密码将用于下单时礼品卡余额的抵扣", `pagesB/me/password/set-pay/set-pay.wxml:12`), while points are one more reduction row in the same list as a coupon. A credit-like payment would put points in the tender list beside that balance, which is not where the mini program keeps them. No new core model is required — the discount record is core's and the ledger entry points at it.

The client carries **two** status fields on a card, and both are needed to say what the wallet and the record page say. It reads `activationStatus` (`activation`/`invalid`/`recycled`/`expired`) and `donationStatus` (`not`/`ing`/`success`), and combines them: `activation` + `success` → 已赠送, `invalid`/`recycled` → 已失效, `activation` → 已激活, `expired` → 已过期 (`pagesB/cards/record/record.js:48-50`), while the wallet separately marks a card 赠送中 from `donationStatus === 'ing'` (`pagesB/cards/mine/mine.wxml:29`). Our card's single `status` plus the transfer's `status` carry exactly this, so the mapping is: card `dormant` with no transfer in flight → 待激活; card `dormant` with a `pending` transfer → 赠送中; transfer `canceled` or `expired` → the card is 待激活 again for its giver, which is the "会员卡退回账户中" the client promises; card `active` reached through a `claimed` transfer → 已赠送 to the giver and 已激活 to the claimer; card `recycled` or `expired` → 已失效. The client's `invalid` and `recycled` are indistinguishable from each other — both render 已失效 — so one value covers both. The serializer derives this from the card and its transfer rather than storing a third status.

Three things the client needs that the tables above did not carry, all added after reading it:

- **The growth-value accrual rate is per product.** 酒量值 accrues from a product's own coefficient — the client reads `goods.wineCoefficient` and can sort a category by it (`wine:desc`, `pagesB/god-user/goods/goods.js`) — so the catalogue has to expose a number per product. That is merchant-managed merchandising data, which is what Custom Fields are for, rather than a column on `spree_products`.
- ~~**A growth-value entry belongs to a period**, because the ranking is per period~~ — **superseded**: the ranking is excluded gamification, so no `period_key` survives into `6.1-points-and-growth-value.md`, whose ledger carries no period column. The original read was that the ledger screen groups by calendar month and the board frames a period with a start and an end (`periodType` 1 = current, 2 = previous, `pagesB/god-user/center/center.js:90-96`), hence the `period_key` on `spree_loyalty_entries` above, mirroring the grants table.
- **The seller axis is the programme's allowlist, not a column on the membership.** The client gates every SVIP price, purchase, card activation, code exchange and right on one server-supplied boolean per site (`globalData.site.siteSvip`, `static/state/siteState.js:55`), while the membership itself is a single customer-level flag with a single end date (`globalData.user.isSvip`). So the entitlement belongs to the customer and its **usable scope** is the seller — seller scope for the goods a right issues is a property of the individual grant (`6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:659-663`), and the participating-seller boolean is a **column on the seller**, authored by the operator and delivered on the seller's own public profile read. It is not a membership-side allowlist and there is no second table (ruled 2026-09-17; `docs/plans/decisions.md`). No seller column is added to `spree_memberships` or `spree_point_accounts`.

`period_key` is what makes the grants idempotent: a daily job that runs twice on the 18th cannot grant a member-day gift twice, and the same key makes the annual gift survive a retry across a year boundary.

The purchase is the `vip` kind of a scenario order, ruled by the operator 2026-09-17, and it is **not** an ordinary cart line. The client never builds a cart: `vip/addOrder` is one call taking `{svipId, siteId, longitude, latitude, distributionCodeId, svipPurpose}` that returns an `orderNum` for `vip/payByWxMp` to pay, exactly as `gift-card/addOrder` returns one for `gift-card/payByWxMp` (`pagesB/components/member/pay-suspension/pay-suspension.js:143-155`). So issuance happens on the scenario order's settlement transition, not on an `Spree::Order`'s `paid`. A pending offline payment still has not paid for the term; the difference is only what is being settled. Issuance creates a **dormant card** and no membership at all, because the entitlement only begins when that card is activated or claimed. See the Key Decision on the dormant card, and `6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md` → "The purchase is a scenario order, and it is one call".

## Migration Path

1. **The design rulings are all taken** — Open Questions 1–8 were settled on 2026-09-17 — so the tables can be written. One build gate remains, and it is not the card. The `subsidy` seller-ledger kind is **five coupled core edits, not one, and none of them need the distribution ledger's payee decision** (see Constraints). The card lifecycle has **no gate at all**: it is built here, against its own two tables, and waits on nothing — not on `6.1-gift-card-purchase-and-transfer.md`, whose phase 2 substrate does not exist yet, and not on the distribution ledger's payee decision, because a membership card's gift is a row in its own table rather than a second subject in somebody else's.
2. Core: the `birthday` column, and the points-redemption decision from Open Question 5.
3. The gem's frame: programme, levels, benefits, account, ledger. Ship the ledger with an admin adjustment and no earning rules, so the append-only discipline and the balance snapshot are proven before anything writes to them.
4. Earning: growth value from orders, points from orders, level promotion on threshold, expiries.
5. Membership: plans as products, issuance on payment, then the dormant card and its four transitions — activate, give, claim, void — with expiry, and per-seller availability. The card can be built in this step rather than after the gift-card plan, because it depends on nothing in it.
6. Grants: birthday, annual gift, monthly rights, member day — all through one table with `period_key` idempotency and a scheduled job.
7. The points mall: rewards catalogue, redemption, and whatever Open Question 5 chose.
8. Register the gem in the starter Gemfile, in `.github/workflows/tests.yml` and in `scripts/test/rspec` — see Constraints.
9. Regenerate types, Zod, OpenAPI and SDK tests as each layer lands.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not build a second audience model beside `Spree::CustomerGroup`.** The tier *is* a group; its rank, threshold, term and rights are added on top of the group and never as a rival table. Do not target the member price through `Spree::PriceRules::CustomerGroupRule`: it is grandfathered, existing rules keep matching, and new setups reach the audience through a catalog that owns the price list (`price_rules/customer_group_rule.rb:3-7`, `decisions.md`, 2026-08-30). The promotion rule of the same name is unaffected and remains a live way to target a group.
- **Do not assume a member price layers over a company catalog.** The fallback is exclusive.
- **Do not model points on store-credit types or categories.** They are being removed in 6.1.
- **Do not add a second non-physical product flag.** `spree_products.gift_card` is the prepaid-instrument predicate (loyalty plan `:131`), and a purchasable membership must coordinate with it rather than add a rival.
- **Do not store a balance without a ledger entry, and never rewrite an entry.** A correction is a new reversal row.
- **Do not recompute a balance from history in a request.** Read `balance_after` or the account row.
- **Do not leave a customer in two tier groups at once, and enforce it in the gem.** `Catalog.for_customer_groups` returns the catalogs of *any* group the customer is in, ordered by catalog position (`catalog.rb:126-135`), and the walk takes the first that yields a price (`internal.rb:48-51`). A customer in two tier groups is therefore priced by whichever tier catalog sits lower — silently, and not by their higher tier. That is the exact shape V-3570 shipped and had to be fixed for (`decisions.md`, 2026-09-04): the code was right, the setup was wrong, and nothing warned.
- **An operator-funded subsidy needs five coupled core edits, not one, and only half of what it needs already works.** `SellerTransfer::KINDS` is frozen at `%w[earning refund_reversal]` and `validates :kind, inclusion: { in: KINDS }` binds that array at class load (`seller_transfer.rb:31,53`), so an extension cannot add a third kind — that is the acknowledged edit. What the shipped machinery already does for a new positive kind: `SellerPayouts::Sweep` partitions unsettled rows by **sign** (`sweep.rb:93`), so a subsidy is swept exactly like an earning, and neither `SellerBalance.for` nor `Seller#balance` filters by kind (`seller_balance.rb:49-56`, `seller.rb:277-282`), so it reads correctly. What it does **not** do, each failing silently: (1) `awaiting_provider` is `earnings.retryable.where(payout_id: nil)` (`seller_transfer.rb:81,91`), so a subsidy left `pending` — a seller not yet payable, or a provider refusal — is invisible to `ExecutePendingJob` and `ExecutePendingDueJob` (`execute_pending_job.rb:35`, `execute_pending_due_job.rb:43`), hence never sent, never `completed`, and never swept: **the seller is never paid and nothing raises**. The predicate that job actually wants is "positive and awaiting the provider", not a kind — it excludes reversals because `transfer!` would send the absolute value of a negative row, which an amount test states directly. (2) `SellerTransfers::Reverse` finds its row through `earnings` and `reversible_amount` returns `0` unless `earning?` (`reverse.rb:42,162`, `seller_transfer.rb:128`), so a refund reverses the sale and **leaves the subsidy standing** — the operator keeps paying its share of returned goods. (3) The `refund_id` index is unique and **not partial** (`20260822000001_create_spree_seller_transfers.rb:46-47`), so a second clawback row for one refund cannot be inserted at all; it has to become `(refund_id, reversed_from_id)`. (4) `Reverse#replay_existing` and its `RecordNotUnique` recovery read a single row by `refund_id` (`reverse.rb:52,70`), which stops being sufficient once one refund writes two, and that method's comment — "only a refund-keyed reversal can collide" — becomes untrue. **The subsidy is a new kind, not a new payee**, so it does not need the distribution ledger's payee decision and must not wait on it; what it genuinely shares is the cross-seller points settlement, which is the same "the operator owes this seller money" row. A first-party order needs no row at all — `SellerTransfers::Create#ensure_creditable` halts when the order has no seller. The amount is the seller's shortfall against their base-price net, `discount × (1 − commission rate)`, written once when the transfer is created and never recomputed. A negative `CommissionLine` looks like a free shortcut — it would flow through `earned_amount` with no new kind — and is worse: `Reverse#commission_totals` subtracts that line from the refunded share (`reverse.rb:139,148-152`), so a negative commission **inflates every clawback** on a discounted line, and it would misstate commission revenue besides.
- **The shared `gift_card` flag must not carry its prepaid-money behaviour onto a membership.** `6.0-6.1-loyalty-gift-cards-store-credits.md:88-96` specifies five things the flag switches off, and three are wrong for a membership: it forces `promotionable` false and excludes the product from price lists (a membership sale should be discountable, and a member price on a membership is not absurd); it refuses payment with a gift card or store credit (buying a membership out of a credit balance is ordinary); and it makes the sale non-taxable on the grounds that "the tax is due on what the card later buys" (a membership is a service period, not stored value, and the sale is taxable in many jurisdictions). Two carry over unchanged — no inventory tracking, and a digital delivery profile so no shipment is created — and the second is already core's job through the delivery profile and `Product#digital?`, not the flag's. So the flag is re-specified as "prepaid and not a physical good", with the per-kind behaviour branching on the kind. **A membership is not a `Spree::GiftCard`** and is never stored as one: there is no face value to spend.
- **`Spree::GiftCards::IssueFromOrder` needs no change, and a membership product must not be sold through a cart.** An earlier draft of this constraint concluded the opposite, on the premise that "a membership is issued on `order.paid` too". That premise is false: a membership is issued on a **scenario order's settlement**, and a scenario purchase creates no `Spree::Order` (`6.1-scenario-purchases.md`), so `order.paid` never fires for one and the workflow cannot reach it. The gift card's own purchase is the same scenario shape, so it does not reach the workflow either — `gift-card/addOrder` carries no cart, no line items, no address and no recipient (`pagesE/gift-card/details/details.js:355-395`), exactly like `vip/addOrder`. On this client `IssueFromOrder` therefore runs only for the storefront cart channel that phase 2 designs it for, the two issuers are disjoint, and neither gem has to learn about the other. The one remaining exposure is a membership product put into a cart; that is this gem's requirement to prevent where a tier binds a product, and never a reason for the gift-card issuer to add a discriminator the phase 2 substrate does not have.
- **A gift is read before sign-in, and it is addressed by the transfer, never by the card.** The receive page calls `receiveRelayCard` when it attaches and only sends an unauthenticated visitor to login when they **activate** (`miniprogram/pagesB/receive-gifts/receive-gifts.js:74,97`), so the read is public and the write is not. A public read makes its address a bearer capability, so the transfer's `token` must be opaque and never a sequential or prefixed id, per the rule in `6.1-marketplace-message-relay.md`. Do not serve the read under `customers/me`, and do not address it by the card's id — the client does exactly that (`pagesB/cards/presentation/presentation.js:60` hands the recipient the giving's id, and `receive-gifts` then passes it back under the name `cardId`), which is the conflation the two resources exist to undo.
- **Do not put a membership card on the gift-card plan's tables, and do not model it as one.** `spree_gift_card_donations` cannot hold one — a membership is never a `Spree::GiftCard` — and neither can `spree_gift_card_recipients`, because a membership is not addressed to a recipient at checkout. The card's activation, gifting, claiming and voiding are this gem's, against `spree_membership_cards` and `spree_membership_card_transfers`. The two plans share the *situation* — bought dormant, activated later — and nothing else. The flag is still shared by both products, which is a statement about the catalogue and not about issuance: see the `IssueFromOrder` constraint above, which now needs no guard at all.
- **Do not expect a refund action in any client, and do not add one.** The mini program has no refund endpoint at all, its membership order list is read-only, and nothing revokes a right client-side. A refunded membership is handled entirely on the server: the term is an absolute end datetime the client renders as-is, so shortening or voiding it displays correctly with no client change.
- **Do not grant twice.** Every grant is keyed by `(store, customer, right, period_key)` with a unique index behind it — in `6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md`, which owns grants now, and where the period is the right kind's own computation.
- **Do not put anything under Promotions** in the dashboard nav or the docs tree.
- **A gem that ships tables is a new pattern for this repository.** No gem under `spree/providers/` has a real `db/migrate` directory, so the install path is unproven: the host app needs the migrations copied and run, and the worktree template rebuilt afterwards. This gem is likely to be the first, so it documents that path and proves it against a real app.
- **Wire the gem in three places or its specs never run.** The starter `server/Gemfile` path block, the CI project list in `.github/workflows/tests.yml`, and `scripts/test/rspec`. Two gems already in this repository are missing from the CI list, so their specs do not run at all.

## Open Questions

All eight are settled as of 2026-09-17. Open Questions 3, 4, 5 and 6 came from this round, and 2 through 6 were settled by reading the mini program at `miniprogram/` — every client path below is relative to it. Each settlement is recorded in full, with the original question kept where it named a real fork.

1. ~~**Does member pricing ride price lists or promotions?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-17: a catalog-owned price list — both layers, because the question assumed they were alternatives.** They are one mechanism: the catalog is the audience and its owned list is the price. A standalone list guarded by an audience rule is out on three counts — a percentage is refused without a catalog (`price_list.rb:471-478`), the walk consults catalog lists first (`internal.rb:48`), and the rule route is the retired `CustomerGroupRule`. A promotion is out because the member price has to be the price that is *displayed*, not a reduction discovered at checkout. See the Key Decisions.
2. ~~**Are points per store or per customer?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-17: the membership and the balance belong to the customer, and the balance is not divided by seller** (`docs/plans/decisions.md`). Store tenancy still applies — `Spree::SingleStoreResource` puts `store_id` on every table, so a customer shopping in two stores holds two balances — but there is one balance per customer per store and no seller axis. `spree_memberships` and `spree_point_accounts` carry no seller column, and the ledger records the seller and order behind each entry so the platform can settle between sellers. The client gates every SVIP price, purchase, card activation, code exchange and right on a single server-supplied boolean per site (`globalData.site.siteSvip`, `static/state/siteState.js:55`) while holding one customer-level `isSvip` flag and one end date, so the entitlement is the customer's and only its **usable scope** is the seller. **The programme-wide allowlist this used to name is superseded**: seller scope is per grant now (`6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:659-663`) and the participating-seller boolean is a column on the **seller** rather than a membership-side table (2026-09-17), while no seller column is added to either table. `getSvipSiteList` needed no reading to settle it: it is dead in this build, referenced nowhere outside its own definition, as are `vip/getSvipList` and `rights/getUserSvipRights`.
3. ~~**Is a purchasable membership a gift-card-flagged product, or does it need the flag relaxed?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-17: it is a gift-card-flagged product, and the flag therefore has to be re-specified.** The author's ruling, and the client agrees with it: the membership purchase is `vip/addOrder` → `vip/payByWxMp`, structurally identical to `gift-card/addOrder` → `gift-card/payByWxMp`, and neither goes through the cart; what is bought is a card that lands in the wallet "待激活/赠送" with a claim window and a return path (`pagesB/cards/mine/mine.wxml:20-21`, `pagesB/cards/presentation/presentation.wxml:19`). So `spree_products.gift_card` marks it and no second non-physical flag is added, which is what the loyalty plan's own constraint demands (`6.0-6.1-loyalty-gift-cards-store-credits.md:131`). The ruling costs one thing, and it is a constraint above: the flag's prepaid-money behaviour must not follow the membership, because three of the five switches are wrong for it. The second cost this answer originally claimed — that `Spree::GiftCards::IssueFromOrder` had to stop keying on the flag alone or it would issue a gift card for a membership purchase — is **withdrawn**: both purchases are scenario orders and neither reaches `Spree::Order#paid`, so that workflow never sees a membership at all. The membership owns that lifecycle itself, against its own two tables — the dormant state, the claim window, the return to the account, the `recycled` void — because a membership is given through the wallet rather than by naming an email address at checkout, and because a membership card is not a `Spree::GiftCard` and so cannot be a row in the gift card's tables at all.
4. ~~**Is the growth-value ranking a Spree concern at all?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-17: the ledger half is ours and the ranking half is a real subsystem that must be named, so the original recommendation cannot stand as written.** The client was read to check it. The board ranks users by 酒量值 accrued from purchases, per product and per period, and the client is strictly read-only: it never sorts, so ordering is server-authoritative (`pagesB/god-user/center/center.js:123-134`); it draws page one's first row at **rank 4**, so the server must supply a podium snapshot *plus* a globally ordered paginated tail (`:183-186`); and it renders per-user positions the server computes — `nationalRankingNum`, `siteRankingNum`, a capped `rankingNum` such as `"50+"`, `withinLine`, `onListCount`, the entry threshold `onListValue`, and the gap to the neighbour above on each board (`pagesB/god-user/center/center.wxml:75`). The recommendation fails on one finding: **those positions are embedded in unrelated endpoints.** The goods-category response feeds `nationalRankingNum`/`siteRankingNum`/`withinLine`/`beforeValue`/`siteBeforeValue` straight into a product listing (`pagesB/god-user/goods/goods.js:99-105`), so "the ranking belongs to whichever system already computes it" is only viable if that system is named and every listing is allowed to call it; otherwise this backend owns the ranking. What is certainly ours, and is now in the tables above: the per-product accrual coefficient, the `period_key` on a growth-value entry, the signed explained deltas and the lifetime total the detail screen renders (`pagesB/god-user/detail/detail.js:42-44,58-59`, `wineValue` for the period and `wineValueTotal` overall), and the period lifecycle metadata (`startDate`/`endDate`/`overDate`/`topN`). One correction to the premise worth recording: `pagesD/tavern/**` is **not** this feature — it is referral commission ("酒友"), and it references 酒量值 nowhere.
5. ~~**How is a points redemption applied to a cart — a discount or a credit?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-17: a discount, `Discount#kind: 'loyalty'`.** The client decides it rather than a preference. `useIntegral` rides `calc/buyCalc`, `calc/cartCalc`, `order/buySubmit` and `order/cartSubmit`; the server answers with `integralTotal` (available), `integralNum` (points that would be spent) and `integralMinus` (the money off), plus an `integral` entry inside the order's ordinary `minusList`, beside `coupon` and `first_order` (`pagesF/static/behaviors/settlementBehavior.js:43`, `pagesF/static/template/order-discount.wxml:42-61`); the toggle is opt-in and re-runs the calculation; the client does no arithmetic; and the points earned afterwards are computed on the amount paid **after** the deduction ("商品积分值将按照优惠后（扣除积分抵扣、优惠券等活动）实付金额进行换算"). A credit-like application would sit beside the gift-card balance instead, which the client keeps apart and guards with a payment password. Two details the plan has to carry: the redemption ratio is **not** the same number as the earn ratio — `getIntegralConversionRatio` is the earn side ("每消费{{integralRatio}}元得1积分", display-only, with no multiplication anywhere in the client), while the redemption side is its own figure ("每{{integralMinus}}个可抵扣1元") — and expiry is a banner over the balance (`willExpireIntegral`/`willExpireDate`), not a row kind in the ledger, whose filters are only 全部/收入/支出 and whose label is a server-supplied `integralExplain`. So an `expire` entry may never need to be listed at all.
6. ~~**What is the interaction between a membership term and a refund?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-17: the term moves on the server, and the vocabulary for reversing what it granted already exists.** The client never offers a refund — there is no refund endpoint anywhere in it, the membership order list renders no buttons, and nothing revokes a right — so the whole interaction is a server concern, and it is expressible in fields the client already reads. The term is an absolute end datetime (`endDate`/`svipEnd`) the client renders verbatim and never computes, so shortening or voiding it needs no client change; the membership's `status` takes the `recycled` value the client already renders as 已失效 for a membership card (`pagesB/cards/record/record.js:49`). Three precedents say the reversal is already house style: `getUserLevelRecord` carries a `consume_return` ("消费退回") source with a **signed** change keyed to an `orderNum`, so loyalty value is already reversed per order; the platform already withdraws unused exchanged coupons when a membership period ends ("当期会员{{endTime}}结束后，所有未使用的券将撤回并失效"); and a refunded order is already excluded from membership statistics ("已退款订单不计入统计"). What has no precedent is an **already-shipped** annual gift — the client knows only 待发货/已发货/已领取, so recalling one needs a new status. Because `6.1-scenario-purchases.md` raises the same question for every scenario kind, this is the answer that serves both: the entitlement's own end datetime is the single source of truth and is moved, and anything the order granted is reversed by the same order-linked negative-entry pattern. A refund **action** in a client is out of scope and should not be added.
7. ~~**Who funds the member discount — the operator or each seller?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-17: the operator funds it, and that settles the shape too.** Because one party funds it and that party is not the seller, the tier catalog is the operator's, store-wide and `automatic`; the assortment is not narrowed to participating sellers for funding reasons, and the seller axis governs who awards and accepts points rather than whose prices are discounted — though that axis is a property of the individual grant now, not the programme-wide allowlist this sentence used to name (`6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:659-663`). The price is an explicit credit back to the seller, which core does not assume and the seller ledger cannot yet carry — see the Key Decision and the constraint on the seller ledger. The original question is kept below for the record. The membership spans every seller (settled 2026-09-17), but a price lands on a variant, and a variant is a seller's offer. One store-wide `automatic` tier catalog prices every offer the member can see, including a seller that does not participate; narrowing it to participating sellers means the tier catalog's assortment must be their products, curated against the seller's own participation boolean, which is a seller column rather than a membership-side table (2026-09-17; the two `vip/*` candidates were dead and are excluded). If instead each seller sets its own rate, one percentage cannot express it and the shape is one catalog per tier and seller, or `fixed` rows per seller. This is the same money question the ledger's seller and order columns answer for points, and the two should get one answer.
8. ~~**Is commission charged on the member price, or on the price the seller actually sold at?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-17: on the actual selling price — a member price and a promotional price alike — which is what core already does, so no core change.** `base_for` reads `subject.taxable_basis` = `[amount + taxable_adjustment_total, 0].max` (`line_item.rb:246-270`): a promotional discount arrives through `taxable_adjustment_total` and a member price arrives already reduced as the line's own price, so the fee follows what the customer paid either way. A price-list member price leaves no `Discount` row at all — the walk returns a `Spree::Price` (`pricing_provider/internal.rb:36-38`) and the line records its origin in `price_list_id` and `price_source` (`carts/price_items.rb:97`) — which is what makes the rule automatic here, and also why the operator-funded subsidy cannot be read off a discount row. The subsidy is the seller's shortfall against their base-price net, `discount × (1 − commission rate)`, so it must be calculated and persisted when the transfer is created and never recomputed later, because the base price it compares against can move after the sale; `SellerTransfers::Create` already runs once at completion, so the credit snapshots with it. The formula's dependence on the rate is arithmetic rather than a defect — the operator receives less commission precisely because the customer paid less, and its net is the same whichever base the fee is charged on. Because `Commissions::Context#commission_basis` weighs a value band on the same discounted figure (`commissions/context.rb:54-68`), a member discount can drop a sale into a lower commission band and lower the rate with it.
