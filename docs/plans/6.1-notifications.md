## Summary

Six plans in this set need to send something to somebody, and none of them owns the sending:

| Consumer | What it sends |
| --- | --- |
| `6.1-phone-verification-and-payment-pin.md` | a verification code and a PIN-change code, by SMS |
| `6.1-miniprogram-platform-services.md` | a WeChat subscribe message (the client holds the consent and reports it) |
| `6.1-referral-invitations.md` | an invitation reminder to the inviter and to the assisting friend |
| `6.1-china-invoicing.md` | an invoice re-send to an address the customer types |
| `6.1-local-delivery.md` | a failed dispatch, to the merchant |
| `6.1-flash-sales.md` | a 开售提醒 for a slot the customer subscribed to |

Until today those six said the sending was upstream's `6.1-marketplace-message-relay.md`. **It is not.** That plan — read in full on 2026-09-18 — relays messages **between a buyer and a seller** so neither learns the other's address: a `Spree::MessageThread` per (order, seller), an opaque alias per thread, an Action Mailbox ingress, and an operator's read-only view for disputes. It is a conversation feature, not a sender, and one of our plans had gone as far as planning to "amend" it into a channel registry. That was a misreading, and it is corrected here: **the relay keeps its own meaning, and our notifications are ours.**

The capability is small and mostly assembly. Spree already has the pieces of a send — `ActiveJob` with `Spree::BaseJob`'s retry and discard policy, `Spree::Integration` for credentials, the host app's Action Mailer, and events to trigger on. What does not exist is one door a consumer calls, and that is what this gem is.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **This is our capability, and the relay is not it.** `6.1-marketplace-message-relay.md` (upstream's) stays a buyer-seller conversation with masked addresses. Nothing here touches it, and no plan "amends" it. Our constraint — which the architecture plan previously wrote as "do not add a second outbound-notification mechanism" — becomes: **do not add a second *system-notification* sender; this gem is the one.**
- **One door, three channels.** `Spree::Notifications.deliver(to:, event:, payload:)` resolves the channel from the event and the recipient's own state, then hands off to a channel class. A consumer never picks a transport: the phone plan asks for a verification code, not for an SMS.
- **Channels are registered subclasses, built the way this repository builds them.** `Spree.notification_channels` + a `Spree::NotificationChannel::Base` with three members to start: `sms`, `wechat_subscribe`, `email`. The registry is the extension seam — a merchant's own SMS vendor, or a fourth channel, is a subclass plus a registration.
- **Consent belongs to the channel, and WeChat's is per event.** WeChat permits at most one message per granted template, and the grant is per event — the client already collects it (`pickType`, `templateIds[{openType, templateId}]`, `report.js:149-187`) and reports it, so the server stores the grant per customer and per event key. SMS and email need no consent, which is why consent is a channel contract method (`consent_for(customer, event)`) and not a column on the send.
- **Templates are per store, and the map lives where the platform-services plan already put it** — on a `Spree::Integration`, the one credential home, because the mini program's app id and secret are needed to send a subscribe message at all. An event key resolves to a template id through it, and a missing entry **fails loudly** rather than sending nothing: WeChat's failure for an ungranted template is silent.
- **No message table.** A notification is fire-and-forget with a retry policy, not a record: `Spree::BaseJob` gives backoff and `discard_on`, and a permanent failure reports through `Rails.error` rather than being stored. The events that triggered it are already durable, and a table of sent messages would grow without a reader. **Accepted trade-off:** "did this SMS actually go out?" is answered by the job log and the provider's own console, not by our database.
- **`email` is the host app's Action Mailer, not a second mail stack.** The channel renders a mailer the host already has (`Spree::Emails`' mailers where they exist) and adds nothing but the door.
- **Rate limiting stays with the consumer that needs it.** The phone plan already designs its own limits (per number, per hour, and a lockout after repeated failures). This gem does not impose a global rate limit that would be wrong for the invoice re-send and irrelevant for the dispatch failure.
- **Transactional only.** Every send is triggered by something that happened (a code was requested, an invoice was issued, a rider never came). There is no campaign, no audience, no scheduling — a marketing sender is a different plan and would need an opt-out this one does not model (Open Question 2).

## Design Details

### The gem

```text
spree/extensions/spree_notifications/     gem: spree_notifications
  app/models/spree/
    notification_channel/base.rb          the contract: deliver / consent_for / available_for?
    notification_channels/{sms,wechat_subscribe,email}.rb
  app/services/spree/notifications.rb     .deliver(to:, event:, payload:) — the one door
  app/jobs/spree/notifications/deliver_job.rb
  app/models/spree_notifications/integration.rb
  lib/spree_notifications/registry.rb
  config/locales/en.yml
```

- **The channel contract is three methods**, and it is deliberately small: `deliver(recipient:, event:, payload:, store:)` performs the send, `consent_for(recipient, event)` answers whether it may, and `available_for?(recipient)` says whether the recipient has the identity the channel needs at all (a phone number for SMS, an `openid` for the subscribe message, an email for email). **Two of those arguments changed as it was built** (2026-09-21): the door names the *event* rather than a template, because the template is the channel's to resolve — it is the one that knows whose account it is sending through — and it carries the *store*, because a send runs in a job and a job is not in a request.
- **A send is enqueued, never inline.** `DeliverJob` inherits `Spree::BaseJob` for the retry and discard policy, and the consumer's own flow never blocks on a provider. A verification code is the exception worth naming: it is still enqueued, and the endpoint answers "sent" only because the code row already exists — a provider failure surfaces as an undeliverable code, not as a failed request.
- **The event key is the vocabulary.** `verification_code`, `payment_pin_code`, `invoice_resent`, `invitation_reminder`, `dispatch_failed`, `seckill_reminder` — one key per consumer, resolved to a template per store, and the same key the WeChat consent is stored against.
- **Credentials live on `Spree::Integration`** (`spree/providers/` convention): one integration per channel per store, so a merchant's SMS account and the store's mini program are separate rows with separate secrets.

### The channels

- **`sms`** — the first member, because the phone plan's first step is a verification code. The transport is a Chinese SMS provider behind a small adapter (`Spree::NotificationChannels::Sms::Provider`), and **which vendor is an open question** (Open Question 1): the candidate set is 阿里云短信 / 腾讯云短信, both of which need a signed template and a per-store credential. The channel handles the vendor-neutral parts: the recipient's phone, the template parameters, the signature/署名, and the failure vocabulary.
- **`wechat_subscribe`** — a WeChat 订阅消息: the template id resolved through the per-store `Spree::Integration`, the `access_token` obtained from the **one token service** `spree/providers/social_auth` owns (ruled 2026-09-18 — the token is unique per appid, so a second cache would invalidate the first), and the consent checked before the call.
- **`email`** — the host app's own mailer, resolved by event key. The one channel that needs no credential of its own.

### What this plan does not build

No marketing sender, no audience or segment, no scheduling beyond "the consumer enqueues it", no message archive, no per-channel analytics, and no second email stack.

## Migration Path

1. **The gem and its three-place wiring** — `spree/extensions/spree_notifications/`, the starter `server/Gemfile`, `.github/workflows/tests.yml` and the gem's own `Gemfile`; the CI gap for the two provider gems closes first. Whichever foundation gem lands first (`spree_grants`, `spree_administrative_divisions`) performs the `spree/extensions/` scaffolding once.
2. **The registry, the base channel and the `email` channel** — the smallest end-to-end path, and the one that needs no vendor.
3. **`Spree::Notifications.deliver`** and `DeliverJob`, with the event-key vocabulary fixed for all six consumers at once, so nobody invents a second vocabulary.
4. **The `sms` channel**, once Open Question 1 is answered, with the phone plan's first consumer behind it.
5. **The `wechat_subscribe` channel** with the template map (platform-services) and the stored consent.
6. **Amend the six consumers' `Depends on:` headers** in the same change — each names this plan where it used to name the relay, and the architecture plan's capability row and constraint are rewritten.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not send from a consumer.** No gem enqueues its own mail but this one, and no gem holds a provider credential of its own.
- **Do not add a second system-notification sender**, and do not read the marketplace relay as one — it is a buyer-seller conversation and nothing here touches it.
- **Do not send a WeChat subscribe message without checking the stored consent**, and do not treat a missing template id as "nothing to send" — it fails loudly, because WeChat's own failure is silent.
- **Do not add a message table.** If a delivered-message record is ever needed, that is a decision, not a column.
- **Do not model an opt-out here** (Open Question 2) and do not let a consumer treat a send as guaranteed: a phone plan's code is delivered best-effort with a retry policy, and the endpoint's job is to have created the code, not to have delivered the SMS.

## Open Questions

1. **Which SMS transport? Settled 2026-09-21: 腾讯云短信.** The choice was the operator's to make — both candidates need a store-level account and template approval — and it is Tencent. The adapter sits behind `Spree::NotificationChannel::Sms` as `SpreeNotifications::Integration` (`SmsSdkAppId`, `SecretId`, `SecretKey`, the 短信签名 and one approved template id per event), signing with TC3-HMAC-SHA256. **The cost, stated rather than discovered:** no cassette can be recorded until the operator's account exists, so the signer is tested against Tencent's own worked example and the request shape against their documented API; and the fork's manual QA without an account runs through the phone plan's console task, which issues a code and prints it.
2. **Does a notification need an opt-out?** Transactional messages do not, and a marketing sender is out of scope — but SMS is a channel where customers expect to be able to stop *something*, and where Chinese regulation has its own rules on 商业性短信 versus 验证码. The plan takes "no opt-out, transactional only" and records it rather than leaving it implicit.
