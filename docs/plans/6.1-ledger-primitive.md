## Summary

Three plans in this set each designed the same machinery: **an append-only table that explains how a balance got where it is, and a reversal that is written rather than applied.** The points plan's `spree_point_transactions` snapshots `balance_after` and claws back a refund with a `consume_return` row; the distribution plan's `spree_referral_rewards` writes a `reversal`-kind negative row and takes its uniqueness from `Spree::SellerTransfer`'s `reversible_amount` and a partial index; the gift-card plan has not even named its table. The audit of 2026-09-18 found the three, and the ruling is the same shape as the other two primitives of this set: **one row shape, three accounts on top of it** (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-18 (later), “The four cross-plan rulings” — the second batch)).

This is the strongest of the three unifications, and the one with the sharpest edge, so both are stated plainly. **Strongest, because a reversal convention that differs per family is a money bug with a long fuse:** a refunded order touches all three — points clawed back, a distributor's reward reversed, a card refunded — and three conventions mean three different moments and three different signs, which is exactly how a ledger ends up disagreeing with a customer's own balance. **Sharpest, because the distribution ledger is not only a ledger:** it also carries the commission rate, the level and the payout state, and those belong to a payout process rather than to a balance movement. The ruling accepts that and splits the row: the *earning* is a ledger entry, the *payout* stays the distribution plan's own.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **One row shape, three accounts, no per-family ledgers (ruled 2026-09-18).** A points balance, a distributor's earnings and a card's history are three different subjects, and the row that records how each moved is the same row. **Accepted trade-off:** the account is polymorphic, so the row knows nothing about what its account means; the discipline is the contract below and the service, not the schema.
- **The row lands in core, and there is no gem (ruled 2026-09-18).** Same reasoning as the transfer primitive's: a new table, a new base model and a new service are new files, no file upstream owns is edited, and there is no kind registry to house in a gem.
- **A reversal is a row that points at what it reverses.** `reverses_entry_id` is a self-reference, and the reversing entry carries a **negative amount in the same unit**. This is the one convention the three families did not share — `consume_return` for points, a `reversal` kind for distribution, `refund` for a card — and it is also what makes a refunded order legible: the pair is visible rather than inferred from a kind name.
- **The amount is signed, and the unit is on the row.** Points are not currency; a card's value is; a distributor's earnings are. `unit` carries `points`, `growth_value` or a currency code, and every reader that sums a balance filters by unit rather than assuming one.
- **`balance_after` is a snapshot where the account has no authoritative balance, and null where the instrument is.** The points account has no balance column — its balance *is* its history — so the snapshot is what makes a balance readable without re-summing. A gift card's `amount_used` and `amount_authorized` stay authoritative and this row only explains them; writing a snapshot there would create the second source of truth the card plan already forbids.
- **Idempotency is an explicit key supplied by the producer**, exactly as the grant primitive's kinds supply theirs. That is what replaces the three different uniqueness rules — the points plan's per-source rule, distribution's `(order_id, distributor_id, kind)`, and the card's none — with one index per account, and it is what makes a retried webhook harmless.
- **The service records; the account performs.** `Spree::Ledger.record!` writes the row and nothing else. The points ledger's spend does its own locking, the card's redemption is `Spree::GiftCards::Redeem`'s, the distributor's earning is computed by the commission engine — this primitive never moves value, never holds a payee and never settles money. It is the same boundary the grant primitive draws for itself, and it is what keeps one shared row from becoming three businesses in one table.
- **No registry of entry kinds.** `kind` is a string column whose vocabulary belongs to the account — the points plan's reasons are operator data the client renders, not a closed set this primitive can validate. The audit already counted five new registered-subclass families in this set and this plan deliberately does not add a sixth.
- **The account keeps everything that is not a movement.** The points plan's account, grants and allocations stay its own; the distribution plan's rate, level and payout state stay its own; the card's holds and status stay the card's. This plan owns one table and the only door into it.

## Design Details

### The row

```ruby
create_table :spree_ledger_entries do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :account, polymorphic: true, null: false   # the point account, the distributor, the gift card
  t.string   :kind, null: false                           # earn / spend / expire / issue / redeem / refund / reversal / adjustment
  t.string   :unit, null: false                           # points / growth_value / <currency>
  t.decimal  :amount, null: false                         # signed: a spend or a reversal is negative
  t.decimal  :balance_after                               # the snapshot; null where the instrument is authoritative
  t.references :reverses_entry                            # the entry this one undoes
  t.string   :idempotency_key, null: false                # supplied by the producer
  t.references :source, polymorphic: true                 # the order, refund or campaign that caused it
  t.datetime :occurred_at, null: false
  if t.respond_to?(:jsonb)
    t.jsonb :metadata
  else
    t.json :metadata
  end
  t.timestamps
end

add_index :spree_ledger_entries, [:store_id, :account_type, :account_id, :occurred_at],
          name: 'index_spree_ledger_entries_on_account_and_time'
add_index :spree_ledger_entries, [:account_type, :account_id, :idempotency_key],
          unique: true, name: 'index_spree_ledger_entries_on_account_and_key'
```

- `Spree::LedgerEntry` inherits `Spree.base_class`, includes `Spree::SingleStoreResource` and `Spree::Metadata`, is **append-only** (no update path, no `status`, no soft delete — a wrong row is reversed, never edited), and publishes lifecycle events.
- The indexes are the two reads that matter: one account's history in time order, and one account's key uniqueness.
- A balance is a sum filtered by unit — `entries.where(unit: 'points').sum(:amount)` — and `balance_after` exists so the common read never has to.

### The account contract

```ruby
# On the account — the points account, the distributor, the gift card.
def ledger_unit       # the unit its entries are written in
def ledger_balance    # what the account says its balance is, for the snapshot
```

- A model that answers neither is not an account, and `Spree::Ledger.record!` says so by name rather than writing a row nobody can read.
- The contract is deliberately two methods. Everything richer — a lock, a redemption workflow, a payout — is the account's own service, because that is where the business rules already live.

### The service

```ruby
Spree::Ledger.record!(account:, kind:, amount:, source:, idempotency_key:, occurred_at: Time.current, reverses: nil)
Spree::Ledger.reverse!(entry, idempotency_key:)   # writes the negative counterpart, once
```

- `record!` is a no-op when the key is taken, which is what makes a job that runs twice safe.
- `reverse!` is the only place that writes a reversal, so the sign, the `reverses_entry_id` and the unit are decided once rather than by each caller.
- Neither method moves value. A caller that has already moved it — the redemption, the commission calculation, the points spend — passes what happened in.

### What this plan does not build

No endpoints, no serializers, no dashboard surface, no registry, no gem, and no payout state. It is a table, a base model, a module of two functions and a two-method contract.

## Migration Path

1. **The core addition** — `spree_ledger_entries`, `Spree::LedgerEntry` and `Spree::Ledger` in `spree/core` and nowhere else. New files only.
2. **The points plan converts first**, because it is the one whose ledger *is* its balance: `spree_point_transactions` becomes entries whose account is `Spree::PointAccount`, `consume_return` becomes a reversal pair, and the plan's own account, grants and allocations are untouched. Its client-facing reads do not change shape.
3. **The distribution plan converts second, and this is the step with the split.** Its earning rows become ledger entries on the distributor; `rate`, `level` and the payout state move to a payout-side row this plan does not define, and the plan must say where they land before this step starts. Its `(order_id, distributor_id, kind)` uniqueness becomes the entry's key, and its `reversal` kind becomes a reversal pair.
4. **The gift-card plan converts third**, and it is the one that gains the most: its ledger gets a name, a shape and a home without a new design, and its `issue`/`redeem`/`refund`/`transfer_in`/`transfer_out`/`expire` vocabulary becomes the entry kinds. The card stays authoritative for its balance, so `balance_after` is written null there.
5. **Each consumer's `Depends on` header names this plan in the same change**, and the three plans' own ledger sections are rewritten to point here rather than describing a shape again — the both-headers rule (`6.1-miniprogram-extension-architecture.md:168`).
6. **Record the ruling** in `fork-decisions.md`, including the split the distribution plan owes, and close the audit's D2 with a pointer.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not write a fourth ledger.** A new balance-bearing feature writes entries through this service; a table that is append-only and explains a balance is this table.
- **Do not edit an entry.** A wrong row is reversed by a pairing row, never updated and never deleted; the primitive has no update path at all.
- **Do not let a caller write its own reversal.** The sign, the pairing and the unit are `Spree::Ledger.reverse!`'s decisions, and three families inventing three conventions is the problem this plan exists to remove.
- **Do not move value here.** No settlement, no payout, no gateway call, no lock on another plan's row — the account performs, this primitive records.
- **Do not write `balance_after` where the instrument is authoritative.** A gift card's balance is the card's; a snapshot here would be the second source of truth its plan forbids.
- **Do not add a kind registry or an enum.** `kind` is the account's own vocabulary; the points plan's reasons are operator data the client renders.
- **Do not let the distribution plan's payout state leak onto the row.** Whether a reward has been paid is a payout fact, not a balance movement; the split is the price of this ruling and it is named in the migration path.

## Open Questions

1. **Where do the distribution plan's `rate`, `level` and payout state land when its earning rows become entries?** This plan does not define a payout table and should not: the distribution plan already has a payout path (`Spree::SellerPayout`) and owes an answer before its migration step starts. **What unblocks it: that plan's own decision entry.**
2. **Does the metadata column carry the commission's inputs, or does the payout row?** An entry can explain a rate without becoming the payout record, and a reader auditing a reward wants the rate beside the entry. Leaning metadata, because the payout row is per settlement and an entry is per movement, but it is deliberately left to the distribution plan to settle rather than assumed here.
