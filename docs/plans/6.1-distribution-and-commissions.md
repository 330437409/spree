## Summary

The mini program runs a distributor programme across 43 `retail/*` endpoints: a customer joins, binds "wine friends" to themselves, earns a rebate when those friends buy, sees a reward centre with levels and records, and withdraws the balance to a bank card. Spree has no customer-to-customer referral concept anywhere — a repository-wide search for referral, affiliate, upline, downline or distributor finds no model, column, endpoint or registry entry — so this is greenfield. It is not, however, greenfield on the money: Spree already ships a commission snapshot, a per-order earning ledger, a scheduled settlement, a balance reader and a payout provider seam, all built for sellers. The hard question in this plan is not what a distributor is; it is whether a distributor's balance reuses those rails or gets its own.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **A distributor is not a seller.** A seller is a supply-side party: it owns variants, stock locations and delivery methods, it is merchant of record, orders are split to it, and it signs in through its own `seller_api` audience (`6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md:184-200`). A distributor is an existing `Spree::Customer` who refers buyers and earns a rebate out of the platform's margin. Money flows platform to customer, the earner did not make the sale, and there is no catalogue and no fulfilment. Building on `Spree::Seller` would put every distributor into `Seller.sellable`, give them a public storefront profile (`spree/api/config/routes.rb:39-41`), enrol them in seller onboarding requirements, and tie their earning to the seller ledger's fulfillment trigger — a pile of guards that costs more than a model.
- **Attribution is bound to the cart, resolved to a binding when the order is placed.** The mini program must be able to attribute a purchase made by someone who had not registered yet when they clicked the share link. A code on the cart, resolved at `order.placed`, is the only shape that survives that. A binding is then unique per buyer: one referrer, enforced by a partial unique index, not by convention.
- **One binding per buyer, and it is revocable but not silently reassignable.** Direct sales and multi-level chains both need this. A partial unique index on `(store_id, customer_id)` where the binding is active is the enforcement; a reassignment is a new binding row with the old one revoked, so the history stays.
- **A reward is a snapshot row, never a recomputation.** `Spree::CommissionLine` sets this precedent (`spree/core/app/models/spree/commission_line.rb:8-9,20-40`: concrete foreign keys, no polymorphic source, written when the order is placed). A reward copies the rate and the amount at the moment it is earned, so a later change to the level table cannot rewrite history. Reversals are their own negative rows, exactly as `Spree::SellerTransfer` does (`spree/core/app/models/spree/seller_transfer.rb:81-98`, `reversible_amount`, a unique partial index on the earning row).
- **Real-name and bank-card verification are requirement submissions, not boolean columns.** `6.0-seller-onboarding-requirements.md:17-19` makes this binding: a verification step is a requirement kind plus a submission with a status, never a flag. `Spree::SellerRequirementSubmission` already carries a private attachment and a `pending`/`accepted`/`rejected`/`waived` status (`spree/core/app/models/spree/seller_requirement_submission.rb:12,23,39`), and `Spree::TaxIdentifier` already models validated identity document data with `validation_status` and `validation_evidence` (`spree/core/app/models/spree/tax_identifier.rb:35,37`).
- **Never store a raw identity document number or a full bank account number in a serializable column.** The account number is stored as ciphertext with a masked reader; the identity number likewise. The mini program's `rebindCashOutBankCard` rebinds rather than editing, which suits an immutable-per-version row.
- **Rewards and withdrawals surface under Loyalty.** `6.0-6.1-loyalty-gift-cards-store-credits.md:17` names Loyalty "the home for a rewards or points programme later" and `:140` leaves a points programme to its own plan. Distribution rewards are the same family and belong there. A reward is **not** a gift card and **not** a store credit: `:12` draws the line — stored value is a liability the store owes, a promotion is margin given up, and a referral rebate is the latter.
- **Withdrawal goes through the payout provider seam.** `Spree::PayoutProvider::Base` already defines `transfer!`, `pay!`, `reverse!`, `available_payout` and an idempotency key. A distributor withdrawal is a payout with a customer payee; the provider gem holds the rail, not the ledger.

## Design Details

### The models

**Core**

```ruby
create_table :spree_distributors do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :customer, index: true
  t.string   :code, null: false
  t.string   :status, null: false            # pending/active/suspended/rejected
  t.references :level, index: true
  t.references :parent, index: true          # upline; null at the root
  t.datetime :terms_accepted_at
  t.datetime :activated_at
  t.timestamps
  t.datetime :deleted_at
end
# unique (store_id, code); unique (store_id, customer_id); index (store_id, status)
```

`spree_distributor_levels` holds the tiers (`name`, `position` via `acts_as_list scope: :store`, `active`) with rate and threshold configuration in model preferences, mirroring how `Spree::CommissionRate` is configured rather than adding free-typed columns.

`spree_distributor_bindings` is the "wine friend" row: `store_id`, `distributor_id`, `customer_id`, `source` (`share_link`/`qr_code`/`scene`/`admin`), `bound_at`, `revoked_at`. Partial unique index on `(store_id, customer_id)` where `revoked_at IS NULL`.

`spree_referral_rewards` is the ledger: `store_id`, `order_id`, `distributor_id`, `binding_id`, `kind` (`referral`/`invite_task`/`level_bonus`/`red_packet`/`reversal`), `rate`, `amount`, `currency`, `status`, `level`, plus `metadata`. Its uniqueness is `(order_id, distributor_id, kind)`.

`spree_distributor_withdrawals` is `store_id`, `distributor_id`, `amount`, `currency`, `status` (`pending`/`processing`/`completed`/`failed`/`canceled`), `provider`, `reference`, `payout_account_id`, `requested_at`, `processed_at`, `reviewed_by_id`, `failure_reason`. Unique partial index on `(provider, reference)` where the reference is present — the same retry guard `spree_seller_payouts` uses.

**Extension gem**

`spree_wechat_distributor_identities` maps mini-program identity to a customer and distributor: `openid` and `unionid`, unique on `openid`.
`spree_distributor_referral_tokens` issues share codes and QR payloads: `has_secure_token`, a `kind` (`share`/`qr`/`scene`/`invite_task`), an optional polymorphic target, and an expiry. The token is opaque and never derived from anything guessable, following `6.1-marketplace-message-relay.md`'s rule.
`spree_distributor_payout_accounts` holds the destination: bank name, holder name, masked number, ciphertext, provider reference, one active row per distributor.
`spree_distributor_verifications` holds the real-name submission: `kind` (`real_name`/`bank_card`), `status`, the provider reference, `validated_at`, an evidence payload, and a private attachment for the document.

### The ledger decision

This is the part that cannot be settled inside an extension.

`Spree::SellerTransfer` and `Spree::SellerPayout` are seller-typed and their uniqueness is written for one earner per order: the migration behind `SellerTransfer` declares a partial unique index on `(order_id)` where `kind = 'earning'` (`spree/core/db/migrate/20260822000001_...`). A multi-level referral writes **several** rewards for one order, so that index has to become payee-scoped, and the same is true of the readers: `Seller#balance(currency)` (`spree/core/app/models/spree/seller.rb:277-282,304-309`), the `Spree::SellerBalance` value object (`spree/core/app/models/spree/seller_balance.rb:14,42-58`), the seller balances controller and the admin route all assume a seller.

Two ways forward, and the plan does not pretend one is free:

| Option | Shape | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Exactly-one-of | Add a nullable `distributor_id` to both tables with a validation that exactly one of `seller_id`/`distributor_id` is present, mirroring `Spree::CommissionLine#exactly_one_subject` (`spree/core/app/models/spree/commission_line.rb:79-83`), and make the partial indexes payee-scoped | Fits the shipped "concrete foreign keys, no polymorphic source" rule; two nullable columns and a broader class of bug |
| B. Payee polymorphism | Make the payee `belongs_to :payee, polymorphic` | This is what `6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md:182` names as the migration path when a concrete non-seller payee arrives — "the reusability that a dealership / B2B revenue-share scenario would want comes from an **abstract payee** … not from a generic model name" — but `commission_line.rb:8-9` records the opposite decision for the shipped ledger, so **the two documents contradict each other and a dated `fork-decisions.md` entry has to resolve them** |

Option C — a parallel distributor ledger in the extension — is listed only to be rejected: two ledgers that must agree about the same order will eventually disagree.

**What is not part of this decision.** An operator-funded member discount is a new `kind` on the seller ledger whose payee is already a seller, so it needs the *kind* widened and nothing of the *payee* widened below — it must not wait on this ruling (`6.1-membership-and-loyalty-points.md`, Constraints). A membership card's gift is not part of it either: the membership card keeps its own transfer table with a plain `membership_card_id`, so it never becomes a second subject here (`6.1-membership-and-loyalty-points.md`). **The distributor is the only instance of this fork**, which is what makes it a question about the seller ledger's payee rather than about transfer tables in general.

### Lifecycle

1. **Join.** `POST /api/v3/store/distributors` creates a `pending` distributor for the signed-in customer, with terms acceptance recorded. Approval is an operator action, and a `pending` distributor accrues rewards but cannot withdraw — mirroring `payouts_enabled?` gating the seller side (`spree/core/app/workflows/spree/seller_transfers/create.rb:78-80`).
2. **Share.** A referral token or QR is issued for a target, rate-limited on creation.
3. **Attribute.** The buyer arrives through the mini program's scene value; the code is attached to the cart (`POST /api/v3/store/carts/:cart_id/referral`), beside `discount_codes` in the existing carts block (`spree/api/config/routes.rb:56-64`). At `order.placed` the cart's code resolves to a binding, creating one if the buyer had none.
4. **Earn.** A subscriber on the order-placed event writes one `spree_referral_rewards` row per upline in the chain, priced from the level table, with the rate and amount copied. The refund seam (`spree/core/app/subscribers/spree/seller_transfer_reversal_subscriber.rb:14`) is the model for the reversal subscriber.
5. **Withdraw.** `POST .../distributor/withdrawals` requests; an operator or a job settles through the payout provider; the settled rewards are stamped with the withdrawal so a payout is itemized, as `Spree::SellerPayout#release_transfers` does.
6. **Verify.** A real-name submission and a payout account must both be accepted before the first withdrawal leaves `pending`.

### Store API surface

| Method and path | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /api/v3/store/distributors` | Join the programme |
| `GET /api/v3/store/customers/me/distributor` | Own profile: status, level, balances, binding count |
| `GET /api/v3/store/customers/me/distributor/earnings`, `/:id` | The reward ledger, filtered by kind, status, currency and date |
| `GET /api/v3/store/customers/me/distributor/bindings` | Attributed buyers, **masked, with aggregate counts only** |
| `GET|POST /api/v3/store/customers/me/distributor/referral_tokens` | Issue and list share codes and QR payloads |
| `GET|POST .../distributor/withdrawals`, `GET .../:id` | Request, list, detail |
| `POST .../distributor/verifications` | Submit real-name evidence |
| `PATCH .../distributor/payout_account` | Rebind the payout destination (singular nested resource, as `resource :tax_identifier` already is) |
| `POST|DELETE /api/v3/store/carts/:cart_id/referral` | Attribution at the cart |

Admin gets `distributors` (with `approve`, `reject`, `suspend`), `distributor_levels`, `referral_rewards` (read-only), `distributor_verifications` (with `accept`/`reject`) and `distributor_withdrawals` (with `complete`/`reject`). Permission scopes register as staff-only in the catalog; a customer-audience read-only key is added only if a distributor panel ever ships, mirroring `seller_earnings` and never reusing `payouts`.

Events published: `distributor.created/approved/rejected/suspended`, `distributor_binding.created/revoked`, `referral_reward.created/reversed`, `distributor_withdrawal.requested/completed/failed`.

`retail/supplementShenBianYunSign` reads like the signature step of a third-party flexible-employment payout platform, which would mean the operator already outsources distributor payouts and their tax withholding. **That is inferred from the endpoint name and is unverified.** If true, the rail is a `PayoutProvider` gem and the ledger still lives where this plan puts it.

## Migration Path

1. **Decide the ledger question** (Open Question 1) and record it in `docs/plans/decisions.md`, because it contradicts two live documents.
2. Core: distributors, levels, bindings, rewards, withdrawals, with the reward ledger reading through whichever payee shape was chosen.
3. Core: the attribution column on the cart and the resolution subscriber at `order.placed`, plus the reversal subscriber on refunds.
4. Extension gem: identity mapping, referral tokens and QR, real-name and payout-account verification against the provider.
5. Store API and Admin API, then the dashboard under Loyalty.
6. The payout rail as a `PayoutProvider` gem, if the operator's existing provider is to be used.
7. Regenerate types, Zod, OpenAPI and SDK tests as each layer lands.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not build a distributor on `Spree::Seller`**, and do not add a commission `Fee` kind or reuse `Spree::Supplier` (`6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md:776-777`).
- **Do not write a second money ledger.** Until Open Question 1 is settled, no new table that sums into a withdrawable balance.
- **Do not model a refund clawback by deleting a reward.** Write a reversal row.
- **Do not store an identity number or a full bank account number in plaintext**, and do not expose a referred buyer's identity to their distributor beyond a masked identifier and aggregate counts.
- **Do not add a boolean verification column.** Requirements and submissions are the shape.
- **Do not put the rewards surface under Promotions.** Loyalty owns it.
- **Do not launch a multi-level scheme before the legal question in Open Question 4 is answered.** Nothing in this repository addresses it.

## Open Questions

1. **Does the distributor balance reuse the seller ledger, and in which shape?** Option A (nullable `distributor_id`, exactly-one-of) or Option B (payee polymorphism), per the table above. This changes shipped tables either way and needs a dated `fork-decisions.md` entry, because `6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md:182` and `spree/core/app/models/spree/commission_line.rb:8-9` currently say opposite things. Unblocked by a ruling.
2. **How deep is the tree, and are rewards cash or in kind?** The endpoint list proves a parent exists (`getParentInfo`, `getParentQrcodeMp`, `levelExplain`, `retailRewardLevelDesc`) but not the depth. `getInviteCoupon`, `getInvitePrize` and `getRetailPrizePost` suggest in-kind rewards alongside cash, which means the reward table needs a kind that never enters the money ledger. Unblocked by a product decision; depth also decides whether the `(order_id)` uniqueness change is needed at all.
3. **When is a reward earned — `order.placed` or `order.fulfilled`?** `SellerTransfer` credits on fulfillment (`spree/core/app/subscribers/spree/seller_transfer_subscriber.rb:16`) and commission on placement (`order_commission_subscriber.rb:14`). Crediting a referral at placement is faster and refund-exposed; at fulfillment it is safer and slower. Unblocked by a product decision; the reversal seam covers the clawback either way.
4. **Is a multi-level commission structure lawful for this operator, and who withholds tax on a rebate paid to an individual?** This is a PRC legal question — the relevant regulation is not addressed anywhere in `docs/plans/`, and the repository's only adjacent statement is that KYC *operations* sit on the Enterprise side of `6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md:9-13,164`. Unblocked by legal advice, not by code.
5. **Who owns the Loyalty nav slot for rewards — this plan or the points programme?** `6.0-6.1-loyalty-gift-cards-store-credits.md:140` reserves it for a points programme that does not exist yet. Unblocked by choosing which ships first.
