# Local delivery: one plugin for the platform's riders, its fee rules and its waivers

> **Fork-only plan.** Ours, never submitted to `spree/spree`. The tracked
> plans beside it are upstream's and are read-only to us; our decisions are in
> `fork-decisions.md`, never in upstream's `decisions.md`.

**Status:** Draft — design settled, the 2026-09-18 rulings recorded, and all three of its original open questions answered: **supply** is a provider family with 达达 first (alcohol is a declared category there and on the WeChat channel, and 闪送 is the member that may refuse it); **price** is the delivery method's own rule price while the platform's quote is cost; **a waiver is a promotion**, funded by the seller at shop level and by the operator through `subsidy` when it is a membership benefit; **a callback is a hint**, with terminal states confirmed from the platform. **Target:** Spree 6.1 — the platform's local-delivery capability: a provider family for supply, delivery-method rules for the gate, and the promotion/membership links for the waiver. **No client endpoint.**
**Kind:** Provider family + platform configuration (one gem, one member per platform or channel)
**Scope:** **Nothing for the client, and that is the point.** The mini program never quotes, dispatches or cancels a delivery — it has 8 read-only endpoints, all of which `6.1-delivery-tracking.md` already owns — so this plan adds no route and the hardcoded 配送方式 row stays correct. What it owns is the seller- and operator-side capability the client cannot express: **how a delivery is priced** (the method's calculator, with promotions and membership waiving it), **how much order it needs** (the 起送 gate), **who supplies the rider** (the shop's own, or a local instant-delivery network), and **what happens when nobody takes it**.

## Summary

Every delivery in this client is made by the shop: the order row reads 酒小二 ({{siteName}}) 配送 — a **literal**, not a field — the map marker says 小二配送中, the rider has the shop's own 接单 APP, and the rider carries the shop's rating and tags. The client can only *watch* a delivery: eight read-only endpoints keyed by `orderId`, no quote, no dispatch, no delivery cancel. So this plan does not touch the client; it gives the **platform** the delivery capability behind it.

Four pieces, and only the second and third are new:

- **Supply** — the shop's own riders (nothing to build) or a local instant-delivery network, through a provider family on the seams core already ships: `FulfillmentProvider::Base#create_fulfillment` dispatches and answers a tracking number and URL, `#cancel_fulfillment` withdraws it.
- **Price** — the **delivery method's own price**, which is what the customer pays and what the promotions act on. The platform's live quote is a *cost*, never the customer's price (ruled 2026-09-18).
- **Waivers** — 满额免配送 and 会员免配送 are **promotions**, built from parts that already ship: `Spree::Promotion::Actions::FreeShipping` writes a `Discount` on the fulfillment for the whole cost and persists it even at zero, which is what `Order#has_free_shipping?` tests. No fee engine, no new calculator, no membership-side change — a tier *is* a `Spree::CustomerGroup`, so "member gets free delivery" is a promotion whose rule is the tier group.
- **The gate** — 起送价, the minimum order the client already renders with peak and off-peak variants, which is where the delivery-method rules live.

Two premises stated rather than assumed. **The supply model is deliberately different from the app being cloned**: 酒小二 runs 2,400+ 前置仓 and 13,000+ riders of its own, and its platform integrations are for *demand*, not last-mile supply — dispatching to a third-party rider network is a swap, and a plan that implied the client already does it would be wrong. And **two of the six platforms are out of reach for a single shop**: 美团配送 does not serve individual developers (chains: ≥30 stores, ≥1,000 orders/day, ¥50,000–100,000 deposit) and 蜂鸟即配 registers software providers or brand merchants, while 达达, 闪送 and 顺丰同城 are self-service. **No official Ruby SDK exists for any of them** — the clients are Faraday, the `spree_wechat_pay` precedent.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

### Supply

- **A provider family, not a platform.** One gem, `spree_instant_delivery`, with a `FulfillmentProvider` per platform — the shape `spree_social_auth` already uses for four identity providers. **The first member is 达达** (ruled 2026-09-18); 闪送, 顺丰同城 and WeChat's own 即时配送 channel follow. Adding a member adds a subclass and changes nothing else.
- **The client contract does not change**, and no route is added: the eight read endpoints stay `6.1-delivery-tracking.md`'s, and the payload is identical whether the rider is the shop's or 达达's.
- **WeChat's 即时配送 channel is a member, not a shortcut.** `api.weixin.qq.com/cgi-bin/express/local/business/*` covers 顺丰同城, 闪送, 美团配送 and 达达 in one integration with the cleanest status vocabulary of the six (`101` 等待分配骑手, `102` 分配骑手成功, `201` 到店, `202` 取货成功, `301` 配送中, `302` 配送成功, `401` 返还成功). It is bound to the mini program, so it cannot serve an H5 or an independent storefront, and it still requires signing with each carrier. **Its calls authenticate with the shared mini-program `access_token`** (ruled 2026-09-18) — the service `spree/providers/social_auth` owns, which this channel reads rather than fetching its own, because WeChat invalidates the previous token whenever a new one is obtained.
- **The dispatcher re-dispatches, because no platform does** (ruled 2026-09-18). Only 达达 publishes a timeout rule (30 minutes unaccepted → 已过期, with a re-dispatch endpoint). A `Dispatch` service walks the merchant's platform order — cheapest, fastest, or a named list — two minutes per attempt, escalating the tip where the platform supports it (达达, 闪送, 蜂鸟 do; 顺丰同城 does not), and cancelling every losing attempt once one rider accepts. A total failure leaves the order in 待配送 — the client's own state — and notifies the merchant through `spree_notifications`.
- **"Dispatched but not accepted" is a state the fulfillment contract cannot hold, and the plan says so.** `create_fulfillment` answers a tracking number or raises; every one of these platforms *begins* there (待接单, which can expire). The dispatcher therefore keeps its own attempt record rather than pretending the fulfillment's status covers it.

### Price

- **The customer-facing fee is the delivery method's own price, and the platform's quote is cost** (ruled 2026-09-18). `DeliveryMethod` carries **two independent columns**, and this ruling is what they are for: `rate_provider` stays `Internal` — the calculator prices, so a flat default fee today and a distance- or time-band calculator later — while **supply is the other column**, `fulfillment_provider`, naming the platform that dispatches. "同城配送 ¥8, dispatched to 达达" is therefore one method, and the platform's live quote never reaches the customer: it is recorded on the attempt as `charged_amount` beside `quoted_amount`, so the merchant (or the operator, for a benefit) carries the difference and a dispute has the numbers.
  - **The rejected shape, named so nobody re-derives it.** A provider-priced method (`uses_calculator?` false) *would* pass the live quote through, and the Estimator's `markup_percent`/`markup_flat` is the built-in margin seam for exactly that. It stays available for a merchant who wants the platform's price plus a margin; the ruling is that our customer pricing follows the platform only when we say so, and the default methods price by rule.
- **A waiver is a promotion, never a fee engine** (ruled 2026-09-18). 满额免配送 is `Promotion::Rules::ItemTotal` (`gte`) plus `Promotion::Actions::FreeShipping`; 会员免配送 is `Rules::CustomerGroup` plus the same action, and **the membership plugin needs no change at all** because a tier is a `CustomerGroup`. `FreeShipping` computes `-cost` against the fulfillment, and its `persist_at_zero?` is load-bearing: `Order#has_free_shipping?` is a row-existence test and `DeliveryRate#final_price` reads it, so a waived fee is a zero row, not an absent one. **Do not build a `free_delivery` right kind, a delivery-fee calculator, or a second discount mechanism.**
- **Who funds a waiver** (ruled 2026-09-18). A **shop-level** rule ("满 39 免配送") is the seller's own money: the customer pays zero, the seller earns less, and no transfer is needed. An **operator-promised** benefit (会员免配送, a platform campaign) is funded through the membership plan's `subsidy` seller-transfer kind — the operator owes the seller the waived amount, settled by the existing sweep and balance — which is **five coupled core edits the membership plan already carries**. This plan consumes that capability and does not re-open it.
- **The client shows a zero fee, not a deduction.** The mini program's `minusList` lines typed `order_service_fee`/`merchant_service_fee` are **added** (`+¥`) and 配送服务费总额 is `deliveryFee` + those lines; there is no negative service-fee rendering anywhere. A waiver therefore reaches the client as `deliveryFee = 0` plus a hint sentence (the client already renders a rich-text hint), and a "免配送费 −¥8" row would be a **client release**, not a server change. The plan states this so nobody builds a line we cannot render.
- **起送价 is an eligibility rule, and it is the one new rule this plan adds.** The client renders a minimum order with peak and off-peak variants (`peakPeriodMoney` / `unPeakPeriodMoney`, 还差 N 元起送, and the refusal 订单未达起送金额 at submit). Eligibility is exactly where delivery-method rules already live, and `DeliveryMethodRules::ItemTotalRule` is already that shape — but a **peak/off-peak** threshold reads the clock, and no shipped rule does. The plan takes the rule kind (the alternative is two methods), because the client shows one method with two thresholds, and it is the only new rule kind here.

### Delivery and its record

- **A callback is a hint, not a fact** (ruled 2026-09-18, after the ingress research). Every member verifies what it can — 达达 signs its callback, the WeChat channel signs twice, UU signs with the same SHA-1 rule as its requests — but **闪送's callback carries no signature at all** by design, and **顺丰同城's `sign` concatenation is genuinely not public**. The one control all four support is to re-query the order with our own signed call before writing a terminal state (`delivered`/`failed`/`return_to_sender`), and 达达 and 闪送 need polling anyway for the rider position. So a callback moves the delivery only as far as a hint, and the terminal transition is confirmed from the platform rather than from the body. Where a platform publishes no signature and no IP list — 闪送 — the compensating controls are a shared secret in the callback URL path, the mandatory re-query, and the dedup fingerprint.
- **The two 达达 signatures are not one signature.** The request side is `MD5_upper("{app_secret}{k=v&k=v…}{app_secret}")` over all params, sorted ASCII-ascending; the callback is `MD5_lower(sort_asc([client_id, order_id, update_time]).join(""))` — **lowercase, three fields, no secret**. A single shared helper implementing one will silently reject every genuine call from the other direction, so `dada/signature.rb` ships two methods, named apart. And the callback's `update_time` is hashed **as received**: it is milliseconds when `order_status == 1000` and seconds otherwise, so any parse-then-hash fails verification on that one status.
- **The platform's vocabulary is mapped in the provider, and the mapping is lossy by design.** 待接单→`pending`, 已接单/骑士到店→`pre_transit` (`available_for_pickup` when the platform means 到店), 已取货/配送中→`out_for_delivery`, 已完成→`delivered`, 已取消→`failure`, 返还中→`return_to_sender`, 妥投异常→`failure`. The platform's own code and Chinese label go into the scan's `description`/`details` verbatim — the merchant reconciling a charge needs the platform's words.
- **The dispatch attempt is the record the fulfillment cannot hold**, and it is also the reconciliation surface: `quoted_amount` beside `charged_amount`, because 已取消仍扣费 and 改派重复扣费 are the two documented daily exceptions on these networks.
- **Coordinates are converted once, per platform, and the source is declared.** The fork's canonical is GCJ-02 (the routing plan); 闪送's order detail and rider track answer **BD-09**; UU registers in BD-09 but calls back in GCJ-02; the WeChat channel takes either at six decimal places. A rider a few hundred metres off a 3 km delivery is a real error.
- **Credentials are `Spree::Integration`s, one per platform per store** — the rate-provider convention; the merchant's prepaid balance with the platform is theirs to fund.
- **The rider's identity comes from the platform; the rider's rating does not exist.** `getDeliveryInfo` serves the rider's name, the shop they belong to, a 好评率 and a tag list — shop-side data about the shop's own rider. When a platform carries the order, the name and mobile arrive in the callback (达达's `dm_name`/`dm_mobile`) and the rating and tags have **no source at all**: the payload serves what it has and the client must tolerate the rest being empty. Stated here because "the rider view is unchanged" is only true of its shape, not of every field in it.
- **Alcohol is a declared category, not a prohibited cargo — with one member that is the exception** (answered 2026-09-18). The draft's premise was wrong twice over and is corrected here: **not every platform takes a two-level category** (only the WeChat channel does), and **alcohol does have a home** on the two that matter — 达达's flat `cargoType` enum carries **24 酒品**, and the WeChat channel's 品类表 carries **日用百货 → 酒水行** (the draft's guess of 甜品饮料 is wrong: it holds only 甜品 · 奶茶果汁 · 咖啡 · 面包/糕点 · 冰淇淋). The prohibitions that exist name **酒精 as a flammable liquid** — 达达's and 闪送's registration agreements share one clause listing 汽油/煤油/酒精 — not packaged beverage alcohol, and 美团 allows 酒类 outright with two conditions (高价值商品不支持配送; 易碎商品需合格包装，否则货损归责商家), while 蜂鸟 files it under 名酒坊 behind a 食品经营许可证.
   **闪送 is the member that may not be able to carry the core product at all**: its public list has **no alcohol category**, and its internal 禁运物品 rule draws the line at a **degree threshold** (three circulating figures — 40%, 45%, 50% — none from an official page). 顺丰同城's intra-city rule and UU's own enum are unpublished. So the family's alcohol fitness is a **per-member fact**, recorded rather than assumed, and the dispatcher must not route a high-degree order to a member that may refuse it.
- **The dispatcher reads a per-carrier capability matrix, not a uniform API.** The WeChat channel publishes the table the walk needs, and its columns differ sharply: **保价** — 达达 yes, 顺丰同城 only if pre-configured in their system, UU city-dependent, **闪送 no**, 美团 only under an offline contract; **收/取货码** — 达达 收货码 only, **美团 neither**, 顺丰同城/闪送/UU both; **小费** — 达达 only while 待接单, 闪送 fixed tiers before a rider accepts, UU before completion, **顺丰同城 and 美团 no**; **cancel** — 达达 and UU only before pickup, the rest any time before completion. So the escalation step is per-member config, and a member that cannot insure or cannot take a code is not a candidate for an order that needs one.
- **Two hard caps make a single delivery impossible sometimes** — the WeChat channel's `goods_value` is `(0, 5000]` yuan and `goods_weight` `(0, 50]` kg, and **one case of 白酒 can breach the value cap**. Also relevant beyond the channel because 美团 refuses 高价值商品 outright. The plan therefore requires the dispatcher to check the declared value and weight against the member's caps **before** offering, and to fail with a reason rather than at the platform.
- **Nothing in the integration can verify age, and nothing needs to.** No platform's API exposes an age check; the client's posture is self-declaration — the login page reads 使用酒小二前请您确认, 您已年满18周岁 … 未满18岁请勿购酒, 擅自购酒后果自行承担, with 未满18岁未成年人请勿购酒 at the end of two goods lists — so the alcohol-specific settings this plan must set are the correct category, insurance with a declared value, and the 收货码, not a verification feature the client cannot drive.
- **The parcel side needs no provider at all** (ruled 2026-09-18). A posted paper invoice, a lottery prize and a physical year-gift need a tracking number shown to the customer and nothing more, and `Spree::Delivery` already models exactly that. No waybill, no label, no cloud print, no weight brackets.

## Design Details

### Supply: the gem and its members

```text
spree/providers/instant_delivery/          gem: spree_instant_delivery
  app/models/spree_instant_delivery/
    integration.rb                         < Spree::Integration (per platform, per store)
    dada/{client,signature,status_map}.rb   the platform's dialect
    dada/fulfillment_provider.rb
    dispatch.rb                            the escalation walk
  app/controllers/spree_instant_delivery/
    dada_callbacks_controller.rb           signature check → UpdateTracking + position ingress
  db/migrate/…_create_spree_instant_delivery_attempts.rb
```

- **The member is a `FulfillmentProvider`, not a `DeliveryRateProvider`** (which is the change the pricing ruling makes): the platform does not price the customer's delivery, so nothing here implements `DeliveryRateProvider` unless a merchant explicitly configures a platform-priced method (Key Decisions, "the rejected shape").
- `generates_labels?` is false — the platform's own rider app is the label — so core never asks for `purchase_label`; `requires_address?` is true.
- **A calculator-priced method carries no service row naming a courier**, and that is a consequence worth stating: `offers_service?` matches service rows against provider estimates, and an `Internal` estimate carries `carrier: nil`, so a method that holds a courier-named service row is silently dropped from checkout. The **platform identity lives on `fulfillment_provider`**, which is where the method already keeps it; the delivery's own `carrier` is written from the dispatch answer.
  - One small core note: `Fulfillments::Fulfill#tell_provider_it_shipped` forwards only `tracking_number` and `tracking_url` from the provider's result hash into `Spree::Deliveries::Create`, which already accepts `carrier:` and `service:`. Either the provider writes the carrier onto the delivery it caused, or that hash grows the two keys (backward compatible — an ignored extra key today). The plan takes the second: the contract should say who the carrier is.

### The dispatch attempt

```ruby
create_table :spree_instant_delivery_attempts do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :delivery, index: true          # the Spree::Delivery being dispatched
  t.references :integration, index: true       # which platform, which credentials
  t.string   :platform_order_id, null: false   # 达达 order_id / the channel's waybill id
  t.string   :status, null: false              # offered / accepted / expired / cancelled / failed
  t.decimal  :quoted_amount, precision: 10, scale: 2
  t.decimal  :charged_amount, precision: 10, scale: 2
  t.integer  :platform_distance_m              # the platform's own routed distance, in metres
  t.integer  :tips_amount_cents
  t.string   :cancel_reason
  if t.respond_to?(:jsonb)
    t.jsonb :details
  else
    t.json :details
  end
  t.datetime :offered_at, null: false
  t.datetime :settled_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

- **The platform's own distance is recorded beside the money** (`platform_distance_m`, returned by 达达's `queryDeliverFee` and by the WeChat channel's order add). It is there because the two numbers are not the same number: the platforms bill on a **routed** distance — 达达's own engineering note reports straight-line ×1.4 missing by more than a quarter in over 40% of cases — while anything we compute would be spherical. A band edge is therefore a policy line, not the platform's kilometre, and the decision needs their number rather than ours (Open Question 2).
- **The merchant's order number is the idempotency key.** Every platform requires a merchant-side unique number (`shop_order_id`, 达达's `origin_id`); it is the Spree order number, stable across retries — a regenerated key on retry is how one order becomes two deliveries.
- One row **per attempt, not per delivery**; the delivery keeps the accepted one's tracking number.

### The callback ingress

- One route per member, one signature rule per platform — the shape `spree_wechat_pay` already ships. The controller verifies first, maps the status through the member's `StatusMap`, then calls `Spree::Deliveries::UpdateTracking` with `scans:` and the tracking plan's ingress for the position. **It writes no rows of its own**: a second writer is how a timeline grows two sources of truth.
- **And it confirms before it commits.** A body that claims 已完成 or 已取消 is a hint; the member re-queries the order with its own signed call and writes the terminal state from that answer. This is the control that survives an unsigned 闪送 callback, a mis-verified 达达 one, and a signature nobody can compute (顺丰同城) — and because 达达 and 闪送 need polling for the position anyway, it costs a call the dispatcher is already making.
- **The 达达 ingress verifies with its own method**: `MD5_lower(sort_asc([client_id, order_id, update_time]).join(""))`, compared against the body's `signature`, with `update_time` hashed exactly as received (milliseconds under `order_status == 1000`). It must not share an implementation with the request-side signature, which is uppercase, secret-wrapped and over every parameter.
- **The dedup fingerprint must tolerate the same status arriving with different rider data.** 达达 re-sends a status on 重新分配骑士 and 骑士转单 carrying the *new* `dm_name`/`dm_mobile`; a fingerprint on `(status, occurred_at)` alone silently drops the reassignment.
- **Positions arrive differently per platform**: UU pushes coordinates in the callback; 达达 and 闪送 answer on a read, so those members poll while a delivery is in `out_for_delivery`.

### Price: the fee, the waiver and the gate

```text
The customer's fee                          The merchant's cost
─────────────────────                       ───────────────────
DeliveryMethod#calculator (Internal)        the platform's quote
   → Estimate → DeliveryRate → Fulfillment#cost
      → order.delivery_total                 → attempt.charged_amount
   ↓ waived by
Promotion + Rules::ItemTotal (满X)
Promotion + Rules::CustomerGroup (会员)
   → Actions::FreeShipping → Discount(fulfillment) = -cost
```

- **A calculator prices the method.** `FlatRate` gives the default fee the ruling names; a distance- or time-band calculator is **not** shipped (the six calculators are `FlatRate`, `FlexiRate`, `PerItem`, `PriceSack`, `FlatPercentItemTotal`, `DigitalDelivery`), so a merchant who wants distance-based pricing needs a new calculator in core — recorded as Open Question 3 rather than designed here.
- **The waiver path is the shipped promotion machinery**, and the plan adds no code to it: the merchant (or the operator) configures the promotion, its rule and the `FreeShipping` action in the dashboard, exactly as for any other promotion. What this plan adds is the **statement that this is the mechanism**, so no gem grows a second one.
- **The funding path**: a shop rule needs nothing; an operator benefit needs the seller made whole, and that is the membership plan's `subsidy` kind (`SellerTransfers::Create` already runs once at completion, which is where a shipping subsidy would be written beside the member-price one).
- **The gate is a delivery-method rule.** `DeliveryMethodRules::ItemTotalRule` already expresses 满 N 元 to see this method at all; the peak/off-peak variant needs a rule that reads the clock and the method's own thresholds, which is the one new class here, and it hooks the same seam (`Stock::Estimator#filter_delivery_methods`) that eligibility already uses.
- **What the Store API must serve**, because the client already reads it and none of it is a new endpoint: `deliveryFee` on the calc payload, the `order_service_fee` / `merchant_service_fee` lines inside `minusList` (additive), `deliveryRate.totalDeliveryRate` on the order read, `settlementWay`, and the 起送 thresholds (`peakPeriodMoney` / `unPeakPeriodMoney`) with their hint sentences. A zero fee is served as a zero number — never as a negative line.

### What happens when nobody takes it

1. Offer on the first platform; wait up to two minutes.
2. Escalate the tip where the platform allows it, and re-offer — 闪送 accepts tips only *before* a rider accepts.
3. Move to the next platform in the merchant's order.
4. On acceptance, cancel every other live attempt; on total failure, leave the order in 待配送, notify the merchant through `spree_notifications`, and let a human decide between a longer wait, the shop's own rider, and cancelling the order.

## Migration Path

1. **The gem and its three-place wiring** — `spree/providers/instant_delivery/`, the starter `server/Gemfile`, `.github/workflows/tests.yml` and the gem's own `Gemfile`; the CI gap for `providers/wechat_pay` and `providers/social_auth` closes first. Faraday per the `wechat_pay` gemspec.
2. **Confirm the two answers that came from research rather than the portal**: 达达's `cargoType` 24 酒品 with their BD or merchant backend, along with the insurance numbers (Open Question 1, narrowed to 闪送's rules and 顺丰同城's/UU's category lists for the members after it), and the callback signature rule (Open Question 2, reconstructed from two independent SDK implementations).
3. **The 达达 integration and credential**, and the shop record on the platform (a `StockLocation` maps to it).
4. **The fulfillment provider and the attempt record** — `create_fulfillment` via the platform's dispatch call, `cancel_fulfillment`, `tracking_url`, and the carrier on the delivery.
5. **The callback ingress** — signature verification, the status map, `Deliveries::UpdateTracking`, positions into the tracking plan's ingress, the reassignment case in the fingerprint.
6. **The dispatcher** — the escalation walk, the tip step, cancelling the losers, the failure notification.
7. **The price and the gate on the merchant's side** — the delivery method with its calculator and its `fulfillment_provider`, the 起送 rule (including the peak/off-peak kind), and the two promotions (`ItemTotal` + `FreeShipping`, `CustomerGroup` + `FreeShipping`) configured as the documented default. Nothing here is new code except the rule kind.
8. **The subsidy link**, once the membership plan's `subsidy` kind lands: an operator-promised waiver writes the seller's credit at completion.
9. **The second member** — 闪送 or the WeChat channel, whichever the operator can open first; a member that needs no change to anything above is the proof the family is a family.
10. Verify against a real delivery, as the tracking plan's own last step insists.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not build a fee engine.** A delivery fee is the method's calculator; a waiver is a promotion. Do not add a fee-rule kind, a delivery-fee calculator, or a negative `Fee` (a fee is non-negative by validation — credits are `Discount`s).
- **Do not let a platform's live quote reach the customer.** It is cost, on the attempt row.
- **Do not add a `free_delivery` membership right.** A tier is a `CustomerGroup` and the waiver is its promotion; the membership plan says in writing that none of its rights waives shipping.
- **Do not render a waiver as a negative service-fee line.** The client cannot; a zero fee plus a hint is the shipped shape, and a `-¥` row is a client release.
- **Do not add a Store API route for dispatch, quote or delivery-cancel.**
- **Do not write a terminal state from a callback body.** `delivered`, `failed` and `return_to_sender` are confirmed by re-querying the platform; the body is a hint. This is the only control that holds for 闪送 (unsigned by design) and 顺丰同城 (algorithm not public).
- **Do not share one signature implementation across 达达's two directions**, and do not normalise the callback's `update_time` before hashing it.
- **Do not build a second position store, and do not let a platform's status vocabulary reach `Spree::Delivery#status` unmapped.**
- **Do not treat 达达's status table as stable** — two versions circulate with `5`/`10` swapped.
- **Do not send an order to a member that cannot carry it.** Alcohol fitness is per-member (闪送 has no public alcohol category and an unpublished degree threshold; 美团 refuses 高价值商品), and so are 保价, the codes, the tips and the cancel window. The dispatcher checks both before offering.
- **Do not let a declared value or weight exceed a member's cap at the platform.** The WeChat channel's are ¥5,000 and 50 kg, and a case of 白酒 breaches the first; the failure must be ours and named, not the platform's.
- **Do not regenerate the merchant order number on a retry**, and **do not build a waybill, a label or a weight-bracket calculator** for the parcel flows.
- **Do not add a second outbound-notification mechanism.** A failed dispatch notifies through `spree_notifications`.

## Open Questions

1. ~~**Is alcohol an allowed cargo category?**~~ **Answered 2026-09-18, and narrowed**: yes on 达达 (**`cargoType` 24 酒品** — verify on the portal, which is behind a login) and on the WeChat channel (**日用百货 → 酒水行**); conditionally on 美团 and behind a 食品经营许可证 on 蜂鸟. **闪送 is the open risk** — no public alcohol category, and a degree threshold in unpublished internal rules — and 顺丰同城's intra-city rule and UU's own enum are simply not public. **What remains to verify, in order: 达达's BD or merchant backend (酒品 acceptability, and the insurance numbers — 保费 5‰ with the value rounded up to ¥100, max premium ¥50, max payout ¥10,000, while the uninsured-loss compensation is reported both as 7× 配送费 and as a ¥30 coupon, which conflict); then 闪送's 《禁运物品管理规范》 before it is ever a member for a spirit order; then 顺丰同城's and UU's category lists.** None of this blocks 达达 for beer and wine.
2. ~~**达达's callback signature algorithm**~~ **Answered 2026-09-18**: `MD5_lower(sort_asc([client_id, order_id, update_time]).join(""))` — three fields, no secret, lowercase, `update_time` hashed as received (lowercase hex; the request side is uppercase, secret-wrapped and over all params, which is why the two live in separate methods). Confirm against the live portal before shipping, and note that the portal is now 京东秒送开放平台 behind a login. **What remains genuinely unknown is 顺丰同城's `sign` concatenation** — that member's ingress keeps the fail-closed stance and the re-query confirmation until it is read off the developer centre.
3. **Does the merchant need distance-band delivery pricing? — deferred, and made decidable by data rather than by argument.** The research says the requirement is *credible* but not *present*: 美团 ships 距离加价 and 区间加价 to merchants as first-class modes for their own riders, so the shape is native to the market — but **no comparable storefront charges the buyer by distance**. 1919 快喝 advertises 全场免配送 with its distance tiers living in the *delivery promise* (≤1 km ≈19 min, 1–5 km ≈59 min), and 歪马送酒 gives free delivery above ¥29. That is a fee-waiver culture, and it is the culture this plan's waiver ruling already serves.
   **Two things are now known that a premature calculator would bury.** First, the zone workaround is unavailable here: the only usable `DeliveryZone` matcher would be postal codes, and this client carries none at all — so if bands are ever needed, a core calculator is the only path, which is why it must not be written speculatively. Second, our distance and the platform's are different numbers by construction (routed versus spherical), so a band edge is a deliberate policy line. **What unblocks it: one month of `spree_instant_delivery_attempts` — `charged_amount` against the flat fee, with `platform_distance_m` beside it.** If the flat fee covers the runs the merchant actually gets, bands are unnecessary regardless of what the platforms charge. If it does not, the parked `6.1-weight-bracket-shipping-calculator.md` is the template: a `:hash` band preference (the `Calculator::TieredFlatRate` precedent), `BigDecimal` throughout, ceiling rounding as the billing rule, origin from `package.stock_location` and destination from `package.owner.ship_address` — never `package.order`, which is nil for a cart — consuming normalized GCJ-02 coordinates and never calling a vendor itself.
