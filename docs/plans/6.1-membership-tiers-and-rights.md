## Ownership rulings (2026-09-18)

An audit of this plan and `6.1-points-and-growth-value.md` found the split between them leaking in eight places. Six of the remedies touch this plan; `fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-18) carries the full entry and the reasoning. Read these before changing anything below.

- **The grant/benefit primitive is its own foundation gem**, not this plan's. `spree_membership_grants` becomes a consumer of it rather than a private table, and the coupon wallet and the points ledger depend on that gem rather than on which plan ships first.
- **A coupon a right grants is held by the coupon wallet.** This plan records what a grant issued and points at the holding; it stores no coupon.
- **The payment legs are `6.1-scenario-purchases.md`'s** — `vip/payByWxMp` and its ChinaUMS twin — along with the rest of the scenario-payment endpoints.
- **A membership card issued by a points redemption is issued here**, through a non-purchase source on the same lifecycle, not by the points gem.
- **The activation gift bag is issued here**, calling the points ledger service and the coupon wallet's service — a three-way header declaration, not an event round trip.
- **`god/getWineValueDetailPage` stays excluded** with the rest of `god/*`; this plan's earlier handoff of it to the points plan is withdrawn.

Two further rulings, the same day, and both are this plan's to carry:

- **This plan owns the member model, and `.custom-extensions/spree_crm`'s membership is superseded by it (ruled 2026-09-18).** `Spree::CrmMembership` and `Spree::CrmMembershipPlan` already run in this fork — a lean subscription model with an expiry sweep wired into `server/config/recurring.yml`, a purchase-by-SKU path through an `order.placed` subscriber, and `auto_renew` / `grace_days` — and **neither it nor this plan mentioned the other**. Both add and remove customers from a `Spree::CustomerGroup`, which is what member prices *and* the member free-delivery waiver read, so leaving them side by side would have produced two writers of one fact. Three things are ported into this plan rather than reinvented: `auto_renew` and `grace_days` (with `past_due` in the term's statuses) onto the tier settings row; the "a tier is bought by SKU" mapping, which here resolves to the `vip` scenario kind rather than to an `order.placed` subscriber, since this plan forbids a cart purchase; and the ending-of-term sweep, which becomes this plan's job. `spree_crm` keeps the half this plan does not touch — relationships, relationship types, company roles and membership roles (the B2B surface) — and that half has **no settled approach** as of 2026-09-23: it is not to be built on or extended until a plan of its own settles the design (`fork-decisions.md`, 2026-09-23). **One writer, named:** only this plan's transitions add or remove a customer from a tier group.
- **A membership card's transfer is a row of the shared transfer primitive (ruled 2026-09-18).** `spree_membership_card_transfers` becomes a row of `Spree::Transfer` (`6.1-transfer-primitive.md`), the same row the coupon wallet and the gift-card plan use, and the transfer's status vocabulary becomes the primitive's `pending`/`accepted`/`canceled` — so this plan's `claimed` is renamed `accepted`. The client's state map does not change, because its states are derived from the card plus the transfer rather than from the word (`:338-344`). The card itself stays this plan's: it is not a `Spree::GiftCard` and nothing about it borrows the gift-card plan's semantics.

- **The six uncalled `rights/*` endpoints are not built (2026-09-22).** Open Question 1 answered the way the plan itself leans: the client never calls `getUserLevelRights`, `getUserSvipRights`, `getUserRightsShow`, `getMonthRightsTotal`, `getYearGiftList` or `getRightsAddDetailsCouponList`, and the rights page reads its list elsewhere. **Accepted consequence:** the rights model carries no per-period tally and no grant-history read; if the backend turns out to have them wired, they arrive with those two reads rather than being guessed at now.
- **The tier and its rights ship before the card (2026-09-22).** A tier is a `CustomerGroup` plus its settings row, and a customer is in one because an operator put them there — which is what the card's four transitions will later write. So the tier, its rights, the type picker and the reads are useful on their own, and the ladder is testable before anything is sold.

## Summary

A membership in this mini program is four things behind one card: the **tier** a
customer holds, the **rights** that tier carries, the **term** a purchase buys,
and the **annual gift** that hangs off the term.

Three of the four foundations already ship. The tier **is** a
`Spree::CustomerGroup` — `decisions.md` (upstream, 2026-09-04: Catalog audiences are alternatives, not layers, “a company buyer is never also priced by their customer gro”) names the group for
exactly this ("a retail loyalty tier"), it ships Admin CRUD, it reaches the
storefront on `customers/me`, and it already owns member pricing through a
`CatalogAssignment`. So this plan builds **no tier table**. It adds the four
things a group does not have — a rank, a threshold, a term and rights — as rows
beside the group, plus the card that carries a term from purchase to activation.

One correction this plan would not have found by reading the domain heading:
**the feature's endpoints are filed under six domains, not one.** The member
centre page drives the annual gift through `yeargift/*` (filed under 优惠券与促销)
and reads the card wallet through `promotionFullCard/*` (also 优惠券与促销). A plan
that trusted the §会员 heading alone would build a member centre with no annual
gift and no card. It also carries the group's banner, filed under §站点 · 地理与门店
as `home/vipPicture` — the member centre's own page furniture, owned here.

Rights turned out to be a **registered family of entitlement kinds** — neither an
enum nor a rule engine. Each kind is a subclass, so it carries its own settings
(which coupon, how many, which weekday, what multiplier) while the row carries the
operator's copy: display name, image, corner badge, rich text and a publish flag.
The ten strings the client switches on are the built-in kinds, and six of them
share one button. So the operator authors a right the way they author a tier, and a
gem adds a kind the way it adds a commission rule — and the same rule follows on
both: no fixed set in code.

Half of the `rights/*` family is never called. Six of its twelve endpoints are
declared and referenced nowhere, including the two that look like the two halves
of the tier read. The rights page does not need them — it reads the catalogue from
`yeargift/getYearRightsDetailsVo` and the customer's own rung from
`getUserVipAllInfo`.

Two rulings sharpened the boundaries (2026-09-17). The **add-on pack** the member
centre sells (`rightsCouponsVo`) is not a product: it is a bundle of promotional
coupons with a tier condition, so what it holds is the coupon wallet's, the
scenario order's existing `rights` kind buys it, and this plan owns only the tier
that may buy one. And the **birthday** is ruled to be a core column on
`spree_customers` that the core customer form accepts, rather than a profile row this
gem owns (`fork-decisions.md`, 2026-09-17) — so any store can ask for it, not only one
running this extension.

**That column has not shipped** (checked 2026-09-18). `spree_customers` has no
`birthday`, `CustomerSerializer` does not expose one and the Store customers
controller does not permit one, so this plan carries a **core change**: a migration on
`spree_customers`, the serializer attribute and the permitted param. It is named here
because a reader who takes this paragraph for a description of what exists will build
against a column that is not there.

The tier vocabulary is the **operator's** data, not a constant on either side.
The mini program hardcodes four card types in **five places that have already
drifted apart**, so copying its model would bake in an enum that takes a client
release to change (below).

## Design Details

### The tier is a `Spree::CustomerGroup` — verified, not assumed

Read from `spree/core/db/migrate/20260115120000_create_spree_customer_groups.rb`
and `..._customer_group_users.rb`:

| The tier needs | Where it already is |
| --- | --- |
| A named, store-owned audience | `spree_customer_groups` — `store_id`, `name`, `description`, `deleted_at`; unique `(store_id, name)` where not deleted; prefix `cg_` |
| Who is in it | `spree_customer_group_users` — `customer_group_id`, polymorphic `user_id`; unique on all three |
| Bulk membership changes | `add_customers` / `remove_customers` on the model, both `upsert_all` / `delete_all` |
| The member price | `has_many :catalog_assignments` → `catalogs` |
| Back-office CRUD | `Spree::Api::V3::Admin::CustomerGroupsController`, route `routes.rb:568` |
| A storefront branch | `CustomerGroupSerializer` on `customers/me` |
| A dashboard home | the `loyalty` nav group, `packages/dashboard/src/nav/default.ts:258-280` |

So there is **no `spree_membership_tiers` table and no `spree_membership_levels`
table.** Either one would be an audience model beside the audience model.

### The tier vocabulary is operator data, and the client proves why

The mini program **hardcodes the four card types in five separate places**, and
the copies have already drifted apart — the fifth is in this plan's own purchase
page, which has drifted a third way (`buy.js:332-337`: 体验, not 体验卡):

| Where | The set it hardcodes |
| --- | --- |
| `static/utils/promotion.js:90-93` | 体验卡 / 月卡 / 季卡 / 年卡 |
| `pagesA/components/goods-details/area-member/area-member.js:13-18` | 体验会员 / 月卡会员 / 季卡会员 / 年卡会员 |
| `pagesB/member/center/center.js:59-62` | 月卡会员 / 季卡会员 / 年卡会员 — **`experience` is missing** |
| `pagesB/member/interests/interests.js:59-69` | a five-entry tab list for four tiers, with `体验会员` **twice** |

One vocabulary says 卡, another says 会员; one map has three keys, another has
five tabs. So the set is fixed on the client, and copying the client's model
means shipping a four-value enum that needs a client release to change.

**The tier set is the operator's data.** A tier is a `CustomerGroup` the operator
creates, its rank and term live in `spree_membership_tier_settings`, and the
serializer returns the tier's **display name** rather than a key the client
translates. Adding a tier, renaming one, or running six of them is then an
operator action with **no client release and no server constant**. The client's
four maps are a defect this plan deliberately does not inherit.

The kind is the right's `type`, reached through the association.

`period_key` is what makes a grant idempotent: a job that runs twice on the 18th

one thing the kinds genuinely differ on — a birthday and an annual gift key on the
year, member day keys on the date, a monthly occasion keys on the year and month.
That is a method on the subclass (`period_key_for`), not a case statement in a job,
so an occasion a gem adds brings its own period rather than editing the scheduler.

### The card is the instrument, the term is the entitlement — **built 2026-09-23**

Two rows, built as designed: the card carries the buyer, the tier it grants, the
purchase that issued it, its deadline and its own status; the term carries the
customer, the tier and the window, and the card points at it. **The buyer does
not move on a claim**: `customer_id` and `activated_by_customer_id` are two
columns, and the term hangs off the card rather than owning it.

**The transitions are workflows**, and each keeps the term and the tier's group
in step in one transaction: `MembershipCards::Activate` (one transition from two
doors — the wallet's and, later, the claim's), `MembershipCards::Recycle` (作废,
or the operator's void), `MembershipCards::Expire` (a deadline nobody met), and
`Memberships::Advance` with `Memberships::AdvanceDueJob` (the hourly sweep: start,
renew, grace, end).

**A term bought while another tier runs waits for it.** Activation writes the
same tier's running term *longer*, and otherwise writes a `pending` term starting
when the running one ends — which is the client's own 自{_lowEndTime}起 warning,
and what keeps one group per customer true without a second writer. The sweep is
what starts it, and starting it is what moves the group.

**What a review of the first cut changed**, recorded because each is a rule a later
reader would otherwise undo. A **void gives back only its own card's share**: two
cards of one tier share a single term — the second extends the first's — so
recycling one shortens the term by what that card granted (`granted_days`, written
when it activated) and only ends the term, group and all, when no other card holds
it. A **card whose term has not started can still be voided**: a cancellation
clamps the cut at the window's start rather than writing an end before it. A card
for a tier **held with no end is refused** rather than written as a term that can
never start. A **renewal runs from now**, not from the lapsed end, and it is
**skipped when a successor term is already waiting** to take the tier — otherwise
the tier renews forever for a customer who has moved on. The sweep **leaves a term
whose customer is gone** rather than raising, and **one row it cannot advance does
not stop the walk**. And a **retired tier's group is still a tier's group** when a
term against it ends: the settings row is soft-deleted, the group it hung from is
not.

**And what the gift's review changed, in step 6.** A window and an activation may
not both be live for one card: the wallet's 激活 refuses while the card is on its
way — 作废 first — because activating it would strand the window, and the
recipient's claim would then be refused by a card that already has a term. The
claim is the one door where a window and an activation belong together, which is
why the guard sits at the wallet's door rather than in the workflow both doors end
at. 相赠 also refuses a card that is not **dormant and giftable** — the wallet's own
definition, which is what the client's button acts on — and its read is
token-addressed and open to guests even on a storefront that gates them, since the
recipient opens the link before there is anybody to sign in as.

**What the port added to the tier settings row**: `auto_renew`, `grace_days` and
`sku`, the three pieces worth keeping from `spree_crm`'s plan. Its SKU resolves to
the `vip` scenario kind in step 7 rather than to an `order.placed` subscriber,
which is why nothing reads it yet.

The original design note follows, unedited.

**A card is addressed by an opaque token, never by its id.** The recipient reads

### The card's state is two fields, and the client merges them

`record.js:48` derives one display status from two independent fields:

```js
cardStatus(activationStatus, donationStatus) {
  return activationStatus === 'activation' && donationStatus === 'success' ? '已赠送'
       : activationStatus === 'invalid' || activationStatus === 'recycled'  ? '已失效'
       : activationStatus === 'activation' ? '已激活'
       : activationStatus === 'expired'    ? '已过期' : undefined
}
```

`activationStatus` carries `activation` / `invalid` / `recycled` / `expired` plus
the unnamed state a dormant card sits in; `donationStatus` carries `success` and
its siblings. They are separate because a card can be given away before it is
ever activated, and the giver's copy keeps showing 已赠送 while the recipient
gets a card of their own.

**Activation pays out a gift bag.** `activateCard` answers with `svipEnd` and a
`levelRelationVo` carrying `giftIntegral` and `giftCoupon` (`mine.js:124-127`),
and the page opens its pay modal whenever either is present. A tier therefore
carries a one-time grant on entry, and the grant is typed — points and coupons.
That is why it lives with the tier's right records rather than on the card.

### The purchase is a scenario order, and it is one call

**Ruled by the operator (2026-09-17): buying a membership is a scenario order,
not an ordinary one.** Nothing ships, so an ordinary order would drag a cart, a
line item, a tax treatment and a shipment through a purchase that has none of
them. It settles through the shared payment-session flow instead — the frame
`6.1-scenario-purchases.md` builds once for all six kinds. This plan registers
the `vip` kind and owns exactly two things: what the purchase costs, and what
settling it issues.

The client is what settles it, because it never builds a cart to buy a term. The
whole purchase is **one call**, and its payload carries no cart, no line item, no
address, no quantity and no shipping choice (`pagesB/components/member/pay-suspension/pay-suspension.js:143-148`):

```js
var s = {
  svipId: this.data.svipCurList.id,                 // which package
  siteId: l.globalData.site.id,                     // where it is bought
  longitude: this.data.malllng || "",
  latitude: this.data.malllat || "",
  distributionCodeId: this.data.qrcodeId || null,   // who referred it
  svipPurpose: e                                    // 1 = for me, 2 = as a gift
};
```

What comes back is an `orderNum`, which goes straight to the gateway and then to a
read-back of what the purchase released (`:155,166-172,181-186`). There is no
address step to skip because there was never one to take: nothing under
`pagesB/member/` or `pagesB/cards/` carries a recipient, an address or a shipment.

Core does ship a way to sell something that needs no address —
`Spree::FulfillmentProvider::Digital#requires_address?` is false and
`auto_fulfill?` is true (`spree/core/app/models/spree/fulfillment_provider/digital.rb`),
with `DeliveryProfile#digital?` behind it (`delivery_profile.rb:49-52`). It is not
used here, for two reasons. It still reaches the customer through a cart and a
checkout the client never builds, and the same frame is wanted for the other five
kinds, three of which have no delivery profile at all. The term stays a
`Spree::Product` with a `Spree::Price` (`vipBuyPage` and `getSvipAmount` above) so
the operator can price, list and manage it — the *purchase* is what is not a cart.

**`svipPurpose` is why gifting needs no second endpoint.** `1` buys for the
caller and `2` buys it to give away (`:96`, from 赠好友 at
`pay-suspension.wxml:25`). Both produce a **dormant card owned by the buyer**, and
for `2` the buyer is told where it went: "购买后的会员卡礼品将暂存至【我的-常用功能-我的卡包】中，您可选择将会员卡赠给好友"
(`pay-suspension.wxml:41`), after which the page opens the card wallet rather than
the success modal and does not mark the buyer a member (`:95-99,219-224`). So a
gift purchase lands in exactly the state a self-purchase would reach after being
activated — one card, and giving it away is the same transfer either way. That is
why `spree_membership_cards.customer_id` can stay "the buyer, and it does not move
on a claim": a purchase for someone else starts there too.

**The pre-purchase check is about overlapping terms, not stock.**
`addOrderCheck` answers with nothing in the ordinary case and with a `lowerData`
object when the customer already holds a term the new one would replace
(`:69-87`); the page then warns "当前{{lowerData.name}}将于{{lowerData.endTime}}到期"
and "自{{lowerData._lowEndTime}}起，您的权益将变更为{{svipCurList.name}}权益"
(`pay-suspension.wxml:62,64`). It is advisory — the page buys on an answer and on
a failure alike (`:85,87`), since either way the purchase is what the customer
asked for. Sold out is a different verdict the page holds as its own
`goodsStatus` property before it calls anything (`:113`). The overlap warning is
the visible edge of the one-tier-group-per-customer rule below.

**Built 2026-09-24.** The answer is a list rather than the client's single
object, because the check has two things it can say and the chosen path already
says so (`membership_purchase_checks`). `overlap` names the term the purchase
waits behind; `open_ended` says a term held with no end will refuse the card —
a fact the client's own page otherwise discovers at activation, with the money
already gone. Both carry the tier's `name`, never a key the client maps, and the
one instant the check turns on: the moment the held term ends, which is the
moment the bought one begins. The client renders that moment twice, as
`endTime` and as its `_lowEndTime`, and they are one timestamp in two formats
rather than two facts — which is why the server answers it once.

**And the wait is computed in one place.** `Spree::Membership.arrival_for`
answers what a term of a given tier would find waiting — the live term of the
same tier it extends, the last live term it queues behind, or a term held with
no end that refuses it — and both the check and `MembershipCards::Activate` ask
it. Before this, the activation was the only reader of the rule, so a warning
computed separately could have described a wait the activation did not apply.

### The kind is registered, and what it asks for is a package id — **built 2026-09-23**

`Spree::MembershipKinds::Vip` answers the frame's four questions and owns no route of
its own: creating, settling, cancelling and listing the purchase are the frame's, and
the client's buy page is `pay_config`'s `offers`, which the kind fills with every tier
this store prices.

**The purchase's own vocabulary** is what the client's one call carries, stored verbatim
on the row: `tier_id` (the package, a tier setting's prefixed id — which is why the tier
settings row gained the prefix id it was missing), `purpose` (`self` or `gift`),
`site_id`, `longitude`, `latitude` and `distribution_code_id`. Only `tier_id` is read by
this plan; the rest is what the operator wants to see about where a term was sold.

**What it costs is the price of the variant the tier's SKU names** — the tier is what the
operator prices and lists, and the SKU is the link between the two. The lookup goes
through the product's lineage so only this store's variants answer, and a tier whose SKU
names nothing, or whose variant carries no price in the currency the customer is shopping
in, is **not for sale** rather than free: `price` answers nil and the frame says so.

**Settling it issues one dormant card owned by the buyer, and nothing else.** Both
purposes issue the same thing, because giving a card away is the transfer the wallet
already has — so a purchase for somebody else lands in the state a self-purchase reaches
after activation. Issuance is idempotent twice over: the settlement's own compare-and-set
and the one-card-per-purchase index behind it.

**The window to activate or give the card away is one term's length from the purchase.**
Nothing else said when a card goes stale and the plan did not name a source; the tier
already carries the length, and an open-ended tier has none, so its cards have no deadline
either — which is the same rule activation already follows for an open-ended tier.

**A refund is the only thing that takes an issued entitlement back**, and it is the
kind's own by the frame's ruling: the card is voided and the term it started ends, by the
same workflow the operator's own void runs. Nothing calls it yet — the refund path arrives
with the merchant surfaces.

### A right is a registered kind — a subclass, not an enum value

A right says **what a tier grants**, so it has the shape of `Spree::PromotionAction`
(what an event does) and not of `Spree::PromotionRule` (whether it applies). The
condition — which tiers hold it — is the association to the tier, so there is no
matcher to write at all. What varies between kinds is what they grant and how they
present, and that is exactly the axis Spree types.

This repository states the principle itself, on `CommissionRule`: *"A rule that
could only name a record — this seller, this category — left everything else
unsayable… Each of those was a schema migration and an edit to the matcher. **As
a class it is a subclass and a registration.**"* The same argument holds here, and
harder: an enum column cannot carry a coupon's own settings, and every new kind
would be a migration plus an edit to the read.

So `spree_membership_rights` is an STI table, and its column is **`type`**, never
`api_type` — the wire shorthand is derived (`Spree::MembershipRights::SurpriseRedEnvelope`
→ `surprise_red_envelope`) and the two happen to agree with the client's strings
exactly:

```ruby
class MembershipRight < Spree.base_class
  include Spree::PreferenceSchema

  has_prefix_id :mright
  acts_as_paranoid
  acts_as_list scope: :customer_group

  belongs_to :customer_group, class_name: 'Spree::CustomerGroup', touch: true

  # The right reaches the store through its tier, exactly as a commission rule
  # reaches it through its rate (`spree_commission_rules` carries no `store_id`
  # either). No second tenancy column, and nothing to keep in sync with the group.
  delegate :store, to: :customer_group, allow_nil: true

  registers_subclasses_via { SpreeMemberships.membership_rights }

  validates :type, presence: true
  # One right of each kind per tier. Among live rows only, matching the partial
  # index — a retired row is history, and a replacement is saved before the row it
  # supersedes is retired.
  validates :type, uniqueness: {
    scope: [:customer_group_id, *spree_base_uniqueness_scope],
    conditions: -> { where(deleted_at: nil) }
  }
  validate :type_must_be_registered
end
```

The shape is `spree_commission_rules` line for line: the parent reference, a `type`
string holding the class name, a `preferences` column for the kind's own settings,
`deleted_at` and `position`. The table is four columns plus its copy, and **adding a
kind adds no column at all**.

Each kind is a class under `Spree::MembershipRights::`, exactly as
`Spree::CommissionRules::*` and `Spree::Promotion::Actions::*` are. The strings the
client switches on — **nine** action constants (`interests.js:10-18`) plus
`svip_date`, which only the purchase page's substring test recognises
(`buy.js:211`) — are the **built-in kinds**, not a closed set. The client's own
vocabulary is in fact larger than the kinds: `pages/me/me.js:60-101` carries a
fourteen-entry label list mapping display names, which is presentation data rather
than a second kind list:

**Six of the ten share one button**, so the client does not distinguish them:
`setBtnInfo` maps `member_price`, `exclusive_coupon`, `coupon`, `large_coupon`,

four have behaviour of their own.

So a right is **not** a discount rule. Four of the ten grant a coupon —
`exclusive_coupon`, `coupon`, `large_coupon` and `surprise_red_envelope` — though
they reach the customer through different panels, since the surprise packet has one
of its own, and a fifth, `add_bag`, is a **bundle of promotional coupons** the
customer buys (below). One is the catalog price the pricing section already
covers, one is a birthday multiplier, one is the annual gift and one is a
calendar day. None is a percentage off and none waives shipping.
`priority_distribution` has no client behaviour of its own beyond the shared
button, so its meaning comes only from the `showName` the server sends.

**The picker is built from the registry, never from this table.** Each kind
declares `human_name`, `description` and its own `preference` fields, and
`subclasses_with_preference_schema` renders them as
`{ type, label, description, preference_schema }` for
`GET /api/v3/admin/membership_rights/types` — the shape
`PromotionRulesController#types` already uses, including the `superseded` flag so a
retired kind stops being offered while existing rows keep rendering. A gem that
wants a new kind appends to `SpreeMemberships.membership_rights` and gets a form, a
label and a place in the picker with no migration and no controller change. A
hardcoded list in the admin would be the same defect this plan rejects for tiers,
one level down.

Each kind owns the settings that make it concrete: the coupon a grant issues, the
multiplier and the trigger for the birthday kind, the weekday for member day, the
count a gift allows. Those are `preference` declarations on the subclass, so the
picker's `preference_schema` describes them and the admin renders the form without
knowing the kind.

**A kind the client has never heard of is still a right.** `setBtnInfo` has no
branch for an unknown `action`, so such a right renders as **去点单** — the client's
fallback button, which navigates to the ordering page (`interests.js:159-163,216-220`)
— and not as the dead button the earlier draft described. The genuinely dead state is
the **unpublished** one, which this plan already handles: a right whose `open` is
falsy reads 即将上线, 敬请期待 and does nothing (`interests.js:154-156`). Either way it
is a client limitation and it must not reach the domain.
The kind exists, lists on the rights page, is authored and published in the admin,
and serves through the Store API; a later client release gives it a button.

**The match is a substring test, not equality.** The purchase page filters with
`e.action.indexOf('exclusive_coupon') !== -1` (`buy.js:211`), so the server may
send a compound action and the client accepts it. The records here should still
hold one kind per row and let the read compose them: a compound string is not
something an admin picker can offer.

**A row carries content; the kind carries the default.** The kind's `human_name`
labels it in the picker, and the row's own copy is what the customer reads. Every
item on the rights page carries `showName`, `icon`, `cornerMark`, a rich-text
`introduction`, a `condition` array and an `open` flag, and the client renders each
of them (`interests.js:151-173`):

| Field | The client does | So the record needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `showName` | the panel heading (`wxml:18`) | an operator-authored display name |
| `icon` | the tab image | an image |
| `cornerMark` | a corner badge, first 3 chars (`wxml:11`) | a short operator-authored badge |
| `introduction` | `j-parse`, a 320rpx scroll box (`wxml:20`) | rich text per right |
| `condition` | `join("/ ")` → 需要会员等级达到{{…}} (`wxml:28`) | nothing — derived from the tiers holding this kind |
| `open` | `false` → disabled, 即将上线, 敬请期待, `doNothing` | a publish flag |
| `isHave` | 已达成 / 未达成 (`wxml:25`) | derived from the customer's tier |

`condition` is **derived, not stored**: it is the tiers that hold a right of the
same kind, read back through the association. A right held by three tiers is three
rows sharing one `type`, and the read collapses them into one item naming three
tiers — which is also why two member-centre sections arrive as one entry per tier,
paged by tier name. The client prints whatever names it is given, so adding a tier
costs no client change. `open` is why a row is published data and not live
behaviour: a right that is not launched yet is still authored, still listed, and
still advertised.

**The rights list itself is not this family.** The page reads it from
`yeargift/getYearRightsDetailsVo` and posts `siteId` with it
(`memberApi.js:7-8`, `interests.js:132-136`), so the list is seller-scoped like
the rest of the member surface. The `rights/*` inventory above is a separate,
mostly dead family.

### The member centre's six sections are a projection of the registry

**The client reads the same rights twice, and the two reads disagree about
sections — which settles which one is the model.** The rights page reads a **flat
list** (`yeargift/getYearRightsDetailsVo`, above): every right arrives with its own
`action`, its own `showName` and its own `open` flag, in one array, with no grouping
at all. The member centre reads the same rights **grouped** into six keys. A flat
list keyed by `action` is what a registry produces; the grouping is a second
reading of it, so the grouping is a projection and the flat list is the model. The
Store API's own rights resource is the flat one.

`getUserVipAllInfo` answers with five optional objects plus a member-day block, and
the **"N 项权益" badge is the server's own `rightsTotal`, not a count the client
derives** — `member-info.wxml:32` binds `userVipInfo.rightsTotal` and `buy.wxml:224`
binds `rightsTotal` off the rights response, and both are opaque to the client. What
the client does count (`center.js:259-262`) feeds the panels' **ordinal tags**
(`权益{{rightsOrder + 1}}`, `vip-day-area.wxml:7`), which is a different number. The client branches on the **key names**
(`center-item.js:35-40`):

| Key | Title | Shape | Kind that declares it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `vipCouponInfoVo` | 专属红包 | one coupon list, plus `nextCouponVoList` for coupons that start later | `exclusive_coupon`, `coupon` |
| `rightsLevelSurpriseVoVos` | 惊喜红包 | **one entry per tier**, paged by `svipName` | `surprise_red_envelope` |
| `yearGiftLevelSettingVos` | 开卡送酒 | **one entry per tier**, paged by `svipName` | `give_gift` |
| `rightsCouponsVo` | 会员加量包 | coupon bundles the tier may buy | `add_bag` |
| `largeCouponInfo` | 兑换大额券 | coupons exchanged upward | `large_coupon` |
| `userVipDayPageVo` | 会员日 | the member-day block, counted separately | `svip_date` |

So the key is **the kind's own declaration, not a list in the serializer.** A kind
declares the panel it presents in (`presents_as`), the read groups live rights by
that declaration, and a kind that declares an existing key joins that panel with no
change to the serializer, the model or the admin. `priority_distribution`,
`member_price` and `birthday_double_integral` declare no key — they are
first-class rights with no panel in this client, which is the correct outcome and
not a gap to paper over: they still list on the rights page and in
`GET /membership_rights`.

A kind declaring a **new** key has no panel here until a client release adds one,
and that is the honest statement of the limit: it is a limit on one view, not on the
domain. The rule that follows is that nothing in the backend may enumerate the six —
the moment the serializer holds that list, adding a kind becomes a server change
again and the registry earns nothing.

Two of the six are **tier-swipered**: the server sends one entry per tier and the
client pages through them by name (`center-item.wxml:82,109,119`). So a panel is
not "my tier's rights" — it is the ladder, with the customer's own rung marked
`isHave`. That is also why `condition` is a list of tiers rather than one. One
consequence worth stating:
the "N 项权益" badge is the server's `rightsTotal` and is opaque to the client, so
**it is this gem's to compute and it should mean what it says** — how many rights the
tier carries — rather than mirroring the panel arithmetic the earlier draft read into
the client.

### The add-on pack is a coupon bundle, and the tier is the condition

**Ruled 2026-09-17: the member add-on pack is a promotion coupon, bound to the
tier.** It is not a product of this plan and not a new scenario-order kind — the
scenario order already declares a `rights` kind whose own row names this plan
*and* the coupon wallet (`6.1-scenario-purchases.md:61`), which is exactly the
split the ruling makes explicit:

| Concern | Owner |
| --- | --- |
| What a pack holds, and the codes it yields | `6.1-coupon-wallet.md` — the bundle family at `:59-63` |
| Buying it | `6.1-scenario-purchases.md` — the existing `rights` kind |
| Which tiers may buy it, and within which term | **this plan** — the `add_bag` right |

So this plan registers no kind for the purchase and owns no pack model. It owns
one thing: the `add_bag` right on a tier, which is the condition that makes a pack
available. That is the shape of every other right — the entitlement is the tier's,
and the tier *is* the condition — so no matcher is written and no new column
appears.

The client confirms both halves. The pack half is a store: it carries `isBuy`,
`isLimit`, `isLootAll` and `limitGetTimes`, its button reads 抢购 or 已抢光, and
its detail answers with `num`, `discountMinus`, `salePrice` and `limitAmountMin`,
with the customer's saving computed as `num × discountMinus − salePrice`
(`center-item.js:265-283`). The membership half is `svipEndDate`, the member's own
term end. The purchase is the scenario frame and not this plan's:
`addCouponOrder {addCouponsId, siteId}` → `orderNum` → `payCouponByWxMp`
(`center-item.js:308-330`).

`vipCouponInfoVo` pays out instead of charging. A coupon carries `redeemable`
and `redeemableMinus`, and the button reads 最高兑换¥X when it has a redeemable
amount, **升级兑换券 when it is redeemable and carries no `redeemableMinus`** (and
is unused), and 去使用 otherwise — 已使用 only when the coupon is already used
(`center-item.js:146-148`) — an upgrade the customer claims rather than buys. The
earlier draft had the third branch's condition inverted. A coupon that has not started yet reads
"N 到账" instead, so the state is derived from `start` and `used`, never stored
by the client.

### The annual gift has two modes, and one claim path is claimed elsewhere

`yearGiftMode` decides the shape (`center-item.js:188`, `buy.wxml:173,192`):

| Mode | The gift | How it is claimed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `coupon` | `yearGiftVos` — a list of coupons | `yeargift/addSvipYearCouponRecord`, then the coupon wallet (`memberApi.js:92`) |
| `logistics` | `vipYearGiftVo` — one physical gift, `deliveryMode: 'express'` | routed to `pagesC/lottery/prize/details/details`, which posts `rights/addSvipYearGiftRecord` (`center-item.js:431`, `year-gift-pop.js:166`) |

Each entry is tier-scoped (`svipName`, `giftLevel`) and count-limited — `yetCount`
against `limitCount`, and when `canCount < 1` while `usableNum > 0` the button
reads 领取完毕 and is disabled (`interests.js:195-205`,
`year-gift-pop.js:87-104`). The entitlement read is
`vip/getSvipYearGiftRights` → `canCount`, `usableNum`, `yearGiftShow`
(`interests.js:109-119`). That is 开卡送酒 — the wine on card activation that
the activation response already advertises through `giftCoupon` and
`giftIntegral`.

**The logistics mode is the one place a membership right is claimed inside the
otherwise-excluded prize module.** The gift belongs here; its claim screen does
not. This is the same split the plan already applies to `vip/addOrder` — this
plan owns what the right *is* and what it issues, the other plan owns the frame
that displays it.

**A right's coupon can be scoped to sellers.** `yeargift/getYearGiftCoupon`
answers with `limitSite`, `moreSite` and `siteName`, and the client renders
全站通用, "{{siteName}}可用" or "{{siteName}}等多地可用"
(`year-gift-pop.js:40`). So seller scope is a property of the individual grant,
not of the membership: the balance stays account-wide (the 2026-09-17 decision)
while one grant may be spendable at one seller.

**Built (2026-09-24): the coupon mode is the right's own list of promotions, and a
claim is a grant.** `give_gift` carries three settings — the coupons a claim draws
from (`gift_promotion_ids`, in the order claims take them), how many times a member
may claim in a year (`yearly_limit`, the client's `limitCount`), and which mode the
gift is (`gift_mode`). A claim draws one coupon: the first the member has not taken,
or the one they named, handed over through the wallet and recorded as a `Spree::Grant`
of `Spree::Memberships::YearGiftClaim` — keyed on the member, **the tier**, the
store's own year and the coupon, so a claim retried is answered the coupon the first
call issued and a new year is a different key with nothing to run. The key names the
tier rather than the right row because the allowance is the tier's: an operator
retiring a gift and writing its replacement must not hand every member of that tier a
second allowance in the same year. The claim names the tier as its `source`, the
holding as what it `issued`, and the count is read and spent under the tier's own row
lock, so two claims arriving together cannot both spend one allowance. It is consumed
as it is written: it is the record that the gift was handed over, not a debt standing.

**The two counts ride the member-centre entry rather than a second read.** The client
reads the gift twice — the panel's `yearGiftLevelSettingVos` and the entitlement
read's `canCount`/`usableNum` — and they are one payload here: a kind with something
of its own to say answers `member_payload(customer:, store:)`, and the member centre
merges it into that right's entry as `gift`. The numbers differ on purpose, and that
difference is the state the client reads 领取完毕 in: `can_count` is what is left of
the year's allowance, `usable_num` is how many of the gift's coupons are still
unclaimed, and an allowance spent while coupons remain reads as exactly that.
`yearGiftShow` needs no key of its own — the entry already carries `is_have`, and the
panel is the ladder, so a customer's own rung is marked on it.

**What this leaves unbuilt, recorded rather than papered over.** The `logistics` mode
— a physical gift, claimed in the prize module this plan excludes — has its mode
declared and a claim against it refused, but no payload of its own: the right is here,
the frame that displays it is not. **Seller scope** (`limitSite`, `moreSite`,
`siteName`, and `yeargift/getYearGiftCoupon`) is a property of an individual grant and
belongs with the seller feature, so no grant here carries it. And
`GET /api/v3/store/membership_year_gifts`, the store's own configuration read, is not
built: the configuration is the right's settings and the panel answers it, so a read
that repeated it would be a second place for the client to look.

### The member banner is group-scoped, and this plan owns it

**Ruled (2026-09-17): `home/vipPicture` belongs to this plan.** The member
centre's banner differs by tier group, so the banner is resolved from the
customer's tier and is one more thing a tier carries.

The payload is `{ pic, name, pictureAreaList: [{ areaRem, link }] }` — a
background image plus absolutely-positioned tap targets (`center.js:462-476`,
`center.wxml:36-37`). The **same shape** also serves the home banner
(`home.js:524-535`) and the special page (`special.js:757`), and those two
callers are outside this plan. So this plan owns the member-centre banner
resource, not the image-map primitive: if that primitive is later extracted for
the other two callers, this resource becomes its first consumer and nothing
here changes.

**Built (2026-09-24).** `spree_membership_banners` holds one row per tier group —
`name`, `pic` and `areas` — and no store of its own, because the group it hangs
from already has one. Two details are decisions rather than accidents:

- **`pic` is a URL, not an uploaded asset.** The client renders it as a
  background image and a merchant's banner comes from their own CDN; the mini
  program's own payload carries the same field, so an upload would be a second
  thing to keep in step for no reader. An asset-backed banner is a change the
  operator screens can make when they want one.
- **An area is the client's own coordinates.** `area_rem` is a CSS style written
  in rem — the unit the client scales to the screen (1rem = 94rpx at its design
  width) — and `link` is what the tap opens. The row refuses an area that
  carries neither, or anything else, because an area the client cannot place is
  a tap target nobody can tap.

The ruled shape said `pictureAreaList`; what is built answers `areas`, with each
entry's `areaRem` as `area_rem` — the gem's own snake_case, which the client maps
the way it maps every other key. The word changed and the shape did not, and the
other two callers of this shape are outside this plan, so the mapping is recorded
here rather than assumed: a reader comparing this response with the ruled shape
should find the difference named.

`GET /api/v3/store/customers/me/membership_banner` answers the banner of the
customer's own tier, and **`null` rather than a refusal** when they are in no
tier or their tier has none: the page has nothing to show, which is not an
error, and a body that is not an object is how a client reads "no banner".
**Recorded rather than assumed** (review, 2026-09-24): the mini program's own
adapter resolves a response as its body's `data`, so a `null` body is read as
*no banner only when there is no banner* — pointing it at this endpoint means
mapping the two, which is what the port does for every read here. The operator writes it
through `GET`/`POST`/`PATCH /api/v3/admin/customer_groups/:id/banner`, which
deliberately does not normalize its parameters — a `link` is an opaque string a
merchant typed, and normalization would decode one that happens to look like a
prefixed id. What is left of this resource is the operator's screens, which are
step 9's.

### Member pricing is the tier's catalog, and the operator funds it

A tier's price is a **catalog-owned price list**: the catalog answers *who* (a
`CatalogAssignment` to the group) and its owned `Spree::PriceList` answers
*what*. `PriceList belongs_to :catalog` (`price_list.rb:19`) and `Catalog
has_one :price_list` (`catalog.rb:44`).

**Confirmed by the operator (2026-09-17):** "组不同，通过价目表定价时自动获取" —
the group differs, and the price is fetched automatically through the price
list. That is this design exactly, and it is why no member-price field exists
anywhere in the plan: grouping is the pricing mechanism.

Three facts decide it, and the alternatives are closed:

- A percentage is refused on a standalone list — `percentage_requires_catalog`
  rejects `price_adjustment_percentage` without a catalog (`price_list.rb:471-478`),
  so "member price is 10% off" is only expressible on an owned list.
- The walk consults catalog lists before rule-matched ones
  (`pricing_provider/internal.rb:48`), so a member price beats a store-wide list
  instead of losing to it.
- The audience-rule route is retired: `PriceRules::CustomerGroupRule.superseded?`
  is true (`price_rules/customer_group_rule.rb:39`) and hidden from the picker
  (the comment at `:3-7` says so; the dashboard filters on it).

A tier's list is `automatic` (a percentage derived on read, so a variant with no
row still gets the member price). **The operator funds the discount**, so the
tier catalog is the operator's and store-wide; the seller is paid as though the
discount had not happened, which needs an explicit credit back to the seller —
core does not do this today, and `SellerTransfers::Create#earned_amount` is
`order.total` less commission (`seller_transfers/create.rb:106-119`), so any
reduction the customer sees currently comes out of the seller.

**That credit is five coupled core edits, not one** (ruled 2026-09-18; the
"one core change" reading in `fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “The operator funds the member discount; core's seller ledger”) was wrong and is corrected
there). `SellerTransfer::KINDS` is frozen at `%w[earning refund_reversal]` with
the validation binding the array at class load
(`spree/core/app/models/spree/seller_transfer.rb:31,53`), so a third `subsidy`
kind cannot be added from a gem. Opening the constant is not enough, because four
other places assume the old pair: `scope :awaiting_provider` is built on
`earnings` (`:91`), so a non-earning positive row is never handed to the payout
provider; `Spree::SellerTransfers::Reverse` looks its counterpart up with
`.earnings` (`reverse.rb:42`) and `SellerTransfer#reversible_amount` returns zero
unless the row is an earning (`:128`); and `refund_id` carries a **non-partial**
unique index (`db/migrate/20260822000001_create_spree_seller_transfers.rb:46-47`)
that the reversal's `RecordNotUnique` recovery depends on (`reverse.rb:52,70`).
The settlement machinery itself needs no change — `SellerPayouts::Sweep`
partitions unsettled rows by **sign**, not by kind — and that is exactly what
makes this worth doing in core rather than as a gem-local second ledger the sweep
would never see. **The amount is `discount × (1 − commission rate)`**, persisted
when the transfer is created and never recomputed, because the base price it
compares against can move after the sale (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “Commission follows the actual selling price”)).

**The catalog fallback is exclusive and this plan accepts it.**
`Catalog.for_context` consults customer groups only when the company axis
returns nothing (`catalog.rb:153-175`), so a retail member who also buys for a
company gets the company's catalog and no member price.

### The subsidy is written where the earning is, and reverses with it

**Built 2026-09-22.** The operator's credit is not a second ledger beside the
seller's: it is a third kind of row in the one they already read, and the
workflow that writes an earning writes it too.

- **The discount is the extension's, the arithmetic is core's.**
  `Spree::SellerTransfers::Create` gathers a `funded_discounts` hook after the
  earning exists — so an extension that raises cannot cost a seller the money
  they are owed — and any handler contributes what the platform funded, per
  line. Core turns that into money: each line's reduction less the commission
  the platform did not charge on it, read off the line's own commission row.
  **Only a percentage rate moves with the price**; a flat fee charges the same
  on a discounted line as on any other, so none of that reduction costs the
  seller. Nothing contributed, nothing written — a promotion, a store-wide
  agreement or a price the seller set is the seller's own concession and stays
  theirs.
- **Both rows are handed to the provider the same way**, so a seller whose
  account cannot be paid yet keeps the subsidy pending beside the earning, and
  `SellerTransfers::ExecutePendingJob` sends both once they are payable.
- **A refund takes back one reversal per credited row**, and the subsidy comes
  back at exactly the fraction the earning does — so a refunded order returns
  the same proportion of everything the platform paid, however the refund was
  attributed to lines. That is why the refund key became `(refund, reversed
  row)`: one refund, two reversals, each keyed to what it reverses. Each
  reversal settles and is clawed back in the currency its own row settled in.
- **The tier's price is a percentage on its owned list**, stored negative
  because a list's factor is `1 + percentage / 100`. The tier row carries the
  catalogue's id and reports the percentage back from it, so the figure an
  operator reads is the number a member is charged — one reader
  (`member_price_list`) answers for the operator's read, the funded order and the
  subsidy's own record alike. `member_discount_percentage` is a virtual
  attribute: assigning it stands the catalogue, the list and the assignment up,
  or moves the price, inside the tier's own save — and a tier that is retired
  takes its catalogue out of effect, because its members keep the group they were
  in.
- **Both read as an enrichment.** A refund on an order whose earning is worth
  nothing reverses what it can and writes nothing for the rest, and a funding
  handler that raises contributes nothing to an earning that is already written.
  Neither may raise: the one would leave the platform's money with the seller,
  the other would lose the seller's own.

### What a tier says it saves, before anybody buys it — **built 2026-09-24**

`vip/getSaveMoneyConfig` is not a computation. The popup it fills quotes a
monthly figure — 开通超级VIP每月约省 — and four rows of copy: what a member saves
on an order, the coupons the tier carries, the days it adds, the gift it hands
over. The client renders each row against its own icon, in the order it received
them, and skips any row whose title or body is blank.

What a member *actually* saves cannot be answered before they spend: it is their
basket times whatever the tier's member price takes off it. So the figure is the
operator's, and so is the copy. The tier settings row grows a `preferences`
column and `Spree::MembershipTierSetting` declares the ten fields — four title
and body pairs, the rules paragraph under them, and the monthly amount.

**Four rows, and the wire keeps the client's order.** The rows are named for
what they say rather than numbered — `saving_order`, `saving_coupon`,
`saving_days`, `saving_gift` — because an operator's form has to label them, and
the read answers them in that order, which is the order the client's icons are
in. A row nobody has written is answered blank rather than dropped: the client
places its icons by position, so a shorter list would shift them.

**The tier, not the client's card type.** The client asks with its own `type` —
`experience`, `month`, `season`, `year` — which is the vocabulary this plan
already refuses to key on. The read takes a `tier_id` instead, the same id the
buy page's own packages carry, and the client's adapter maps from what it
renders rather than the server learning four words it must not fix.

**And it is authenticated**, like the ladder and the purchase checks beside it:
the popup only opens from a page behind a token.

**A declaration read by its own key, and the write that got past it.** Spree
reads a declared preference by the key's own name and that name is a *symbol*,
while an API payload, an import and a console all send strings. A string-keyed
row is therefore one every reader answers with the default — silently — and the
models here now symbolise the column on the way in, so both spellings land on the
one the readers use. Two consequences a later reader should know. It fixes writes
that happen *after* it: a row already written with string keys still reads blank,
and the next save writes the blank back — the check is
`record.preferences.to_h.keys.any? { |key| key.is_a?(String) }` over
`spree_membership_rights` and `spree_membership_tier_settings`. And a wholesale
`preferences` write skips the typecast each preference's own writer does, so the
tier setting validates what a declared type may hold; without it an operator
typing a word into the monthly figure would take the whole popup down for that
tier with a raw Ruby message. What that validation deliberately does *not* do is
refuse a key nobody declared: core's own `apply_preferences` ignores one rather
than refusing it, so a client echoing back a wider payload is not an error, and
the key is a value no reader will ever look at.

### One tier group per customer, and it has to be enforced

`Catalog.for_customer_groups` returns the catalogs of **any** group the customer
is in, ordered by catalog position (`catalog.rb:126-135`), and the walk takes the
first that yields a price (`internal.rb:48-51`). A customer in two tier groups
is therefore priced by whichever tier catalog sits lower — silently, and not by
their higher tier. That is the shape of V-3570 (`decisions.md`, 2026-09-04): the
code was right, the setup was wrong, and nothing warned. A tier change moves the
customer between groups in **one transaction**, and the gem refuses a second
tier group.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not build a second audience model beside `Spree::CustomerGroup`.** No
  `spree_membership_tiers`, no `spree_membership_levels`. Everything is rows
  beside the group.
- **Do not fix the tier set in code, on either side.** The client hardcodes four
  card types in five places that already disagree (above), and the set is the
  operator's data. Return the tier's display name from the serializer, never a
  key the client maps — adding, renaming or renumbering a tier must cost no
  client release and no server constant.
- **Do not model a right as a rule.** A right is an entitlement, not a condition:
  the tier it belongs to *is* the condition, so there is no matcher to write. Its
  kind has the shape of `Spree::PromotionAction`, and its variations are typed
  subclasses rather than settings on one table.
- **Do not encode a right kind as an enum, a constant list or a serializer branch.**
  A kind is a registered subclass of `Spree::MembershipRight` listed in
  `SpreeMemberships.membership_rights`, with a `type` column, its own `preference`
  declarations and a `presents_as` panel — the `CommissionRule` pattern, in this
  repository's own words: *"As a class it is a subclass and a registration."* The
  ten strings the current client switches on are the built-in kinds, not a closed
  set. A serializer that enumerates the six member-centre keys, or a model
  validating `type` against a literal array, rebuilds the problem this design
  removes: an installed gem could not add a kind without editing this gem.
- **Do not let the client's missing button reach the domain.** A kind this client
  has no branch for renders 去点单 rather than a dead button; it is still authored, published,
  listed on the rights page and served by the Store API. Do not refuse to create
  one, do not hide it from the admin picker, and do not treat the client's
  ten-string vocabulary as the server's.
- **Do not target the member price through `PriceRules::CustomerGroupRule`.** It
  is grandfathered — existing rules keep matching, new setups reach the audience
  through a catalog that owns the price list. The *promotion* rule of the same
  name is unaffected and remains a live way to target a group.
- **Do not add a member-price column to the tier, and do not store the
  percentage on the tier row.** The price is a percentage on the tier's
  catalogue-owned list and nothing else; `member_discount_percentage` is a
  reader over that list and a writer that stands the catalogue up.
- **Do not let anything but an extension's own contribution reach the subsidy.**
  Core funds only what a `funded_discounts` handler contributes — a promotion, a
  store-wide price list or a seller-set price must contribute nothing, because
  those are the seller's concession and already come out of their earning.
- **Do not key a reversal on the refund alone.** One refund reverses every
  credited row the order carries, so the natural key is `(refund, reversed row)`;
  a lookup by refund answers one of them and a unique index on the refund refuses
  the second.
- **The funding hook is an enrichment, not a gate.** A handler that raises is
  reported and contributes nothing; it must never unwind a run whose earning is
  already written, because the earning is money the seller is owed and no
  redelivery comes back for it. The cost is the mirror image of the accepted gap:
  a promise nobody could read leaves no subsidy, and the error report is what an
  operator has to work from.
- **Do not cast an id to an integer.** A contribution keyed by a line's id and
  the rate read back for it are keyed the same way — as strings — because a store
  whose keys are not integers collapses every `to_i` key onto zero and credits the
  whole reduction including the commission the platform did not charge.
- **Do not assume a member price layers over a company catalog.** The fallback is exclusive.
- **Do not store a grant without its idempotency key.** The shared row keys a grant
  `(store, kind, idempotency_key)`, and this plan's kind supplies the key as
  `"<right api_type>:<period_key>"`; the period is the right kind's own computation
  (`period_key_for`) rather than a case statement in the job that runs it.
- **Do not give a grant its own kind column.** An occasion is a right kind. A
  `kind` list reading `birthday`/`annual`/`member_day` restates three of the ten
  kinds and drifts from them the first time either list changes.
- **Do not put a membership card on the gift-card tables.** A membership has no
  face value to spend, so it is not a `Spree::GiftCard`, and it is not addressed
  to a recipient at checkout, so `spree_gift_card_recipients` cannot hold it either.
- **Do not route a membership purchase through a cart or an `Spree::Order`.** The
  client buys in one call and never builds a cart (above), so an order-shaped
  purchase invents a checkout the app does not have and makes a term look like a
  shipment. Register the `vip` kind with the scenario order instead.
- **Do not model the member add-on pack as a product, an order of its own or a
  scenario kind.** Ruled 2026-09-17: it is a bundle of promotional coupons, and
  the scenario order's `rights` kind already carries its purchase
  (`6.1-scenario-purchases.md:61`). This plan's whole contribution to it is the
  `add_bag` right naming the tiers that may buy one.
- **Do not give the birthday a table of its own or a gem-owned profile row.**
  Ruled 2026-09-17: it is a nullable `birthday` date on `spree_customers` and the
  core customer form accepting it, because any store may want birthday marketing
  and one column is smaller than a row that has to be joined to be read.
- **Do not change `Spree::GiftCards::IssueFromOrder`, and do not sell a membership
  product through a cart.** **None of this substrate exists yet** (checked
  2026-09-18): `spree_products.gift_card`, the recipient table and
  `Spree::GiftCards::IssueFromOrder` are phase 2 of
  `6.0-6.1-loyalty-gift-cards-store-credits.md`, which that plan itself marks
  unbuilt (`:3`, `:88`). The constraint below is written against that substrate **as
  designed**, and this plan depends on that plan delivering it — a dependency its own
  header must declare once this is fixed.
  Both products would carry the shared non-physical flag
  (`spree_products.gift_card`), and an earlier draft of this constraint concluded
  that the issuer therefore needed a discriminator. It does not. The issuer runs on
  `order.paid`, and a membership is issued on a scenario order's settlement, which
  creates no `Spree::Order` — so `order.paid` never fires for one. The gift card's
  own purchase is a scenario order too, so on this client the workflow runs only for
  the storefront cart channel that phase 2 designs it for. The residual exposure is
  a membership product put into a cart, and that is this gem's requirement to
  prevent where a tier binds a product, not something the gift-card issuer detects.
- **Do not serve the transfer read under `customers/me`, and do not address it by
  the card's id.**
- **Do not build a membership card transfer table.** The gift is a row of
  `Spree::Transfer` (`6.1-transfer-primitive.md`), with that primitive's statuses
  and its own one-pending-window index; this plan implements the contract on
  `Spree::MembershipCard` and nothing else. The four transitions are still this
  plan's, but 相赠 and 领取 are the primitive's service calling back here.
- **Do not let a second engine add customers to a tier group.** `.custom-extensions/spree_crm`
  ships `Spree::CrmMembership`, which maintains the plan's `CustomerGroup` membership
  the same way this plan will, and **it is superseded for consumer membership** (ruled
  2026-09-18). This plan's transitions are the only writer: the CRM engine keeps
  relationships, relationship types, company roles and membership roles, and its
  membership models, its `order.placed` purchase subscriber and its `ExpireLapsedJob`
  registration are removed once this plan's equivalents land. Their three useful
  pieces — `auto_renew`, `grace_days` and the purchasable SKU — are ported rather
  than re-invented, and the SKU resolves to the `vip` scenario kind rather than to a
  subscriber, because the purchase never builds a cart.
- **Do not let the tier's price and the term's status disagree.** Member pricing
  reads the group; the group is written by this plan's transitions alone; a term
  that ends must leave the group in the same transaction.
- **Do not give a term a `membership_card_id`.** The card carries the link, and
  the term reads it (`has_one :card`). A column pointing back at the card is a
  cycle in which the two ends can disagree.
- **Do not move a card's buyer when it is claimed.** `customer_id` is who bought
  or was granted it, `activated_by_customer_id` is who is entitled, and a card
  given away stays in the giver's record as 已赠送.
- **Do not start or end a term anywhere but through the four transitions.** An
  activation writes one, the sweep advances them, and a void ends one; a direct
  `update(status:)` is how a term and the tier's group come apart.
- **A card is never edited, only moved between states.** The customer's own
  activation and the operator's void are the same `Recycle`/`Activate` workflows,
  which is why the admin surface has one write and no update.
- **Do not add a refund action to any client.** The mini program has none. A
  refunded membership is handled on the server: `ends_at` is an absolute
  datetime the client renders verbatim, so shortening or voiding it needs no
  client change.
- **A gem that ships tables is a new pattern here.** No gem under
  `spree/providers/` has a real `db/migrate`, so the install path is unproven —
  the host app needs the migrations copied and run, and the worktree template
  rebuilt afterwards. This gem is likely the first, so it documents and proves
  that path.
- **Wire the gem in three places or its specs never run:** the starter
  `server/Gemfile` path block, `.github/workflows/tests.yml`, and
  `scripts/test/rspec`.
- **Answer "which tier is this customer in" with the one expression, and give it
  one home the next time a caller is touched.** That expression is
  `Spree::MembershipTierSetting.for_store(store).for_customer(customer)`, and it
  is now spelled out at four call sites — `Memberships::MemberCentre#tier`,
  `Memberships::PointsMultiplier`, `Memberships::FundMemberDiscount` and
  `MembershipBanner.for_customer` (which reaches it through a scope of its own,
  the fifth spelling of the same question). The lookup is one line, so the cost
  today is small; the risk is that a sixth caller spells it slightly differently
  and the banner, the member centre and the points multiplier begin answering
  different tiers for the same customer. This is a deferred cleanup, recorded
  rather than done: collapse it into a single reader when a slice next opens one
  of the four.

2. **Buying an open-ended tier you already hold takes the money and grants

   review of the pre-purchase warning (2026-09-24). A tier with no
   `validity_days` is held for good; `offers` sells it like any other package;

   the card goes `active`, and no day and no right is added. Three ways out, none
   of them one this plan can pick alone: warn from the check (a third kind, which
   is a client contract change), refuse at activation (a change to a merged
   transition), or stop selling an open-ended tier to somebody who holds it (a
   ruling about what an open-ended tier is *for*). **Deferred, not lost:** the
   read the plan asked for is built, and the hole predates it.

3. **The wait can name an instant that has already passed.** A term whose
   `ends_at` went by but which the hourly sweep has not reached yet, and a

   be a time in the past, and the client's 自{date}起 sentence then promises a
   change that already happened. The activation writes that same past instant as
   the term's `starts_at` (preserved from before this work, and the sweep starts
   it on its next pass). The fix is one clamp — the later of the end and now —
   but it belongs in `arrival_for`, because clamping only the display would put
   the warning and the activation back into disagreement, and it needs the grace
   window's own semantics settled first: a term in grace is *supposed* to have an
   end behind it. **Deferred until the member day's slice touches the sweep.**

**A stated limit, not a question.** The warning is answered for the *buyer*,

claims). So a buyer who holds nothing can claim a card into an open-ended term

pre-purchase warning is the buyer's — they are the one paying — so this is
recorded rather than fixed, and the claim's own refusal is what the claimer sees.
