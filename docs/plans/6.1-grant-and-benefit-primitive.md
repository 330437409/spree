## Summary

Five places in this plan set need the same idea: **the store owes this customer something, it has a source, and it stops being owed at some point.** A membership right that grants a gift (§会员), a coupon a draw hands over, a points lot with an expiry, a welfare gift card, a referral reward. Today three of them are three unrelated tables — `spree_membership_grants` keyed on a right with a `period_key`, `spree_coupon_holdings` keyed on a coupon code with a nullable holder, `spree_point_grants` a lot with `remaining` — and the architecture plan's own words are that they should not be: *"A small shared model the loyalty-family gems depend on, so a coupon and a membership right are not two unrelated tables"* (`:64`).

A ruling on 2026-09-18 moved the owner away from "whichever of the three ships first" to a foundation gem of its own, because leaving it to the first builder makes the other two wait on it and because the two that already exist **disagree on what a grant is** (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-18, “The loyalty family's shared ownership: a foundation gem for ”)). This plan is that gem — **with one amendment made the same day, and it is the amendment that makes the rest work: the row itself lands in core**, and the gem owns the kinds and the service. `6.1-coupon-wallet.md:127` had already recommended exactly that placement for its own holding, on the grounds that a gem would otherwise have to decorate `Spree::CouponCode` and `Spree::Order`; putting the base row in core answers that argument once for every consumer instead of once per consumer, and a new core table plus a new core base model is the cheapest kind of core change there is — **new files, no edits to any file upstream owns**.

**Two things the audit of 2026-09-18 turned up, and both are answered below rather than left open.** First, the four consumers do not share a shape — they share a *vocabulary*: a points lot is consumed in part, a coupon is one-shot and may be unclaimed, a membership grant is unique per period and computed by its kind, and a welfare card's grant row has neither an amount nor an expiry because the issued card carries both. That is why "shared" means one table with kinds and per-kind side tables, not a base class over per-family tables. Second, `6.1-coupon-wallet.md:127` recommends **core** for its own holding, on the grounds that a gem would otherwise have to decorate `Spree::CouponCode` and `Spree::Order` — and the same day's follow-up ruling adopted it for the whole primitive: the row is core, the kinds and the service are the gem.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

The four rulings behind the design, dated because each constrains the others:

- **"Shared" means one table with kinds, not a base class over per-family tables (ruled 2026-09-18).** The architecture plan's own justification is that a coupon and a membership right should "not be two unrelated tables" (`:64`), and a shared ancestor with three separate tables keeps exactly the criticism it was written to answer. **Accepted trade-off:** the row carries nullable columns — a holder may be unclaimed, an expiry may be absent — and the discipline that keeps them honest is the registry, not the schema.
- **The row lands in core; the gem owns the kinds and the service (ruled 2026-09-18).** This is the amendment to the same day's ruling recorded in `fork-decisions.md`. It answers the coupon wallet's placement argument for every consumer at once, it follows this fork's own doctrine that a generic capability goes into core, and by the fork's merge-cost rule it is nearly free: **a new table and a new base model are new files**, where an edit to an upstream file is a permanent tax.
- **The gem is `spree_grants` (ruled 2026-09-18)**, at `spree/extensions/spree_grants/`. It takes the architecture plan's own word, matches the model it serves, and collides with none of the names the other plans already hold (`Spree::CouponHolding`, `Spree::PointAccount`, `Spree::Membership*`).
- **The welfare grant is a kind of this primitive (ruled 2026-09-18).** It has neither an amount nor an expiry and points at a card that already exists (`6.1-gift-card-purchase-and-transfer.md:56`) — which is a kind whose `consumable?` and `expires?` both answer false, not a reason for a fourth grant-shaped table.
- **The primitive is a row plus a registry plus a service, and no consumer gets its own copy of any of the three.** The row says what is owed; the registry says what kinds exist and how each one computes its own idempotency key; the service is the only door.
- **What is genuinely common is the vocabulary, not the columns.** Owner (`customer_id`), `kind`, `source` (polymorphic), `granted_at`, `expires_at`, `status`, and a polymorphic pointer at whatever was issued. Everything else is a kind's business — and **the kinds that need more get a side table beside the row, not a wider row**: the points lot's `remaining` and its allocations stay in the points plan's own tables, keyed to the grant.
- **Expiry is a date fact, never a status a job flips.** The points plan settled this against `Spree::GiftCard`'s discipline (`6.1-points-and-growth-value.md:202-222,566-568`) and it becomes the primitive's rule for every kind: `expires_at` is read, `scope :usable` / `:expired` are queries, and the "no job flips anything" constraint is stated once here rather than re-derived by each consumer. **The coupon wallet's `expired` status value is reconciled by making it a display status, not a stored one** (ruled 2026-09-18): `Spree::GiftCard` already does exactly this — `DISPLAY_STATUSES = statuses + %w[expired]` (`gift_card.rb:23-25`) with `display_status` derived (`:165-173`) — so a coupon holding's stored status never takes that value and its expiry is the same read every other kind does.
- **Idempotency belongs to the kind.** A membership grant is unique on `(store, customer, right, period_key)` and the key is computed by the kind (`period_key_for`, `6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:294-300`); a coupon is unique on its code; a points lot is unique on its source. The primitive therefore takes an idempotency key from the kind rather than imposing one, and `grant!` is a no-op when the key is taken — which is what makes a job that runs twice harmless.
- **The service performs no issuance and holds no money.** A coupon a grant releases is held by the coupon wallet (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-18, “The loyalty family's shared ownership: a foundation gem for ”)), a membership card by the membership plan, a points credit by the points ledger. The primitive records *that something was owed* and whether it was taken; the thing itself stays with the plan that owns it, per the rule the points plan already carries (*"the issue step is registered by the plan that owns the thing issued"*, `:582-583`).
- **Kinds are registered subclasses, built the way this repository already builds them.** `registers_subclasses_via` + `find_by_api_type` + `subclasses_with_preference_schema`, exactly as `Spree::CommissionRule` does (`spree/core/app/models/spree/commission_rule.rb:46,48`), so a kind appears in a picker without a case statement and an extension can add one without touching this gem. The concern's own comment says the registry is *"a lodger rather than a natural fit"* in `PreferenceSchema` and suggests extracting `Spree::RegisteredSubclasses` when a family needs it without preferences (`preference_schema.rb:9-14`) — this plan does not need that extraction, and says so rather than doing it speculatively.
- **The primitive is not a second points ledger.** `Spree::StoreCreditEvent` is the shape a balance ledger copies, and the points plan's ledger is its own; this gem never sums to a balance and never writes a ledger row.
- **The gift card's welfare grant is a kind** (ruled 2026-09-18, above): it carries no amount and no expiry and points at an already-issued card (`6.1-gift-card-purchase-and-transfer.md:56`), which is a kind whose `consumable?` and `expires?` both answer false — not a reason for a fourth grant-shaped table.

## Design Details

### The row

**The table and `Spree::Grant` live in `spree/core`** (ruled 2026-09-18); the gem subclasses nothing and owns no row of its own. A consumer's kind is a gem-local subclass of the core model — which is not decoration, and is the case the extension boundary explicitly allows: a gem may add its own classes, it may not reopen someone else's.

```ruby
create_table :spree_grants do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :customer, index: true            # nil while a coupon sits unclaimed
  t.string   :kind, null: false                  # the registered subclass
  t.string   :status, null: false                # granted / claimed / consumed / revoked
  t.string   :idempotency_key, null: false       # supplied by the kind
  t.string   :source_type, :source_id            # what caused it: an order, a right, a campaign
  t.string   :issued_type, :issued_id            # what it released, owned by another plan
  t.datetime :granted_at, null: false
  t.datetime :expires_at
  if t.respond_to?(:jsonb)                       # PostgreSQL jsonb, plain json elsewhere
    t.jsonb :metadata
  else
    t.json :metadata
  end
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

- `Spree::Grant` inherits `Spree.base_class`, includes `Spree::SingleStoreResource` and `Spree::Metadata`, uses `has_status` and `acts_as_paranoid`, and publishes lifecycle events. Uniqueness is `(store_id, kind, idempotency_key)` with `scope: spree_base_uniqueness_scope`, enforced by an index — the registry's kinds differ in *how they build the key*, not in whether one exists.
- **`customer_id` is nullable on purpose**: the coupon wallet reserves a code by a campaign draw before anyone claims it, and a transferred code sits unclaimed (`6.1-coupon-wallet.md:45`). A non-null owner would break the one consumer whose artifact exists before its holder does.
- **No amount column.** An amount belongs to the thing owed — a points lot's balance, a gift card's value — and putting one here would make the row a second source of truth for a number another plan owns.

### The registry and the kinds

```ruby
class Spree::Grant < Spree.base_class
  registers_subclasses_via { Spree.grant_kinds }
end
```

- `Spree.grant_kinds` is a registry the gem's engine appends its built-ins to, and a consumer's gem appends its own — the `config.after_initialize` pattern the provider gems use, with the same comment about core assigning its registries in its own `after_initialize`.
- A kind answers three things: `idempotency_key_for(context)`, `consumable?` (one-shot, partial, or not consumable at all — a welfare grant is neither), and `expires?`. Nothing else is required of it, and a kind that needs more columns adds its own side table keyed to the grant.
- Built-in kinds in this gem are **only the ones with no owning plan**, and today that is **none**: `referral_reward`, named by the architecture plan (`:64`), sat here as the single built-in until `6.1-referral-invitations.md` claimed it as that gem's kind (2026-09-18, `fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-18 (later), “The last five plans: eight rulings”)) — the loan the header of that plan declares. **The annual gift is not one of them** — it is a membership right kind (`give_gift`) whose claim *produces* a grant, so it is a consumer, not a kind of this primitive.

### The service

```ruby
Spree::Grants.grant!(customer:, kind:, source:, idempotency_key:, expires_at:, issued: nil)
Spree::Grants.claim!(grant, customer:)   # an unclaimed grant finds its holder
Spree::Grants.consume!(grant)            # the kind decides what that means
Spree::Grants.revoke!(grant, reason:)
```

- `grant!` **never issues anything**: it records the debt and, when the caller passes what it released, points at it. The caller that issues — the membership plan, the coupon wallet, the points ledger — does so in its own transaction and passes the result in, which is how the "issue step belongs to the owning plan" rule stays true while the record is shared.
- `consume!` delegates to the kind. For a one-shot kind it stamps the row; for a partial kind it calls the owner's service (the points plan's spend, which does its own locking and writes its own ledger row) and the primitive records the consumption rather than performing it. **This is the boundary that keeps the primitive from becoming a second ledger.**
- Reads are scopes, not services: `usable` excludes what is expired or consumed, and `expiring_before(date)` is what a warning banner needs. Expiry is read here exactly as `Spree::GiftCard` reads it (`gift_card.rb:151-153,53-59`).

### What this gem does not do

No endpoints, no controllers, no serializers, no dashboard plugin — the anatomy asks every gem for those *where it has a surface*, and this one has none. It ships a model, a registry, a service, a migration and specs. The one admin surface a reader might expect — a picker of kinds — belongs to whichever consumer renders it, through `subclasses_with_preference_schema` on the kinds that have preferences.

## Migration Path

1. **Fix the CI gap first**, as the architecture plan requires before an eighteenth gem (`:170`): `providers/wechat_pay` and `providers/social_auth` are absent from `.github/workflows/tests.yml:59,104,136,296`.
2. **The core addition** — `spree_grants`, `Spree::Grant` and the `Spree.grant_kinds` registry hook, in `spree/core` and nothing else. It is `SingleStoreResource`, `Metadata`, `has_status` and paranoid, publishes lifecycle events, and **edits no existing core file**: the registry is a new key in `Rails.application.config.spree`, assigned where core assigns its own. This step is what the coupon wallet's placement argument needed answered, so it lands before any consumer.
3. **Create `spree/extensions/` and the `spree_grants` gem** — the kinds, the service and the built-ins no other plan owns — wired in the three places, with `scripts/test/rspec` gaining the `spree/extensions/$engine/spec` branch (`scripts/test/rspec:30-37`) and `CLAUDE.md`'s monorepo table gaining the row. Those two steps `6.1-administrative-division-gem.md:110` also claims; whichever gem lands first performs them once.
4. **Wire the consumers**, each keeping its own artifact and calling the service: the membership plan's grants become rows of the core table keyed by its kind's `period_key_for`; the coupon wallet's holdings do the same for its codes; the points plan's lots keep `remaining` and their allocations in its own side table and record consumption through the service; the gift-card plan's welfare grant becomes a kind that is neither consumable nor expiring. **Each consumer's `Depends on` header is amended in the same change** — the architecture plan requires the declaration in both headers (`:168`).
5. **Reconcile the expiry contradiction** — ruled 2026-09-18: the coupon wallet's `expired` becomes a **display status derived from the date**, the `Spree::GiftCard` pattern, so the stored status never takes that value. The coupon plan's `status` list and its `has_status` declaration change accordingly in the same step.
6. **Record the amendment** — that the row lands in core rather than in the gem — in `fork-decisions.md`, and close the architecture plan's Open Question 3 with the answer rather than leaving it as "whichever ships first".

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not give this gem an endpoint, a controller or a dashboard surface.** It is infrastructure; a surface belongs to the plan that owns the thing owed.
- **Do not let it hold a balance or write a ledger row.** A balance is the points plan's, a card's value is the gift-card plan's, and the primitive only records what was owed.
- **Do not add an amount column** to the grant row, and do not let a consumer's amount live in two places.
- **Do not make expiry a status a job flips.** `expires_at` is read; nothing schedules anything.
- **Do not let a consumer issue its own artifact through this gem.** The owning plan issues, this gem records.
- **Do not add a column to the shared row that only one kind needs.** A kind's extra semantics go in its own side table keyed to the grant — that is what keeps the row carrying the vocabulary rather than the union of four shapes, and it is the rule the points plan's `remaining` and allocations already follow.
- **Do not add an extension-to-extension dependency to a consumer without naming this gem in its header**, and do not let this gem depend on a consumer: it is the floor, and a floor that depends on a wall is not one.

## Open Questions

The four questions this plan opened — what "shared" means, where the coupon holding lives, whether the welfare grant is a kind, and what `consume!` promises across kinds — were settled on 2026-09-18 and now sit in **Key Decisions** with their trade-offs. The fourth follows from the first: consumption is delegated to the kind, because a primitive-level quantity would put points' partial arithmetic back into the shared row it was just taken out of.

Two items are genuinely deferred. **The `Spree::RegisteredSubclasses` extraction** the preference-schema concern itself suggests (`preference_schema.rb:9-14`) is not needed by this gem — its kinds carry no preference schema — and waits for a family that needs the registry without one. And **`consume!`'s error semantics** (a no-op or a refusal when a one-shot kind is asked twice) are left to the kind's own service, because the two consumers that already exist answer differently and neither has been contradicted by the client.
