## Summary

The mini program runs 拼团 across 21 endpoints: a merchant publishes an activity with a required team size and a discounted price, a customer opens a team, others join it, and the team either reaches its size inside the window or fails and every member is refunded. Spree has nothing for this — a repository-wide search for group-buy, team-buy or groupon returns only unrelated hits. What it does have is a stock reservation primitive (`Spree::StockReservation` with reserve, release, extend and an expiry job) which looks like the right tool and, on inspection, is not: reservations live ten minutes by default, they must belong to exactly one cart or one order, they die with their line item, and the availability calculation subtracts every active reservation from the variant's global availability. A team of fifty is a hold measured in days. That mismatch is the whole difficulty of this plan, and it is stated first because it may change the answer.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **The activity and the team are separate records.** The source shows it plainly: `activityId` is the offer and `groupId` is one team, a team has its own member list, countdown and outcome, and several teams run against one activity at once. One table with a self-reference would make every activity query filter teams out.
- **The lifecycle is workflows, not callbacks**, matching the returns plan: `GroupBuys::Open`, `Join`, `Succeed`, `Fail`, `Expire`. Succeeding and failing each touch many rows and may call an external refund, so each is a transaction with an explicitly declared external step rather than a chain of `after_commit` hooks.
- **A failed team's refund is one job, idempotent per membership, and it does not settle the money in the same transaction that fails the team.** Fifty members means fifty gateway refunds. WeChat refunds are asynchronous and rate-limited, and `Refunds::Create` serializes on the payment, so the fan-out is per payment and the work is a queue, not a loop.
- **The membership row is the refund cursor.** `refunded` and `refund_failed` are statuses on the member, and the sweep is an `ActiveJob::Continuable` job that walks members in a settled state. That is what makes a retry safe and a partial failure visible.
- **Reuse `Spree::Refunds::OrderPayments` rather than draining payments by hand.** `refund_order_payments(order:, amount:, record:, refunder:)` (`spree/core/app/workflows/spree/refunds/order_payments.rb:43-84`) already drains completed payments oldest-first through `Spree.refund_create_workflow` with a row-locked balance check, and `Spree::Refund` already carries `processing`/`completed`/`canceled` with `apply_status!` for an asynchronous gateway.
- **Eligibility refusals are 422 with a machine-readable code, not the legacy numerics.** The mini program gates joining with `516408` (not new), `516409` (SVIP only), `516410` (new users may join only) and `516411` (WeChat only). Those become one error shape with a `code` per rule, so the client stops branching on magic numbers.
- **The group-stage payment buys a coupon, and the coupon is redeemed into the delivery order.** That is what the source does: on success the member "用券下单 … 核销拼团券", and on failure the payment is refunded. Modelling it as a paid order awaiting delivery would entangle group state with fulfillment state; modelling it as a coupon reuses `6.1-coupon-wallet.md` and keeps the failure path to a refund of a purchase whose goods were never ordered.

## Design Details

### Tables

| Table | Notable columns |
| --- | --- |
| `spree_group_buy_activities` | `store_id`, `product_id`, `variant_id`, `min_team_size`, `team_window_minutes`, `starts_at`/`ends_at`, `group_price`, `status` (`draft`/`scheduled`/`active`/`ended`/`canceled`), `audience_kind`, `leader_must_be_new` |
| `spree_group_buys` | `activity_id`, `store_id`, `leader_customer_id`, a `team_size` snapshot taken when the team opens, `joined_count`, `status` (`forming`/`succeeded`/`failed`/`expired`/`canceled`), `expires_at`, `succeeded_at`, `failed_at`, `fail_reason` |
| `spree_group_buy_memberships` | `group_buy_id`, `customer_id`, `order_id`, `join_kind` (`open_team`/`share_join`/`direct_join`), `invited_by_customer_id`, `quantity`, `status` (`paid`/`confirmed`/`refunded`/`refund_failed`/`canceled`), `coupon_id` |

`team_size` is snapshotted at open time so a merchant editing the activity mid-window cannot change the deal a team is already chasing. Unique `(group_buy_id, customer_id)` stops one customer taking two places. `joined_count` is a counter cache whose truth is the memberships table, not the reverse.

### The hold

This is the part that decides whether the feature is buildable as specified.

What the source requires: when a member pays, their quantity is committed to the team, and it must still be available when the team succeeds — which could be a day later. What Spree offers (`spree/core/app/models/spree/stock_reservation.rb`, `spree/core/app/services/spree/stock_reservations/*`):

| Requirement | What the primitive does | Conflict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hold for a day | Default TTL is ten minutes (`spree/core/app/models/spree/store.rb:98`); the only validation is greater than zero, so it can be raised | Raising the store-wide default would hold every ordinary checkout far longer than intended |
| Hold for many buyers at once | `validate :exactly_one_owner` — one cart or one order (`stock_reservation.rb:19-20,129-131`), and it is destroyed with its line item | A team is neither a cart nor an order, and it outlives both |
| Hold without starving everyone else | `Stock::Quantifier#total_on_hand` subtracts **all** active reservations from variant availability (`quantifier.rb:29-38,64-92`) | A five-hundred-unit team hold would make the variant read as out of stock for ordinary shoppers for the whole window |
| Hold a backorderable or preorder variant | `Reserve` skips them (`reserve.rb:59-61`) | No hold exists at all for those variants |

So there are three honest positions, and the plan does not pretend they are equal:

- **A. A new hold primitive keyed to something other than a cart or an order.** Correct, and it means a core capability: a hold that belongs to a team, is invisible to ordinary availability except through a policy the variant opts into, and has its own expiry. This is the same kind of change asynchronous refunds were for WeChat Pay.
- **B. Accept store-wide unavailability for the window.** Cheap and wrong for a merchant running a flash sale beside a group buy, because the group hold would hide the variant from everyone else.
- **C. Do not hold; reserve at success.** The team succeeds, and only then is stock reserved, with members refunded if it cannot be. This inverts the failure the feature exists to prevent — a team that succeeds and then cannot be fulfilled is worse than one that fails.

Recommendation is A, because B damages the rest of the catalogue and C breaks the promise the customer paid for — **and it is ruled with a sequencing** (2026-09-18): `6.1-flash-sales.md` ships A's shape as a **gem-local** hold for its own ticket, and this plan generalises it into core when it lands, rather than both plans opening the same capability separately. The price of the sequencing is a migration over live pools when the hold moves; the benefit is one implementation rather than two that must afterwards be reconciled. Until then the two must not diverge, which is why flash sales' hold answers the four questions in the table above.

### The refund path

1. `GroupBuys::Expire` (or an explicit cancel) moves a `forming` team to `failed` and stamps `fail_reason`. No money moves in this transaction.
2. A `GroupBuys::RefundMembers` job picks up failed teams, iterates memberships in a `confirmed` state with `ActiveJob::Continuable`, and for each one calls `refund_order_payments` with the membership as the refund's originator.
3. Each membership moves to `refunded`, or to `refund_failed` with the gateway's error attached. A retry skips what is already refunded.
4. Because WeChat refunds settle asynchronously, a refund in `processing` is reconciled by the existing WeChat Pay sweeper rather than by this job waiting.

### Store API surface

Nineteen calls, and the family's own naming is kept in the mapping rather than in our routes. Three of the client's reads are **folded** into the resource they read (the rules, the frame and the join verdict are fields of the activity or the team), because a rewritten client needs one call per screen and those three were a second call to the same row.

| `teamBuy/addTeamBuyOrder`, `teamBuy/addTeamBuyLinkJoin` | `POST /api/v3/store/group_buy_memberships` — one create, with `join_kind` (`open_team`/`share_join`/`direct_join`) as the plan already models it. Eligibility refusals are 422 with the machine-readable code (Key Decision), on this route |

The kind is registered with `6.1-scenario-purchases.md` as `team_buy`, so the purchase and its payment legs ride that frame rather than a second one.

## Migration Path

1. **Resolve Open Question 1.** If the answer is A, the hold primitive is a core change and needs its own decision entry before any gem code exists. If B or C, record the accepted cost explicitly.
2. Open Question 2, the refund originator, because it changes a shipped model's contract.
3. The gem frame: activities and teams, with `Open`, `Join` and `Expire` and no success path, so the hold and the expiry are proven first.
4. `Succeed`: the team reaches its size, coupons are issued, memberships become `confirmed`.
5. `Fail` and the refund job, tested against a gateway that defers settlement.
6. Eligibility rules and their error codes.
7. The Store API surface as written above — the activities and categories, then the team read with its join verdict, then the membership create and cancellations — with the share and preview routes consumed rather than built; then the admin surface; then regeneration of types, OpenAPI and SDK tests.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not raise the store-wide reservation TTL to make group buying fit.** It changes ordinary checkout behaviour across the whole store.
- **Do not add a fourth `Refund#originator` type silently.** `6.0-returns-exchanges-claims.md:558` declares the set closed at three types plus nil; reopening it is a decision, not a detail.
- **Any model used as a refund originator must implement `refunded_line_amounts`**, or per-line refund attribution is silently lost (`spree/core/app/models/spree/refund.rb:94-96`).
- **Do not settle a team's money inside the transaction that fails the team.** Fail the team, then refund.
- **Do not count a team's joined members from a counter alone.** The memberships are the truth; the counter is a cache.
- **Do not try to fix the reservation location double-locking defect here.** `6.0-order-routing.md:169` already records that reservations pick a location independently of routing; a multi-day hold multiplies that defect by the team size, and it is that plan's to fix.
- **Do not let a group-stage purchase create a shipment.** It buys a coupon; the coupon is redeemed later.
- **Do not build a price calculation, a share payload or a payment leg of your own.** `teamBuy/buyCalc` is `POST /api/v3/store/price_preview`, the link and poster are `POST /api/v3/store/shares`, the two payment endpoints are the scenario plan's, and the reminders are the notifications sender's. This plan's own surface is the two resources above and nothing else.

## Open Questions

1. ~~**Is the hold a new core primitive, and if not, which cost is accepted?**~~ **Settled 2026-09-18**: option A's shape, sequenced — `6.1-flash-sales.md` ships the hold gem-local for its own ticket and this plan generalises it into core. What remains open is **when**: this plan's migration is the trigger, and until then the two implementations must not diverge.
2. **What is the refund's originator?** The membership, which means reopening the closed set of three types plus nil and implementing `refunded_line_amounts` on it; or an existing type that happens to fit and would misreport the reason. Unblocked by a decision recorded in `docs/plans/decisions.md`.
3. **Does a group buy ever span sellers?** The marketplace dimension is not researched. If a team can contain lines from two sellers, the failure path refunds across two payouts and the hold spans two stock locations, which changes both the job and the primitive.
4. **Is the group-stage price a coupon or a discounted order?** The source redeems a coupon on success. A coupon is simpler and reuses the wallet; a discounted order would need the price to be conditional on an outcome that is not known when the order is placed. Recommendation is the coupon.
5. **What happens to a member who is refunded but whose coupon was already issued?** A partially successful team — some members refunded, some fulfilled — needs a rule for whether the team counts as succeeded. Unblocked by a product decision.
