## Summary

Two programmes share a vocabulary and very little else, and the plan's first job is to say so:

- **邀请有奖** is a **site-wide programme**: the site carries the flags (`siteInvitation`, and `invitationCash` when the payout is a WeChat transfer), a visitor arrives through a share or a scene and **binds to one inviter**, and the inviter earns when that friend's **first paid order clears a threshold** (`新注册好友{transferPrizeDays}天内完成实付金额≥{_activityAmount}元的首单`, where the client's own fallback is on the **amount** — `|| 20` yuan — and the window has no default at all, `home.wxml:28-29`). The binding is **one per buyer and new users only** — the client's six refusals are explicit (`不能邀请自己哦` 513005, `您已经被邀请过啦` 513006, `未注册的新用户才能享受专属福利` 513007/513010, `您已参加其他的邀请活动` 513011, `您已经注册过啦` 513012, `transfer.js:513-531`). Rewards are money, coupons or goods depending on the operator's configuration, and every friend has a state (`已下单`/`未下单` × `已发放`/`待发放`) with a lose-by date.
- **邀请任务** is a **per-campaign mechanic**: a customer **launches** a task, N friends **assist** (`邀{assistNum}人`, new users or anyone per `taskObject`), and the prize is a **coupon or a goods at ¥0** (`兑换券` against a struck-through price). One launch and one assist per customer (148913, 148907), a window (`limitTimeEnd`), a stock counter, and an optional order-amount gate (`当前站点订单实付金额≥{_thresholdMoney}元即为助力成功`).

**And there is no tier ladder anywhere in the task side.** One task, one `assistNum` threshold, one binary outcome — the client's progress board counts avatars, not rungs. If this plan ever wants tiers it is inventing them, and says so. (The client's one ladder belongs to the **distributor** programme, `retail/retailRewardLevelDesc`, which is `6.1-distribution-and-commissions.md`'s.)

**Where a reward lives was the collision, and it is ruled** (2026-09-18): `6.1-distribution-and-commissions.md:51` has already put **both** `referral` and `invite_task` into the `kind` list of a withdrawable money ledger keyed on a `distributor_id`, while `6.1-grant-and-benefit-primitive.md:34,110` forbids an amount on the shared grant row and forbids that primitive writing a ledger row at all. Rather than two live documents both writing on `order.placed`, the boundary is drawn in Key Decisions: **invitations own the relationship, the distribution ledger owns the money**, and a coupon or goods reward is a grant.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **One gem, two sub-features, and the seam between them is stated rather than blurred** (ruled 2026-09-18). They share a binding concept, a QR, a share payload and a grant kind; they differ in every rule they enforce (one binding per buyer versus one assist per campaign; a reward after an order versus a prize after N assists; money versus an instrument). One gem because the shared pieces would otherwise be built twice; two namespaces inside it because collapsing them is how the wrong rule gets enforced in the right table.
- **The gem is `spree_referrals`** (ruled 2026-09-18), at `spree/extensions/spree_referrals/` — the architecture plan's own word for the family, and not `spree_invitations`, which would read as core's existing `Spree::Invitation`.
- **A cash reward runs through the distribution ledger, and a coupon or goods reward is a grant** (ruled 2026-09-18). Invitations own the relationship and the ledger owns the money, because that ledger is already withdrawable, settled and reconciled; this gem writes the grant and calls it. **Both headers declare the dependency** — the distribution plan's now names this one, and its `:51` already carries `referral` and `invite_task` as ledger kinds — and the ledger's uniqueness grows the invitation's own key rather than a second table appearing.
- **A binding is a row of this gem's own** (Open Question 1). It is neither a customer column (the client's rules are per-store, per-campaign and revocable, with history) nor the distribution plan's `spree_distributor_bindings` (whose payee is a distributor, and whose row is keyed on one). `Spree::CompanyInvitation` is the shape it copies — pending, accepted, revoked, bound at acceptance — with the inviter and the invitee both customers.
- **A fan binding and an assist are two tables, not one.** The client enforces different uniqueness on each (one binding per buyer, ever; one assist per customer per campaign) and both can hold for one customer at once, which a single row cannot express without a second key.
- **The reward is a grant, and the row carries no amount.** `referral_reward` moves from the primitive's built-ins to this plan's kinds — the primitive said it would (`6.1-grant-and-benefit-primitive.md:78`) — and a kind's extra semantics go in **its own side table**, per the primitive's rule. What the client renders as `sendState` 0/1/2 (待发放/已发放/发放异常) is that side table, not a column on the grant.
- **The money totals are a ledger read, and this plan does not become a ledger.** `getInvitePrizeSend` renders `paymentAmount`/`redAmount`/`couponAmount` totals; the primitive forbids holding them and this plan does not open a second money ledger — the distribution plan's constraint is explicit ("Do not write a second money ledger", `:121`). The cash case therefore **reads and writes through that ledger** (ruled 2026-09-18): the totals are its own reads, and this gem adds no sum of its own.
- **The QR is a provider capability, not a gem's own.** Nothing in this repository generates a QR code — `Spree::PrefixedId` encodes ids, no gem depends on a QR library, and the one QR endpoint in the client (`common/createPopularizeQrCode`) is declared once and called nowhere (`static/api/orderApi.js:58-62`), so no screen depends on what it does. The client needs a mini-program scene code (`qrcodeSrc`), a composed poster (`posterSrc`) and a server-authored deep-link path (`pushText` becomes `onShareAppMessage().path`), all of which need a **mini-program `access_token`** — a credential that belongs with the mini-program identity, not with payments.
- **The share payload is this plan's; the generic share metadata is not.** `6.1-store-api-miniprogram-gaps.md:72` owns the product share card ("a serializer addition driven by the product, not a new resource"); the invitation's `pushText` and scene payloads are invitation-specific and are here.
- **A referrer is an owner, not an actor.** `acted_by` answers "which staff member or key did this", its registry is admin-side, and the ruling's own constraint keeps ownership associations in the plain form (`6.0-action-actors.md:249-262`). The client compares two identities directly (513005, 148911, and 148912's refusal when the helper is a 推广员), which is a relationship, not an audit trail.
- **The three site flags are this plan's data.** `siteInvitation`, `siteInviteTask` and `invitationCash` gate both features and no plan mentions them; they are columns on the seller, following the participating-seller precedent (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17 entries)).

## Design Details

### The binding

```ruby
create_table :spree_invitation_bindings do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :seller, index: true            # the site flags live on the seller
  t.references :inviter, null: false, index: true   # a customer
  t.references :invitee, null: false, index: true   # a customer, bound at acceptance
  t.string   :source, null: false              # share_link / qr_code / scene / face_to_face
  t.references :campaign, polymorphic: true, index: true   # the programme or a task launch
  t.string   :status, null: false              # pending / bound / revoked / expired
  t.datetime :bound_at
  t.datetime :revoked_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

- **One live binding per invitee per site**, enforced by a partial unique index on `(store_id, invitee_id) where revoked_at IS NULL` — the client's 513006 and 513011 in one constraint, and the same shape the distribution plan chose for its own bindings.
- The **pending** state is what makes `updateParticipantSum` and the landing pop meaningful: the friend arrives through a scene, sees the inviter's card (`getParentInfo`), and the row is created when they log in or register — `Spree::CompanyInvitation`'s "bound at acceptance" pattern rather than a row created by a click.
- **The scene is the share's, not the row's.** Nothing here needs a `has_secure_token`: the handle the friend arrives with is the inviter's scene code, minted by the QR capability (below) and resolved by `getInfoByScene`, so the binding is written on arrival. `Spree::Invitation` and `Spree::CompanyInvitation` are the precedents for the **state shape and the expiry**, not for a token column — and if a future scene needs an opaque server-side handle rather than a mini-program scene value, that is a token column here and nothing else.
- **`spree_distributor_referral_tokens` is not this plan's table.** The distribution plan issues share codes and QR payloads with a `kind` list that already includes `invite_task` (`6.1-distribution-and-commissions.md:58`) — but its token is scoped to a **distributor**, and an invitation's inviter is a customer. The boundary is stated rather than assumed: this gem mints no token, and if an invite task's share turns out to need one, it is a distributor's token (that table) rather than a second table here.

### The two reward paths

**邀请有奖** — the inviter is owed when the friend's qualifying order is paid:

- A subscriber on the order-paid event checks the binding, the threshold and the window, then calls the primitive: `Spree::Grants.grant!(customer: inviter, kind: 'referral_reward', source: order, idempotency_key: <binding>:<order>, expires_at: lose_at, issued: <the coupon holding>)`.
- The kind's `idempotency_key_for` is the binding and the order, which is what makes a replayed event harmless — the client's own `已发放` state assumes exactly that.
- **The money variant calls the distribution ledger** (ruled 2026-09-18) rather than shipping a `StoreCredit` that ledger would never see: the grant records that the reward is owed and the ledger owns the payment, which is why both headers declare the dependency.

**邀请任务** — the prize is owed when the Nth assist lands:

```ruby
create_table :spree_invite_tasks do |t|          # a 邀请任务 campaign, the operator's
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :seller, index: true
  t.string   :title, null: false
  t.string   :audience, null: false            # taskObject: new_user / whole_user
  t.integer  :assist_num, null: false          # 邀{assistNum}人
  t.integer  :stock, null: false, default: 0   # activityStock / inviteStock
  t.decimal  :threshold_amount                 # the optional order gate, thresholdMoney
  t.string   :status, null: false              # scheduled / live / ended
  t.datetime :starts_at, null: false
  t.datetime :ends_at, null: false             # limitTimeEnd
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end

create_table :spree_invite_task_launches do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :seller, index: true
  t.references :customer, index: true          # the launcher
  t.references :task, index: true
  t.string   :status, null: false              # ing / success / fail
  t.datetime :expires_at, null: false          # limitTimeEnd
  t.integer  :assists_count, null: false, default: 0
  t.timestamps
end

create_table :spree_invite_task_assists do |t|
  t.references :launch, index: true
  t.references :customer, index: true          # the helper
  t.references :order, index: true             # set when the gate is an order amount
  t.string   :status, null: false              # pending / accepted / invalid
  t.timestamps
end

create_table :spree_invitation_rewards do |t|    # the grant kind's own side table
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :grant, index: true             # the core grant row
  t.references :binding, index: true           # the fan programme's; nil for a task prize
  t.references :launch, index: true            # the task's; nil for a fan reward
  t.string   :state, null: false               # pending / sent / failed — the client's sendState
  t.datetime :sent_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

- **One launch per customer per task** and **one assist per customer per launch**, both partial unique indexes. **The client's wording is looser than that**: 148907 and 148913 both say 其他 ("you have joined another invite/assist activity", `transfer.js:623-626,638-640`), which reads as a site-wide restriction rather than a per-campaign one — the server's semantics are not visible from the client, so the per-campaign scope is this plan's modelling and is confirmed against the first dataset (Open Question 1).
- The audience (`taskObject`: `new_user` / `whole_user`) decides who may assist, and 148912 (a 推广员 may not assist) is a rule the distribution plan's distributor model answers: an assist checks that the helper is not one. The task side raises **eight** refusals (`transfer.js:617-647`); the four this plan's rules turn on are 148907, 148911, 148912 and 148913.
- The **prize** is a grant like the referral's, with `issued:` the coupon holding or the goods' own issue step — and the state the client renders (`sendState` 0/1/2, 待发放/已发放/发放异常) lives in `spree_invitation_rewards`, the side table the primitive's rule requires rather than a column on the grant.

### Where the QR and the share payload come from

- The QR is a **mini-program scene code** minted against the account's `access_token` and rendered into a poster by the same service that composes `posterSrc`. The plan places this capability with the **mini-program identity** work — the architecture plan already routes mini-program login to `spree/providers/social_auth` (`:127`), and the one-credential-home rule (`6.0-integrations-admin.md:21`) forbids a gem carrying its own copy.
- **That placement has a credential consequence the plan states rather than discovers**: the mini-program app id and secret pair exists **today on the WeChat Pay gateway** (`SpreeWechatPay::Gateway#preferred_mini_program_app_id`/`_secret`, `gateway.rb:23-24`, read through `merchant_context.rb:43-44`) because JSAPI payment needs it. Putting the QR and the identity flow in `spree/providers/social_auth` therefore means that gem **reads the same credential** — one `Spree::Integration`, two readers — not that the pair moves and breaks payments.
- `pushText` is composed server-side because the client assigns it straight to `onShareAppMessage().path`; the target pages are fixed (`linkTypeValue.js:40,101`), so the composer is a small, testable mapping from a binding type to a path and a scene.

### Why an invite task is not a team buy

Both ask a customer to gather others, and they share nothing else. **An assist carries no money and no order**: the helper is registered at arrival (`transfer.js:574-601`), the prize is a 兑换券 or a 0-yuan goods against a struck-through price (`activity.wxml:17,28`), and the gate, when there is one, is a *threshold* the helper's own order must clear — not a payment the task collects. **A team buy is a conditional order**: it is priced through `buyCalc`, committed with `addTeamBuyOrder`, cancelled when the group fails, and refunded by a sweep. That is why `teamBuy/*` is `6.1-group-buying.md`'s and none of its 19 endpoints are named here.

### What the plan does not build

No money ledger; no second coupon holder (the coupon wallet's); no tier ladder (neither programme here has one); no second share-metadata resource (the gaps plan's); no QR renderer of its own; and no token table (the scene is the share's, above).

## Migration Path

1. **The gem and its three-place wiring** — `spree/extensions/spree_referrals/`, `server/Gemfile`, `.github/workflows/tests.yml` and the gem's own Gemfile — which `6.1-grant-and-benefit-primitive.md:101` performs **once for the whole family**, and which this plan therefore inherits rather than repeats.
2. **The reward boundary first** (ruled 2026-09-18): this gem writes a **grant** for every reward, and the **cash** case additionally calls the distribution ledger — both headers now declare it, and the ledger's uniqueness grows the invitation's key before anything else lands.
3. **The binding and the programme read**, with the partial unique index and the seller flags.
4. **The coupon and goods rewards** through the grant primitive, which lands before any consumer, with `spree_invitation_rewards` as the kind's own state.
5. **The QR and share capability in `spree/providers/social_auth`** (ruled 2026-09-18) — a later addition to a shipped plan, since `6.0-social-login.md:150` declares the mini-program flow out of scope for its first release — and the share payload that follows.
6. **The task side**: campaigns, launches, assists, the audience rules and the prize.
7. **The order-side participant counter**, which is small and only matters once an order exists.
8. Regenerate types, Zod, OpenAPI and SDK tests.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not write a money ledger, and do not sum one.** The distribution plan's constraint stands; the cash reward **calls** that ledger (ruled 2026-09-18), and this gem writes no ledger row and holds no total of its own.
- **Do not put an amount on a grant row**, and put the kind's own state in its own side table.
- **Do not merge the binding and the assist**, and do not relax either uniqueness rule.
- **Do not build a tier ladder.** Neither programme here has one — one `assistNum` threshold, a binary outcome and a progress board that counts avatars (`invite-task/record/record.wxml:37-49`). The ladder the client does carry is the **distributor** programme's (`retail/retailRewardLevelDesc`, `spree_distributor_levels`), which is `6.1-distribution-and-commissions.md`'s and never this plan's.
- **Do not render a QR here.** It is a mini-program capability with a credential that belongs with the mini-program identity.
- **Do not claim the generic share metadata**, and do not build a second share resource.
- **Do not use `acted_by` for a referrer.** It is an owner relationship, and the constraint keeps those in the plain form.
- **Do not let the excluded `threeOrder/*` feature become an owner** of this plan's endpoints just because it reads them: it consumes the invitation data and links into both invite pages, and if its exclusion is confirmed that stays true.
- **Do not forget the two dead spots**: `invitation/getSpecialId` has no caller, and the sensors tracker's `/pagesC/invite-task/detail/detail` does not exist.

## Open Questions

Three of this plan's four questions were settled on 2026-09-18, and `fork-decisions.md` carries them because two reach documents that already existed:

- **A cash reward runs through the distribution ledger; a coupon or goods reward is a grant.** Invitations own the relationship and the ledger owns the money — it is already withdrawable, settled and reconciled — so this gem writes a grant and calls it. Both headers declare the dependency, and the ledger's uniqueness grows the invitation's own key.
- **The mini-program QR capability goes to `spree/providers/social_auth`**, where the mini-program identity already lives and where a second credential home is forbidden.
- **One gem, two namespaces** — `spree_referrals` — because the binding, the QR, the share payload and the grant kind are shared while every rule the two programmes enforce differs.

One remains: **is the binding its own table or a reuse?** This draft writes its own, modelled on `Spree::CompanyInvitation`'s pending/accepted shape; a customer column is refused (the client's rules are per-store, per-campaign and revocable) and the distribution plan's bindings are refused (wrong payee), so what is left is confirming the shape against the first dataset.
