## Summary

A seckill in this client is **a window and a pool on one activity, bought through a ticket**. The activity carries `detailStart`/`detailEnd`, `now`, `status ∈ {ing, going}` — an ended activity is simply absent from the lists rather than rendered in a third state — and three pool figures at once — `limitStockAllQuantity`, `limitStockDayQuantity` and `limitStockDetailQuantity` (per activity, per day, per time slot) — with `limitStockDetailMax`/`Used` and a `percentage` for the 已抢光 bar. A **time slot** is a sub-record (`timeDetailId`) and the reminder is keyed to `(promotionId, timeDetailId)`.

The part that decides the design is what happens between tapping 立即抢购 and paying, and the client states it outright:

- The confirm button does **not** add to cart — it claims a **ticket** (`promotion/addTicket`, `components/j-format/j-format.js:855,956-980`).
- A ticket is one live qualification per customer for a fixed quantity: claiming another invalidates the first (`重新抢购原抢购资格将立即失效`, `seckill-ticket.wxml:8-12`).
- **It holds pool units while unpaid**, and the client says so in the pool's own units: `前面还有 {{standbyTicket}} 人未支付` (`:20-25`).
- It expires in minutes — the settle page renders `ticket.endDate` as 仅剩 mm:ss 订单就失效了 (`pagesF/settlement/settlement.js:318-324`), and the unpaid-ticket list repeats 请在5分钟内完成支付 (`components/order/order-ing/order-ing.js:98` — the only literal five-minute string in the client).

So a ticket is **a stock hold whose owner is not a cart and not an order** — which is precisely the shape `6.1-group-buying.md:44-47` audited and found the shipped primitive cannot express: `Spree::StockReservation` requires exactly one cart or order owner, dies with its line item, and — the fatal one — **subtracts from the variant's global availability**, so a held pool would read as the goods being out of stock for ordinary shoppers.

The pool the bundles plan handed over is the same mechanism seen from the other side: it found that a combo carries `limitStockAllQuantity`/`limitStockDayQuantity`, that the client renders 活动库存不足 with its own message distinct from the per-customer caps, and it handed them to the time-boxed pricing work rather than to composition, naming this plan as their owner (`6.1-product-bundles.md:28,149,158`). This plan is that work, and it inherits the pool, the caps and a combo-shaped workload with them.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **A ticket is the hold, and the hold is this gem's own for now** (ruled 2026-09-18, **against this draft's recommendation**). Neither `Spree::StockReservation` nor anything else in core can express "N units of an activity's pool, held by a record that is not a cart or an order, invisible to ordinary availability, released on expiry or re-issue" — `6.1-group-buying.md` found the same gap for a multi-day team hold. This draft proposed building the capability in core here, on the argument that flash sales is Wave 2 and group buying Wave 3, so the second consumer would inherit rather than re-open. **The ruling is the other way: this gem ships its own hold, and group buying generalises it later.** The price is stated rather than discovered — generalising it means a migration over live pools, and until then **the two implementations must not diverge**: the gem's hold answers the four questions group buying's audit named (owner, TTL, visibility, backorderable) so it can be lifted rather than rewritten. Three of them are answered in Design Details; the fourth is answered by what the hold is *not* — a pool-scoped hold owns no `StockLevel` and never consults `Stock::Quantifier`, so the backorderable question group buying's audit raised (whether a hold on a backorderable variant is refused, `reserve.rb:59-61`) does not arise here, and the gem says so rather than leaving the fourth answer to be inferred.
- **The pool is not stock.** It is an activity-scoped counter with its own reserve/release lifecycle, and it never touches `Stock::Quantifier`'s arithmetic. The goods' own stock (`specStock`, `shopStock`) stays the seller's, and the client checks the two in order and shows two different messages (`limit.js:256-258` 商品库存不足 before `:274-277` 活动库存不足) — a distinction the plan keeps rather than collapsing.
- **The three pool figures are three scopes of one counter**: all-time, per-day, per-time-slot. The client intersects all three (`limit.js:118` for `type === "seckill"` adds `limitStockDetailQuantity` to the day and all-time minimum) and so does the gem, on write.
- **The price is a discount row, not a promotion** (ruled 2026-09-18). The promotion engine reconciles its own rows destructively and winner-only per competition group (`adjusters/promotion.rb:50-59,248-250`), and the client's own rule is that a seckill refuses points and coupons per activity anyway (`seckillIntegralFlag`, `seckillCouponFlag`, `order-discount.wxml:35,38-41`). The bundles plan's ruling is the precedent — a gem-owned `manual`-kind discount, outside the engine — with one difference this plan must state: a seckill **does** display a struck-through price, so the read serves `price` and `nowPrice` for the wire while the write applies the discount.
- **One live ticket per customer per activity**, and re-issuing replaces it (`res: "exist"`, `seckill-ticket.wxml:8-12`). The replacement is what keeps the pool honest: the old hold must be released *in the same transaction* that grants the new one, or a customer can hold pool units for free by re-claiming.
- **The countdown has one source of truth, and the client already disagrees with itself about it.** The PDP, the home zone, the settle page and the order list tick against the server's `now`/`serverNowDate`; the seckill list and the special page tick against the device clock (`seckill-list.js:150,157`, `special.js:1369`). The plan returns absolute end instants plus `server_now`, and says the two device-clock surfaces are a client fix rather than a server contract.
- **Seller scope is read, not resolved.** Every read carries `siteId` like every other request; the plan consumes the request context and never resolves a seller itself.
- **The queue is this plan's, and the inventory's "~4" means the four `promotion/*` endpoints** (ruled 2026-09-19). `promotion/addTicket`, `promotion/getTicket` and the reminder pair are owned here rather than left unclaimed: without a claim path the pool is a counter nobody draws from, and the alternative reading — the three `goods/*` reads plus the home zone — leaves the only mechanism that makes the pool work with no owner at all.
- **The pool never overrides the shelf** (ruled 2026-09-19). Effective availability is the smaller of the pool's remaining units and the goods' own stock, so a seckill sells neither what the shelf does not hold nor more than the activity promised. This is the client's own check order (stock, then pool, then the caps) enforced where the write happens, and it means an activity can read "已抢光" with pool left — the pool was right, the shelf was empty.
- **The settlement discount rides the price preview rather than opening a second calculation** (ruled 2026-09-19; built 2026-09-20). The claim writes nothing but the ticket; the preview prices the line from the activity and the write applies the difference as a `manual`-kind discount, and the activity's 积分/优惠券 refusals travel with that answer. When the preview did not exist yet this plan stopped at the claim rather than pricing on its own — the seam it waited for is `Spree.price_preview_sources`, which is now what it registers against.
- **The activity refuses points and coupons per activity, not globally.** `seckillIntegralFlag`/`seckillCouponFlag` are per-activity flags the preview answers, and the settle page renders 秒杀活动不支持 / 活动不支持 when they are off — so this is a property of the activity, not a second discount engine.

## Design Details

### The activity

```ruby
create_table :spree_flash_sales do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :seller, index: true
  t.string   :title, null: false
  t.string   :code, null: false               # seckill / discount (the client's two card types)
  t.string   :status, null: false             # scheduled / live / ended
  t.datetime :starts_at, null: false
  t.datetime :ends_at, null: false
  t.integer  :pool_all, null: false, default: 0     # limitStockAllQuantity
  t.integer  :pool_per_day, null: false, default: 0 # limitStockDayQuantity
  t.integer  :pool_per_slot, null: false, default: 0# limitStockDetailQuantity
  t.integer  :purchase_cap_all                     # limitAllQuantity
  t.integer  :purchase_cap_day                     # limitDayQuantity
  t.string   :audience                             # seckillLimitUserType, e.g. channel_user
  t.boolean  :allows_points, default: false, null: false   # seckillIntegralFlag
  t.boolean  :allows_coupons, default: false, null: false  # seckillCouponFlag
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

```ruby
create_table :spree_flash_sale_slots do |t|       # a timeDetailId
  t.references :flash_sale, index: true
  t.datetime :starts_at, null: false
  t.datetime :ends_at, null: false
  t.integer  :pool, null: false, default: 0
  t.integer  :purchase_cap                        # limitPeriodQuantity
  t.integer  :position, null: false, default: 0
end

create_table :spree_flash_sale_items do |t|       # which offer, at what price, with what pool
  t.references :flash_sale, index: true
  t.references :variant, index: true
  t.decimal  :sale_amount, null: false, default: 0
  t.integer  :pool, null: false, default: 0       # this item's share, when it has one
  t.integer  :position, null: false, default: 0
end
```

- `Spree::FlashSale` inherits `Spree.base_class`, includes `Spree::SingleStoreResource` and `Spree::Metadata`, uses `has_status` and `acts_as_paranoid`, and publishes lifecycle events. Slots and items are its children.
- **`code` is the client's two card types**: `seckill` (its own PDP and popup) and `discount` (the ordinary PDP with the activity badge), which one card component renders differently (`seckill-area.js:61-64`). They are one activity with two presentations, not two models.
- The **purchase caps are not the pool**: the client renders them with different messages and checks the pool first (`limit.js:274` before `:278`). They are per-customer and belong on the activity and the slot.

### The ticket and the hold

```ruby
create_table :spree_flash_sale_tickets do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :flash_sale, index: true
  t.references :flash_sale_slot, index: true
  t.references :variant, index: true
  t.references :customer, index: true
  t.integer  :quantity, null: false
  t.string   :status, null: false             # holding / settled / expired / replaced / canceled
  t.datetime :expires_at, null: false         # ticket.endDate
  t.string   :hold_id                         # this gem's hold record, below
  t.timestamps
end
```

- **A ticket owns this gem's own hold** (ruled 2026-09-18): a table and a service inside `spree_flash_sales`, so that generalising it into core later is a lift rather than a rewrite. The hold is a reserved quantity against an activity-scoped pool, owned by a record that is not a cart or an order, invisible to `Stock::Quantifier`, released by expiry, cancellation or replacement. Its interface is deliberately the smallest thing that serves both this plan and group buying: `reserve!(owner:, pool:, quantity:)`, `release!(hold)`, `sweep_expired!` — and **it does not know what a flash sale is**.
- Uniqueness: one live ticket per `(customer, flash_sale)` with a partial index on the holding status, so the replacement rule is enforced by the database rather than by the service. `oid` on the re-claim names the ticket being replaced, so the release and the grant are one transaction.
- **The expiry is what releases the pool**, and it is read as a date fact — but the pool counter is a *write* target, so unlike the points ledger there is something to settle: a sweep job releases lapsed holds and decrements the counter, and the plan says plainly that a sweep that does not run delays the release rather than corrupting the count, because every release is idempotent on the hold's own id.

### Price at claim, price at settlement

The client claims a **quantity** at ticket time and is priced at settlement (`calc/buyCalc` with `promotionCode: "seckill"` and `frozenQuantity`). So:

- The ticket reserves units and **not a price** — a price frozen at claim time would let a customer hold yesterday's price through a repricing.
- The settlement preview prices the line from the activity, and the write applies the difference as a **`manual`-kind discount** on the line, per the Key Decision. The preview also answers the price's absolute end instant (`price_ends_at` — the open stretch's close, or the activity's own end when it is sold throughout), which is the instant the countdown ticks against, per the ruling that the countdown has one source of truth. When the activity ends between claim and payment, the settle page's `511010` / `511013` are the contract: the payment is refused and the customer is sent back to the list (`settlement.js:294,296-305,918-930`).
- **The discount row names the activity**, the way the bundles plan marks its own rows (`6.1-product-bundles.md:35`): core reads `manual` as the merchant-entered kind, so a row that does not say where it came from reads as a compensation. The row carries the activity and the ticket, which is also what makes a seckill line's refund recomputable. **Where the money shows, found while building it:** a cart sums every discount into `adjustment_total` and the customer's total, but its own `discount_total` column — and the line's — counts `promotion` rows only (`workflows/spree/carts/recalculate_totals.rb:82,195`). So a seckill's saving is real in what is charged and invisible in that one column; what the line *displays* as its price comes from the preview, which quotes the activity. A report that reads `discount_total` to find seckill sales will find nothing, and must read the discount rows instead.

### Reads

- The PDP read answers the activity with its window, its status, the pool figures, the price pair and the progress percentage. `status` is derived from the server clock, never from the client's.
- The list read answers `{ going, ing }` groups by category, sorted — the grouping is a presentation contract of the kind `fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “The ordering menu is a page composition”) already ruled on for the menu, so the API answers the flat collection and the client groups.
- The home zone answers one activity; it is the same read as the list's, narrowed to the featured one.

## Migration Path

Steps 1–8 are built (2026-09-20). Steps 5 and 7 landed on the price preview, which `6.1-store-api-miniprogram-gaps.md` owns, rather than as a second price calculation.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not route the pool through `Spree::StockReservation`.** It subtracts from the variant's global availability and would make a held pool read as the goods being out of stock.
- **Do not raise the reservation TTL, and do not make the pool a reservation.** The hold is its own capability.
- **Do not let a second consumer write its own hold.** Group buying generalises this gem's into core when it arrives (ruled 2026-09-18); until then the two must not diverge, which is why the gem's hold answers the four questions the lift depends on.
- **Do not express the pool as a promotion action.** The engine reconciles destructively and winner-only.
- **Do not freeze a price at claim time.** The ticket holds units; the price is computed where the client computes it.
- **Do not let a replacement leave the old hold live for even an instant** — one transaction, or the pool leaks.
- **Do not write a line without both checks**: the pool's remaining units and the shelf's own stock both have to allow the write (Key Decisions, 2026-09-19).
- **Do not collapse the pool and the purchase caps**, and do not collapse the pool and the goods' own stock: the client checks stock, then pool, then caps and gives each its own message (`limit.js:256-258,274-277,278-285`).
- **Do not let the client's clock decide a status.** Absolute instants plus `server_now`; the two device-clock surfaces are a client fix.
- **Do not let the home zone's CMS filing decide ownership.** It is the same activity read from a second surface, and claiming it is recorded rather than assumed.
- **Do not build a second notification path.** The 开售提醒 is delivered by `6.1-notifications.md`.
- **Do not resolve the seller here.**
