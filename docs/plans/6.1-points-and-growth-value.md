## Ownership rulings (2026-09-18)

Five boundaries were settled together with `6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md`, after an audit found the split between the two leaking in eight places. `fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-18) carries the full entry and the reasoning.

- **The grant/benefit primitive's row is core and `spree_grants` owns its kinds and its service** (ruled 2026-09-18; `6.1-grant-and-benefit-primitive.md`). A points lot becomes a kind of it, with `remaining` and the allocations staying in this plan's own side table — the primitive does not carry an amount. **The ledger service remains this plan's seam**, not the primitive's: the reviews plan's dependency waits on its interface — **which this plan specifies below** (`Spree::Points::Ledger`).
- **`vip/getUserVipInfo` is not this plan's.** The membership endpoint serves that payload whole, because the client fetches it once and the tier is its core.
- **`delIntegralOrder` is `6.1-scenario-purchases.md`'s**, as a `destroy` on the scenario-order resource: deletion is a property of that resource, not of one kind. This plan's sentence that the scenario plan "already names them" was false — it names create, list, show and cancel — and the citation is corrected in place.
- **The `vip_card` a redemption issues is opened by the membership plan**, through its non-purchase source. This gem records the payload; it issues no card, per its own constraint.
- **`god/getWineValueDetailPage` stays excluded** with the rest of `god/*`, and **the payment legs** (`integral-mall/payByWxMp`, `vip/payByWxMp`) belong to the scenario plan like every other scenario's.

## Summary

Two balances, one ledger, and two halves: how points arrive, and how they leave.
积分 (points) are earned on an order and spent in a shop; 成长值 (growth value) is
earned on an order and never spent, because it is what moves a customer up the
membership ladder. What the client proves is narrower than "they are one design":
both balances are read as signed changes and rendered with the same `+` rule, but
**only the points ledger is month-grouped** — the growth-value page is a flat list,
with its own response shape (`pageDto.records`, not `integralDetails.records[].changeInfo[]`)
and its own client-side label dictionary (`grow.js:14-26`; `grow.wxml:19-28` is the
flat list that consumes it) — so the shared ground is one ledger with two balances, not two
reads that look alike. This plan builds the ledger once, with a `kind`, on that
narrower ground.

Two corrections came out of reading the client rather than the domain heading,
and each moves an endpoint between plans.

**`vip/getUserLevelRecord` is not "tier change history". It is the growth value
ledger.** Its page is titled 成长值记录 (`pagesB/member/grow/grow.wxml:5`), it
renders 当前成长值 from `experience` (`:7`), and it carries the ladder arithmetic
— `levelExperience` more, "可升级至{{nextLevel}}" (`:12`). The membership plan
filed it as an append-only record of tier changes over `CustomerGroupUser`. It
belongs here.

**`god/getWineValueDetailPage` is not the growth value ledger either.** It is
酒神值, the excluded 酒神 program's own counter. An earlier reading moved it here
believing it was 成长值; it goes back with the rest of `god/*`.

Two facts decide the ledger's shape. **Points expire and growth value does not** —
the first half is the client's own display (`mall.wxml:24-27`), the second is this
plan's ruling and no client screen states or denies it —
and the client shows exactly that — the balance arrives with `willExpireIntegral`
and `willExpireDate` (`pagesD/integral/mall/mall.js:86-88`). Expiry is already a
date fact rather than a stored transition in this codebase, so the gift card's
pattern carries over instead of a job that flips rows. And **points are
account-wide while the shop is seller-scoped**. The shelf is visibly scoped —
`getSvipIntegralGoods` names a seller in its own payload
(`pagesB/member/buy/buy.js:229-233`) while `integral/get` names none — but that
absence settles nothing by itself, because `siteId` rides the headers of **every**
request (`static/config/api.js:40-49`). The scope of the **balance** rests on the
ruling, not on the payload: a membership and a points balance belong to the
customer and span every seller (`docs/plans/`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “A membership and a points balance belong to the customer”)).

## Design Details

### The plan is two halves: earning and spending

Everything below answers one of two questions, and the client puts both on one
screen: the 赚积分 block (`pagesD/integral/mall/mall.wxml:37-77`) and the shop
underneath it.

**获取 — how points arrive.** Three inputs, all measured on the order the customer
actually paid, plus two multipliers this plan consumes and does not own.

| Input | Client evidence | Where this plan puts it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 消费积分 — 每消费 N 元得 1 积分 | `integralRatio` (`mall.wxml:48`) | A store preference |
| 商品积分 — a per-line value with its own cap | `item.integral`, "已达商品所得积分上限" (`supported-goods.wxml:8-10`) | A per-line input beside the ratio |
| 满 N 元门槛 — below it nothing is earned | `shoppingIntegralThreshold` (`not-supported-goods.wxml:9`) | A preference beside the ratio — and the client receives it on the **seller-scoped** price preview (`settlement.js:433`), not from the parameterless ratio read, so a per-seller override is expressible (Open Question 2) |
| 生日双倍 · 会员日双倍 | the rights `birthday_double_integral`, `svip_date` | **Consumed from the membership plan**, never registered here |

评价积分 is the client's fourth earning channel and is deliberately not in that
table: it is earned for writing a review rather than for paying, so the review
extension owns it and credits it through this plan's ledger service (see *Earning
runs on events*). 任务积分 is the fifth and stays excluded with gamification — its
control reads 敬请期待 and links nowhere (`pagesD/integral/mall/mall.wxml:73-76`).

**消费 — how points leave.** Two ways, each with its own section below.

| Way | What it is | Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 抵扣 | An opt-in discount on an ordinary order | *Spending points on an ordinary order is a discount…* |
| 兑换 | The mall: points buy a catalogue row that issues something | *The catalogue row declares what a redemption issues* |

Both debit at creation, and that is ruled rather than assumed (2026-09-17), so the
two differ in what can go wrong afterwards rather than in when the points leave.
Undoing either is a reversal ledger row, never an edit — 退回 on a refund, and the
`after_cancel` hook when an order is cancelled.

### Points are not a store credit — checked, not assumed

`Spree::StoreCredit` is the nearest thing core has, and it is money by
construction. It requires a currency (`validates :currency, presence: true`,
`store_credit.rb:33`), it is a payment source (`has_many :payments, as: :source`,
`:30`), and every value in `Spree::StoreCreditEvent`'s vocabulary is a step in a
payment's life — `void`, `cancel`, `credit`, `capture`, `eligible`, `authorize`,
`allocation` (`store_credit_event.rb:24-37`).

The plan that owns stored value draws the line itself: a gift card or a store
credit is "prepaid money the store owes, a balance on the liability side of the
books" (`6.0-6.1-loyalty-gift-cards-store-credits.md:12`). Points sit on no side
of the books. They are a marketing instrument: an integer, with no currency,
spendable only where the operator permits, and expiring.

Three consequences follow, and they are why this is a new model rather than a
decorated `StoreCredit`: an integer balance, no `currency` column, and lots that
carry expiry dates.

`Spree::Price` is closed for the same reason — `validates :currency, presence:
true` (`price.rb:53`) — so a points price cannot be a price row. It gets its own.

**And a points ledger is not a custom field.** The repo already uses a custom
field named `loyalty_points` as its worked example
(`spree/core/spec/models/spree/exports/customers_spec.rb:38`), which is the right
home for a number a merchant types by hand. A ledger is system data with
invariants and it needs real columns and real indexes.

### One ledger, two balances

What the client proves is that the two balances come from **one ledger**: both reads
carry a signed change and render it with the same `+` rule, while their grouping,
their field names and even where their labels come from all differ — the points list
reads the server's `integralExplain`, the growth-value list maps its own dictionary
(`grow.js:14-26`):

```js
// points — pagesD/integral/details/details.js:47
e.showIntegral = e.integral > 0 ? "+".concat(e.integral) : e.integral

// growth value — pagesB/member/grow/grow.wxml:27
{{(item.changeRecord>0) ? '+'+item.changeRecord : item.changeRecord}}
```

So there is one `Spree::PointAccount` per `(store, customer, kind)` and one
`spree_point_transactions` ledger, with `kind` naming which balance a row moves.
Two parallel pairs of tables would be the same code twice with different column
names.

The third kind is already visible and is deliberately left unbuilt. 酒神值 is the
same **idea** again — a dated, signed change against a running total — but **not the
same rendering**: it prints no `+` prefix and carries a separate `isPlus` flag
(`pagesB/god-user/detail/detail.js:43-44`). The 酒神 program is excluded
gamification, so the schema must not be closed to it and nothing registers it.

| Table | Columns that matter |
| --- | --- |
| `spree_point_accounts` | `store_id`, `customer_id`, `kind` (`points`/`growth_value`), `lifetime_earned` — unique `(store_id, customer_id, kind)`; the row a spend locks. **No `balance` column**: the balance is derived (below) |
| `spree_point_grants` | The kind's **side table beside the core `spree_grants` row**: `grant_id` (the primitive row), `account_id`, `amount`, `remaining`, `reason`. `expires_at`, `source` and `granted_at` belong to the primitive row and are not repeated here |
| `spree_point_transactions` | `account_id`, `delta`, `balance_after`, `kind`, `reason`, polymorphic `source`, `occurred_at` — the append-only ledger the client reads. `balance_after` is the **snapshot taken at write time** (the `Spree::StoreCreditEvent` pattern), not a claim about the current balance |
| `spree_point_allocations` | `transaction_id`, `grant_id`, `amount` — which lots a debit drew from: the audit trail and the basis of a reversal, not the reason `remaining` is knowable |
| `spree_point_products` | `store_id`, `seller_id` (null means store-wide), `name`, `image`, `jxe_type`, `points`, `money`, `position`, `featured`, `stock`, plus the issued-thing keys |

### Expiry is a date fact on the lot, never a stored transition

`Spree::GiftCard` already solved this and the pattern transfers unchanged:

| Gift card | Points |
| --- | --- |
| `def expired?; !redeemed? && expires_at.present? && expires_at <= Date.current; end` (`gift_card.rb:151-153`) | `def expired?; expires_at.present? && expires_at <= Date.current; end`, on a grant |
| `scope :expired, -> { where(status: :active).where(expires_at: ..Date.current) }` (`:57`) | `scope :expired, -> { where(expires_at: ..Date.current) }` |
| `scope :active, … where(expires_at: [nil, Date.tomorrow..])` (`:56`) | `scope :usable`, the same predicate |
| `DISPLAY_STATUSES = (statuses + %w[expired])`, "a date fact rather than a stored transition" (`:23-25`) | expiry is never a `status` value at all |

**No job flips anything.** A grant is expired the moment its date passes, exactly
as a card is. The two reads the client makes are then plain queries over the
grants table:

- `integralTotal` — the sum of `remaining` over usable grants
- `willExpireIntegral` + `willExpireDate` — the sum of `remaining`, and the
  soonest `expires_at`, over grants expiring inside the warning window

This is the same discipline the repo already applies and it avoids the failure
mode where a scheduled job does not run and points silently never expire.

### The account row is the lock, and the balance is derived

A spend must not race another spend. `spree_point_accounts` is one row per
`(store, customer, kind)` and a spend locks it, reads the balance, allocates
against grants and writes the ledger inside that transaction.

The row carries `lifetime_earned` rather than deriving it on every read, because
it has to exist for the lock anyway. `lifetime_earned` is
populated for **both** kinds — this plan's own answer rather than a `fork-decisions.md`
ruling (audited 2026-09-18) — even though only the points
screen reads a total today; the growth value read exposes `experience` alone.
**The account row keeps the lock and `lifetime_earned`, not the balance: the
balance is the sum of usable grants' `remaining`** (ruled 2026-09-18). The
earlier draft stored it and claimed a checkable invariant — "it equals the sum of
usable grants' `remaining`" — which cannot hold: expiry is a date fact and nothing
flips it (`:202-222`), so between an expiry and the next writer the stored total
and the truth diverge, and the spend path would either pay with expired points or
fail to allocate. Deriving the figure removes the class of defect the invariant
was guarding against, rather than adding a task that reports it. It is also what
the wire already needs: `integralTotal` and `willExpireIntegral` are sums over
usable grants (`:217-219`), so this is one computation done once and cached, not
a second one. Only the ledger service writes a grant's `remaining`, never a
caller.

### Soonest-expiry-first, not oldest-first

`Spree::StoreCredit` spends the oldest credit first and says so
(`store_credit.rb:41-42`). That is right for money, which does not expire. With
lots that do, oldest-first is only correct when every grant carries the same
validity period — and it is not, because a promotional grant and an earn grant
can have different windows.

So allocation is **soonest-expiry-first**: the grant closest to expiring is spent
first. Every debit records which grants it drew from, in
`spree_point_allocations`. `remaining` is the lot's own stored figure and the
ledger service is its only writer; the allocation rows are the audit trail and
the basis of a reversal, not the reason `remaining` is knowable.

**Every ledger row records the seller and the order it came from** (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “A membership and a points balance belong to the customer”), the entry's Consequences paragraph) — two columns, not a derivation, because `source` also points at reviews and at manual adjustments, neither of which has a seller.

**The deduction is capped by the line it lands on.** `spree_discounts` validates
`amount <= 0` and nothing else, so a `loyalty` row larger than the line's net
would push it negative; the spend path clamps `integralMinus` to that line's own
net before the row is written.

**The admin cannot delete a `loyalty` row, and today it would.** The order screen
renders a discount's kind as one of two — `promotion` or `manual` — and offers a
delete on everything that is not a promotion
(`packages/dashboard/src/components/spree/orders/order-adjustments-cards.tsx:270,280`),
so a `loyalty` row would appear as "Manual" and be deletable, and deleting it
would remove the row with no points returned. Two changes, in this order: the
server refuses to destroy a `loyalty` row directly — the reversal is a ledger
row, per the rule above — and the dashboard learns the third kind and hides the
affordance.

**The ledger service has a name and a shape**, because another plan calls it:
`Spree::Points::Ledger.credit!(account:, amount:, reason:, source:, idempotency_key:)`,
`.debit!(account:, amount:, reason:, source:)` and `.balance(account)`. `credit!`
is a no-op when the key is taken, which is the check `6.1-product-reviews.md:283-286`
says it needs before a review's reward is credited.

### The catalogue row declares what a redemption issues

A good carries its own `id`, `img`, `sort`, `integral` and `money`, but the name
the client shows comes from the thing being issued rather than from the good — a
coupon good reads `coupon.name`, a card good reads `vipCard.name`
(`pagesD/integral/mall/mall.js:183-198`). So the row is a declaration of *what
gets issued, and for how many points*, and its own presentation is secondary.

`jxeType` is the discriminator, and the repo already has the shape for one:
concrete foreign keys with an exactly-one-of validation, and **no polymorphic
source** — `Spree::CommissionLine` states the rule (`commission_line.rb:9`) and
carries the validation (`:56,79-83`). So the row holds the coupon definition for
`coupon`, `customer_group_id` for `vip_card`, and `variant_id` for a shippable
good, with exactly one of them present.

`points` is a plain integer column, because `Spree::Price` cannot hold it
(`price.rb:53` requires a currency).

### A redemption issues something another plan owns

A good in the shop carries `jxeType`, and the client branches on it when
building the row it displays (`pagesD/integral/mall/mall.js:168-198`):

| `jxeType` | The payload the good carries | Who issues it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `coupon` | `coupon: { name, discountType, discountMinus, limitAmount, limitAmountMin, instruction }` | the coupon plan |
| `vip_card` | `vipCard: { name, length, rightsCount, siteCover }` | the membership plan |
| (a shippable good, implied by `noStock`) | — | an ordinary order |

So this plan owns the catalogue row and the price; **it does not own the
issuance.** The scenario plan already frames this correctly — a kind "declares
its price, its issue step and its eligibility" — and the issue step for a points
redemption is registered by whichever plan owns the thing being issued.

The good also carries its own eligibility rules, which are conditions on the
customer rather than on the price: `limitUserType` (`vip_user`, `we_chat_user`),
`subscribeSiteWeChat` with `weChatQrCode`, and `siteEqual` — the good is offered
only where the customer's site matches.

### The debit is at creation, on both paths, and later states only reverse it

`addIntegralMallOrder` is one call that returns `convertType`, and the client
branches on the **answer** rather than making a second request: when it reads
`integral` there is no payment call at all — the redemption goes straight to the
result page with **no payment request at all**
(`pagesD/integral/goods/goods.js:226,262-267,272`; the `paySuccess` literal at `:253`
is set before the branch and is not evidence) — and only the points-plus-money
branch reaches `payByWxMp` (`:272`). A redemption that completes with no payment
step can only have been settled by the create call, so **points are spent at
creation in both branches**, and the window in which one purchase could spend the
same points twice does not exist. No hold is needed.

That turns the mirror image into a requirement: an order the scenario plan moves
to `canceled` or `expired` (`6.1-scenario-purchases.md:35,52`) has to return both
balances as a reversal row — the `consume_return` reason the client already names.

Deletion is not that reversal. `delIntegralOrder`'s control is offered in **every**
order state: it sits outside the state blocks, so it shows even on an order
already rendering 已取消 (`pagesD/integral/order/details/details.wxml:25,34`), its
confirm reads "订单删除后将不可恢复, 确定删除？" (`:87`), and the detail read answers
"该订单已删除" for any id it cannot find (`details.js:51`). An action offered on a
cancelled order is housekeeping, and it must not refund — a customer who redeemed
a coupon and then deleted the order would keep the coupon and take the points
back.

**The deduction is debited at creation too, by one ruling rather than two** (ruled
2026-09-17). There the creating call is `order/buySubmit` or `order/cartSubmit` —
the request that persists the `loyalty` row — so the points leave the balance in
the same act that writes the row.

The two paths then part on what can go wrong. A mall order that is never paid has
a state to sit in (`6.1-scenario-purchases.md:35,52`); an ordinary order does not.
`Spree::Order::STATUSES` is `draft`, `placed`, `canceled`
(`spree/core/app/models/spree/order.rb:130`), with no outcome for an abandoned
purchase, and core runs no job that closes one. **So an order submitted with a
points discount and then never paid holds those points until something cancels
it** — that is the cost of this ruling, stated rather than discovered later.

The cancellation is where the reversal goes, and the seam already exists:
`Spree::Orders::Cancel` declares `hooks :before_cancel, :after_cancel`
(`spree/core/app/workflows/spree/orders/cancel.rb:11`) and runs `after_cancel`
**inside** the transaction that stamps the status (`:55-59`). Returning the points
belongs in that hook, not in a subscriber on `order.canceled` (`:66`): the same
transaction that cancels the order must not be able to leave the balance spent if
it rolls back. The `loyalty` row stays on the order — it records what the order
carried, and the reversal is the ledger row.

### Spending points on an ordinary order is a discount, and it needs a core kind

The mall is not the only way points leave the balance. The settlement page
carries a second mechanism: an opt-in toggle that reduces an ordinary order's
total. Both are in scope (ruled 2026-09-17 — neither is built yet), and they are different mechanisms
rather than two faces of one.

| | The deduction | The mall |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Endpoints | none — the boolean `useIntegral` rides `calc/buyCalc`, `calc/cartCalc`, `order/buySubmit`, `order/cartSubmit` | 9, its own domain |
| What it writes | `Spree::Discount`, `kind: 'loyalty'` | a standalone scenario order |
| Surface | one row in the ordinary `minusList`, beside `coupon` and `first_order` | five pages, its own order list |

The client sends the boolean and performs no arithmetic: the server answers
`integralTotal`, `integralNum` and `integralMinus`, and the row renders
`-¥{{item._minus}}` (`pagesF/static/template/order-discount.wxml:37-63`, with the
toggle at `:58` and "可用积分：N积分, 可抵扣M元" at `:73` — and the **same row again** in
the buy-now popup, `components/buy-format/price-details/price-details.wxml:47-63`). **Two client fields carry the
same figure**: the row's `_minus` comes off the `minusList` entry
(`settlement.js:411-412`) while the hint line reads the top-level `integralMinus`
(`:427`) — the server sends both, and the plan must not treat one as the source of
the other. `calc/*` is the preview
— three numbers and nothing written — and `order/*Submit` is what persists the
row.

**This is the one thing here that needs a core change, and the value it needs was
never granted.** The 2026-09-17 ruling named `kind: 'loyalty'` as "the value
`6.0-6.1-split-adjustments.md:194,307` reserved". The split plan does list it
(`:194`) and does say custom discounts write it (`:307`), but the shipped model
froze the list at two values — `KINDS = %w[promotion manual].freeze`
(`spree/core/app/models/spree/discount.rb:16`), validated with
`inclusion: { in: KINDS }` (`:27`) — and the serializer publishes it as a closed
enum (`discount_serializer.rb:8`). Nothing was reserved; `'loyalty'` is not merely
unvalidated, it is not representable.

`Spree::Fee` is the sibling built to be extended, and it is the **only** unfrozen
`KINDS` in core — nine others freeze theirs. It carries the shape to copy: `KINDS`
without `.freeze` and a comment saying extensions may append (`fee.rb:10,14`), the
validation reading the constant through a lambda so an append is seen
(`inclusion: { in: ->(_fee) { KINDS }, allow_blank: true }`, `:25`), and the open
tier on the wire (`enum_type_name: 'FeeKind'`, `fee_serializer.rb:8`), which
`spree/api/lib/typelizer/open_enums.rb:1-9` names as the archetype. `Discount`
becomes the second. The tradeoff belongs in the open: because this gem is an
extension, an appended kind makes the generated `DiscountKind` union depend on
which extensions are installed. `Spree::Actor` refuses that tradeoff — a frozen
`BUILT_IN_KINDS` naming only what core registers, an open union, and no
autocomplete for an extension's kind (`actor.rb:14-21`). Either shape is
defensible; a gem un-freezing a core constant is not, so the change lands in
`spree/core` and `spree/api`. No column and no migration change is needed:
`spree_discounts` already permits a row with no promotion source
(`6.0-6.1-split-adjustments.md:353`) and the adjusters already clamp so combined
discounts never push an adjustable below zero (`:99`).

**Why not a promotion action**, which would need no core change: the promotion
engine reconciles its own rows destructively —
`adjustable.discounts.where(kind: 'promotion').destroy_all unless best`
(`6.0-6.1-split-adjustments.md:269`, with `:271,275`) — so a discount whose amount
depends on a customer's balance and an opt-in boolean, which is not a "best action
wins" computation, would be cleared by any recalculation in which no action is
best.

The debit is a separate act from the row but not a later one. The row follows the
live cart and then the order, exactly one owner
(`typed_adjustment_line.rb:11-12`), and the points leave the balance in the same
`order/*Submit` request that writes it — the one ruling that covers both paths is
above.

### Growth value drives the tier; the threshold is the membership plan's

The client states the relationship outright:
`/{{userVipInfo.nextLevel}}成长值` (`components/me/personal/personal.wxml:17`) and
"还差 `levelExperience` 可升级至 `nextLevel`" (`pagesB/member/grow/grow.wxml:11-12`).
Growth value is compared against the next tier's threshold, and the tier's
threshold column is `spree_membership_tier_settings.threshold`, which the
membership plan owns.

That is the clean split and it is why these are two plans: **this plan owns the
value and the ledger that produced it; that plan owns the ladder and the
threshold.** Neither has to know the other's tables — a tier promotion reads one
number, and a ledger entry never needs to know what a tier is.

Until the ledger lands, the membership plan's statement stands and tier changes
are operator-assigned.

### The reason vocabulary is operator data

The client hardcodes four growth value reasons
(`pagesB/member/grow/grow.js:14-26`) and translates them with a local map:

| Hardcoded value | Displayed |
| --- | --- |
| `consume` | 消费获赠 — earned by spending |
| `consume_return` | 消费退回 — clawed back by a refund |
| `person_info` | 完善资料 — completing the profile |
| `manual` | 手工调整 — a manual adjustment |

This is the same defect class as the membership tier names: a vocabulary fixed
on the client needs a client release to grow. The reason is a row keyed to an
operator-editable list, and the serializer returns the display label, so a fifth
reason is an operator action.

**评价积分 is already that fifth reason.** The review gem earns on an event this
plan does not see (`6.1-product-reviews.md`), so it appends a reason of its own
rather than this plan enumerating one for it — which is the same argument that
made the list a table instead of a constant, arriving one release early.

`consume_return` is also the answer to a question the scenario plan left open
("what happens to an already-granted entitlement when a scenario purchase is
refunded"): **the client already expects growth value to be clawed back on a
refund**, and the same reversal is what returns points.

### Earning runs on events: three inputs on the order, one service for everything else

Points and growth value are earned by the same events, so a subscriber reacts to
the order lifecycle and writes two ledger entries through one service. Three
inputs decide how much, and this section is those three, the reversal, and the door
every other earning channel comes through.

**1. 消费积分 — the store-wide ratio.** The client displays it as
"每消费{{integralRatio}}元得1积分" (`pagesD/integral/mall/mall.wxml:48`). It is a
store preference, per the standing rule that configuration uses Model Preferences.

**2. 商品积分 — a per-line value with its own cap.** The client shows
"预计额外获取 {{item.integral}}积分", and when that value is zero it shows
"已达商品所得积分上限" instead
(`components/buy-format/not-supported-goods/supported-goods.wxml:8-10`). The value
is 额外 — on top of 基础购物积分 — and it rides the **ordinary** cart line: it is
projected with `integralTip` and `memberDayFlag` in the general goods-line mapping
(`components/j-format/j-format.js:1275`), not inside a member-day component. So it
is a per-line input to the earn, and the ceiling is that line's own rather than a
store setting.

**3. 满 N 元门槛 — below it, nothing is earned.** The client renders
`shoppingIntegralThreshold` as "订单实付金额≥{{threshold}}元可获得奖励"
(`components/buy-format/not-supported-goods/not-supported-goods.wxml:9`). It is a
single number, so it is a preference beside the ratio rather than a mechanism.

**The base is the amount actually paid, after every reduction.** The same client
line states the rule — "商品积分值将按照优惠后（扣除积分抵扣、优惠券等活动）实付金额进行换算"
(`:9`) — so the deduction, a coupon and a promotion all reduce what is earned.
Reading the order's pre-discount total over-credits every order that carried a
reduction. That one line also fixes the shape of this section, because it names
**基础购物积分** and **商品积分值** as two separate things — inputs 1 and 2 above —
and opens with the threshold.

**The two multipliers arrive from elsewhere.** 生日双倍 is the membership right
`birthday_double_integral` and 会员日双倍 is `svip_date`
(`6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:510,513`), each carrying its multiplier as a
`preference` on the right's own subclass (`:543-547`). This plan reads them and
does not register them: the rights own when a multiplier applies.

**A fourth channel earns by hooking an event, and it is why no rule registry is
built.** The ledger service is the only door into a balance, and it is public for
exactly this reason: an extension that earns points on something that is not an
order subscribes to its own event and calls that service. Nothing registers a
matcher and nothing is validated against a list. The three inputs above are settings
on the one subscriber this plan owns, and a channel that is not an order cannot be
written as a fourth setting — which is the whole argument for the event seam rather
than a rule table.

**评价积分 is that channel, and it belongs to the review extension rather than
here.** A review is not a purchase, so the order lifecycle cannot reach it, and the
review extension owns both the amount and the trigger. The client already splits it
that way: the order payload carries `integralNum`
(`static/behaviors/orderListBehavior.js:66`, read into the prompt at
`pages/order/order.js:188-193`) and the review prompt renders it as "得{{N}}积分"
beside 立即评价 (`components/order/evaluate-medal/evaluate-medal.wxml:13`). The
server states what a review is worth **before the review exists**, and crediting it
when the review is submitted is the review extension's call to this plan's service.
The review extension also takes the channel's own line in the 赚积分 block
(`pagesD/integral/mall/mall.wxml:55-56`), "评价有礼获得积分奖励".

What does not exist is a **rules surface**, not the conditions: the client prints
each one from a server-supplied value — 每消费{{integralRatio}}元得1积分 (`mall.wxml:48`),
评价有礼获得积分奖励 (`:56`), 生日当天消费可获双倍积分 (`:66`), 完成任务获得积分奖励 (`:74`),
and the 满 N 元门槛 in the buy popup — while the 积分规则 control is a static article
rather than something rules are read from (`mall.wxml:38-40` → `mall.js:317-320`, a
web-view of `integral_description`).

The ledger is append-only. A refund does not edit the earn; it writes a reversal
row, which is exactly what `consume_return` names and what keeps the history the
customer can read true to what happened.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not build points on `Spree::StoreCredit`.** It requires a currency and it
  is a payment source. A points balance has neither property, and giving it one
  to reuse the code puts a marketing instrument on the liability side of the
  books.
- **Do not give points a currency.** They are an integer count. Any code that
  reaches for `Spree::Money` on a points amount is wrong.
- **Do not schedule expiry.** `expires_at` on the grant is the truth, read the
  way `Spree::GiftCard` reads it. A job that flips a status will eventually not
  run, and the points will silently never expire.
- **Do not store the balance.** It is the sum of usable grants' `remaining`, recomputed
  inside the account's lock on every spend; only the ledger service writes a grant's
  `remaining` and nothing else touches it.
- **Do not allocate oldest-first.** Soonest-expiry-first, or customers lose the
  points that were about to expire.
- **Do not fix the reason vocabulary in code.** The client hardcodes four and
  needs a release to add a fifth; the serializer returns the label.
- **Do not put a points price in `Spree::Price`.** It requires a currency.
- **Do not make the points balance seller-scoped.** It belongs to the customer
  and spans every seller (`docs/plans/`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “A membership and a points balance belong to the customer”)). A missing `siteId` in a
  payload is not the evidence for this — `siteId` rides every request's headers
  (`static/config/api.js:40-49`). The catalogue row may be seller-scoped and the
  balance may not, or a customer's points fragment across shops.
- **Do not issue a coupon or a membership card from this gem.** The issue step is
  registered by the plan that owns the thing issued.
- **Do not read growth value out of `god/*`.** That is 酒神值, a different
  counter, in an excluded program. The growth value ledger is
  `vip/getUserLevelRecord`.
- **Do not route the deduction through a promotion action.** The promotion engine
  reconciles its own rows destructively (`6.0-6.1-split-adjustments.md:269,271,275`),
  so a discount whose amount depends on a customer's balance would be cleared by
  any recalculation in which no action is best. It is a `Discount` with
  `kind: 'loyalty'`, which is why opening that constant is core work and not
  something this gem can do for itself.
- **Do not debit the points at payment.** Both mechanisms debit at creation (ruled
  2026-09-17), and the reversal for a purchase that never completes belongs in
  `Spree::Orders::Cancel`'s `after_cancel` hook, inside its transaction, rather
  than in a subscriber on `order.canceled`. A debit at payment reopens the window
  in which one balance is spent twice, once in the mall and once on an ordinary
  order.
- **Wire the gem in three places or its specs never run:** the starter
  `server/Gemfile` path block, `.github/workflows/tests.yml`, and
  `scripts/test/rspec`.

## Open Questions

1. ~~**Is `lifetime_earned` wanted for growth value, or is the current value the
   whole story?**~~ **Settled: both kinds carry it** — **this plan's own answer, not
   a ruling**: an audit on 2026-09-18 found no entry for it in `fork-decisions.md`, so it
   stands as a design choice open to challenge rather than as a recorded decision. No client
   screen asks for the second number on growth value — 成长值记录 renders 当前成长值
   and the ladder arithmetic and nothing else (`pagesB/member/grow/grow.wxml:5-13`,
   read at `pagesB/member/grow/grow.js:50-56`), and the member card shows the same
   single figure (`components/me/personal/personal.wxml:16`). The cumulative number
   belongs to the excluded 酒神值 page, where it is labelled 累计酒量值
   (`pagesB/god-user/detail/detail.wxml:14`). So this is an operator requirement
   rather than one the client states: `lifetime_earned` is populated for **both**
   kinds, and the growth value read keeps exposing only `experience` until a screen
   reads the total.
2. **Where do the two rates live when they differ per site?** The client reads one
   store-wide earn ratio, so this plan assumes one — but there are **two** figures,
   not one: `getIntegralConversionRatio` is the earning side
   ("每消费N元得1积分") and is display-only, while redemption has its own
   ("每N个可抵扣1元"). Modelling one preference would conflate them. If the
   operator also wants a seller-level rate — which the seller-scoped member shelf
   suggests — the preference needs a per-seller override, and that is a third
   mechanism.
3. ~~**评价积分 is the one earning channel with no home.**~~ **Settled: it belongs
   to the review extension** (`6.1-product-reviews.md`, ruled 2026-09-17 and
   recorded in `fork-decisions.md`). A review is not a purchase, so the extension that
   earns on it subscribes to its own event and calls this plan's ledger service —
   the same seam every other earning channel uses. The proposed `PointRule` family
   is withdrawn; see *Earning runs on events*.
