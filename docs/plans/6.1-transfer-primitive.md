## Summary

Three plans in this set each designed the same small machine: **a customer gives something away, an opaque token carries it to a recipient who can read it before signing in, and it ends one of three ways — accepted, cancelled, or expired.** The three built it three times, to the point where the vocabulary drifted: the coupon wallet and the gift-card plan end a transfer in `accepted`, the membership plan ends it in `claimed` (`6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:326`), and the difference is one more thing for the client to translate and for a later "gifts I received" list to reconcile.

The audit of 2026-09-18 filed this as the first of four duplications, and the ruling is the same shape as the one that produced the grant primitive: **one row, three domains on top of it** (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-18, “The four cross-plan rulings”)). It differs from the grant primitive in one way that makes it simpler: there are no *kinds* here, because what a transfer moves is already a record owned by another plan — a coupon holding, a gift card, a membership card. So this primitive is a row, a contract and a service, with no registry, no gem and no second vocabulary.

**What is common is the transfer, not the thing transferred.** The three differ exactly where they should: a coupon transfer moves a holding and keeps the promotion linkage; a gift-card donation moves a card and holds it against spending while pending; a membership-card transfer activates a card and starts a term for whoever claimed it. Those are the three domains' own business and stay there. What they share is the token, the window, the three endings, and the fact that the recipient can look before signing in.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **One row, three domains, no per-domain transfer tables (ruled 2026-09-18).** A coupon possession, a gift card and a membership card are three different things, and that is precisely why the *transfer* of each should not be three implementations: the shared part is not the object but the journey. **Accepted trade-off:** the row carries columns one domain does not use (`message` is the coupon wallet's and the gift-card plan's; the membership card has no message) and the discipline that keeps that honest is the contract below, not the schema.
- **The row lives in core, and there is no gem (ruled 2026-09-18).** The grant primitive's ruling — row in core, gem for the kinds and the service (`6.1-grant-and-benefit-primitive.md:29`) — applies here with its gem half removed, because there are no kinds to own: the "kind" is the class of the record being moved. A gem around one table and one service would add a dependency and a wiring step and buy nothing. By this fork's merge-cost rule the placement is nearly free: **a new table, a new base model and a new service are new files**, and no file upstream owns is edited.
- **The vocabulary is `pending` / `accepted` / `canceled`, and `expired` is a date, not a status (ruled 2026-09-18).** This adopts the discipline the grant primitive already ruled for expiry (`6.1-grant-and-benefit-primitive.md:34`) and it is `Spree::GiftCard`'s own (`gift_card.rb:23-25,149-167`): `DISPLAY_STATUSES = statuses + %w[expired]`, `expired?` reads the date, and the stored status never takes that value. The membership plan's `claimed` becomes `accepted` — the client's own state map does not change, because its two states (赠送中, 已赠送) are derived from the card plus the transfer, not from the word (`6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:338-344`).
- **The effect belongs to the owner of the thing, through a contract, not a registry.** The transferable implements three methods — `on_transfer_given(transfer)`, `on_transfer_accepted(transfer)`, `on_transfer_canceled(transfer)` — and the primitive calls them inside its own transaction. This is deliberately **not** a registered-subclass family: the audit counted five new registries in this plan set (`audit-cross-plan-siloing.md`, D3) and none of them is needed to answer "what happens when this is accepted". A model that does not implement the contract cannot be transferred, and that is a validation rather than a registry.
- **Three routes, one model.** The client addresses the three by different endpoints and reads different payloads, so the routes stay per-domain (`coupon_transfers`, `gift_card_donations`, `membership_card_transfers`) as three thin controllers over one table. Collapsing them into one polymorphic route would put the payload differences into the response instead of the path, and the client's link shapes are already fixed by the mini program.
- **The primitive never moves money and never writes a ledger row.** A gift-card donation writes `transfer_out` and `transfer_in` in *that plan's* ledger (`6.1-gift-card-purchase-and-transfer.md:54`), from its own callback. The rule the grant primitive states for itself holds here word for word: the shared row records the journey, the owner performs the move.
- **The window is `expires_at`, and it is the only thing that ends a transfer by itself.** No job flips a status. One consequence is named rather than hidden: the gift-card domain *reserves* something at give time — the card's hold — and a hold is money, so a pending transfer that has passed its date must release it from that domain's own path rather than from a date nobody reads. That release is the gift-card plan's obligation, stated in its own constraints, and it is the single place in this design where an expired transfer needs a reader.
- **Sending is not this plan's, either.** A transfer that wants to tell the recipient does so through `6.1-notifications.md`, like every other consumer in this set; the primitive sends nothing.

## Design Details

### The row

```ruby
create_table :spree_transfers do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :transferable, polymorphic: true, null: false  # the holding, the card, the membership card
  t.references :from_customer, null: false                    # who gave it
  t.references :to_customer                                   # nil until accepted
  t.string   :to_phone                                        # the address the gift was sent to
  t.string   :status, null: false                             # pending / accepted / canceled
  t.string   :token, null: false                              # has_secure_token, opaque
  t.string   :message
  t.datetime :accepted_at
  t.datetime :canceled_at
  t.datetime :expires_at
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end

add_index :spree_transfers, :token, unique: true
add_index :spree_transfers, [:transferable_type, :transferable_id],
          unique: true, where: "status = 'pending' AND deleted_at IS NULL",
          name: 'index_spree_transfers_on_transferable_while_pending'
```

- `Spree::Transfer` inherits `Spree.base_class`, includes `Spree::SingleStoreResource`, uses `has_status`, `acts_as_paranoid` and `has_secure_token :token` — the convention the codebase already uses for bearer tokens (`spree/core/app/models/spree/company_invitation.rb:21`, `refresh_token.rb:7`), and the reason the token is never derived from the record it carries.
- **One pending transfer per thing**, enforced by the partial unique index rather than by a validation alone — the membership plan already reached for exactly this shape for its own table (`6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:326`), and it is what stops a double tap from opening two claim windows on one card.
- **`from_customer_id` is non-null and `to_customer_id` is nullable**, which is the opposite of the grant row's choice (`6.1-grant-and-benefit-primitive.md:69`) and for the same reason: a grant can exist before anyone owns it, a transfer cannot exist before somebody gives it.
- **`store_id` is on the row**, so a transfer is scoped and queried like every other store-owned record, and the public token read resolves through the store the request already carries.
- The scope that reads the window is a query, not a job: `pending` excludes what is past `expires_at`, and `display_status` reports `expired` exactly as `Spree::GiftCard` does.

### The contract

```ruby
# On the transferable — the coupon holding, the gift card, the membership card.
def on_transfer_given(transfer)     # reserve it: the card's hold, nothing for the others
def on_transfer_accepted(transfer)  # move it: ownership, ledger rows, activation
def on_transfer_canceled(transfer)  # release the reservation; the client's 会员卡退回账户中
```

- The three methods run **inside the service's transaction**, so an accepted transfer and the move it caused commit together or not at all.
- A model that answers none of them is not transferable, and `Spree::Transfers.give!` says so by name rather than failing later at accept time.
- The gift-card domain's `on_transfer_given` is where the hold is placed and its `on_transfer_canceled` is where it is released; the membership domain's `on_transfer_accepted` is the activation the client sends one call for (`6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md:329-334`); the coupon wallet's moves the holder and nothing else, because a promotion linkage has no balance to hold.

### The service

```ruby
Spree::Transfers.give!(from:, transferable:, to_phone:, message: nil, expires_at:)
Spree::Transfers.accept!(transfer, customer:)   # claims it for a customer, signed in by now
Spree::Transfers.cancel!(transfer)              # while pending, by the giver
```

- `accept!` takes the customer rather than reading a session, because the recipient may have created an account in the same request — the gift-card plan's own route does exactly that (`6.1-gift-card-purchase-and-transfer.md:71`).
- Both transitions are idempotent by status: accepting an accepted transfer and cancelling a cancelled one return the record rather than moving it twice, which is what makes a double tap harmless on a phone that lost its connection mid-request.
- Reads are scopes: `pending`, `for_recipient(customer)`, `expiring_before(date)`. A consumer renders the token-addressed read from `find_by(token:)` and its own serializer, because the payload differs per domain.

### What this plan does not build

No endpoints, no controllers, no serializers, no dashboard surface, no registry, no gem, and no notification. It is a table, a base model, a module of three functions and a validation.

## Migration Path

1. ~~**The core addition**~~ **Built (2026-09-23)**: `spree_transfers` with the one-pending-window index (partial where the adapter has partial indexes, over a stored key where it does not, as core's seller transfers and the membership gem's terms already do), `Spree::Transfer` with `has_secure_token`, the window scopes and `display_status`, and `Spree::Transfers.give!/accept!/cancel!` calling the contract inside their own transaction. A refusal from the thing being moved raises `Spree::Transfers::Refused` and takes the transition with it. `Spree::Transfer` publishes lifecycle events through `Spree::Api::V3::TransferSerializer`.
2. **The three consumers convert in one change**, because a half-migrated set is two vocabularies at once: `6.1-coupon-wallet.md` drops its transfer table and implements the contract on `Spree::CouponHolding`; `6.1-gift-card-purchase-and-transfer.md` drops `spree_gift_card_donations`, keeps its ledger's `transfer_out`/`transfer_in` rows in its own callback, and states the hold release; `6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md` drops `spree_membership_card_transfers` and moves `claimed` to `accepted`. Each keeps its own routes and serializers.
3. **Each consumer's `Depends on` header names this plan in the same change** — the both-headers rule the architecture plan states (`6.1-miniprogram-extension-architecture.md:168`).
4. **Record the ruling** in `fork-decisions.md`, and close the audit's D1 in `audit-cross-plan-siloing.md` with a pointer to this plan rather than leaving it as an open row.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not build a second transfer-shaped table.** A fourth thing that is given away joins this one, and the way to know it fits is that it can answer the three contract methods.
- **Do not move money or write a ledger row here**, and do not let a consumer's ledger become this primitive's business. The gift card's two rows are the gift-card plan's.
- **Do not store `expired` as a status and do not add a sweep job.** The window is a date fact; a job that flips statuses would be the second implementation of a rule the codebase already has.
- **Do not read the status without reading the date.** A pending transfer past `expires_at` is expired, and every consumer that shows or acts on one — including the gift-card hold release — must go through the scope rather than through `pending?`.
- **Do not let a domain write a transfer's status directly.** Every transition goes through the service, which is what keeps the contract's callbacks from being skipped.
- **Do not add a registry of transfer kinds.** The transferable's class is the kind, and the audit counted five new registries in this plan set already.
- **Do not give this primitive a route.** The client's three link shapes are per-domain and the payloads differ; a polymorphic route would move that difference into the response body.

## Open Questions

1. **Can the three routes collapse into one later?** A fourth consumer would make the argument for a single polymorphic route stronger; today the client's own link shapes (`receiveGiftCardDonationUrl`, the coupon wallet's transfer link, the membership share link) and their differing payloads argue for keeping three. **What would unblock it: a fourth domain, or the client folding its three receive pages into one.**
2. **Does the gift card's hold release need an active sweeper after all?** This plan says the release happens from the gift-card domain's own read path, because a date nobody reads cannot release money. If that plan finds the lazy release insufficient — a card held by an abandoned transfer that nobody ever reads again — the answer is a sweep owned by *that* plan, not a change to this row's expiry rule. **What unblocks it: the gift-card plan's answer, which it owes before implementation.**
