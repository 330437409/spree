## Summary

A provider gem, `spree_cainiao`, that lets a Spree store issue, cancel and track
**Cainiao electronic waybills** (菜鸟电子面单) for merchants who hold their own
monthly settlement accounts with Chinese couriers. It implements two core
interfaces — `Spree::FulfillmentProvider::Base` and
`Spree::DeliveryRateProvider::Base` — and adds no tables, no routes and no new
abstractions. Waybill numbers are obtained through `cainiao.waybill.ii.get` at
fulfil time, cancelled through `cainiao.waybill.ii.cancel`, and tracked through
the carrier's own scan feed, which is normalized into Spree's delivery vocabulary
and recorded as `Spree::DeliveryEvent` rows.

The rate provider **does not call Cainiao for a price**, because the electronic
waybill line has none to give: Cainiao issues the waybill number and the
merchant's freight is settled on their own contract with each courier. The
provider delegates pricing to the method's calculator — exactly as
`DeliveryRateProvider::Internal` does — and exists only to attach the courier and
product identity to each quote, which is what lets a delivery method carry a
courier at all. See "Why a rate provider, and what it is not" below.

This plan is **not** core-change-free, but it no longer treats that as an open
question. Two small additive core changes are settled prerequisites, each with a
named home — see "Core Prerequisites" below: a weight-bracket shipping calculator
(Spree cannot currently express Chinese domestic pricing), which has its own plan
at `6.1-weight-bracket-shipping-calculator.md` and no dependency on this one, and
a `json` format on `ShippingLabel` (so the cloud-print payload has somewhere to
live that is not a JSON response), which is a change list rather than a design
question. Neither is implemented by this plan, and neither is left as a question
for a reader to answer.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

### Why a rate provider, and what it is not

- **The gem ships a `DeliveryRateProvider` that delegates pricing to the
  calculator (2026-09-10).** It invents no price and calls no rating endpoint.
  `Internal` already delegates to the calculator; its class comment says it
  "carries no carrier metadata and no delivery date", and that omission is
  precisely the problem. Spree has no field that says *which external courier a
  delivery method ships with* except `Spree::DeliveryMethodService#carrier` —
  which is validated, serialized in the Admin API and writable in a single
  `PATCH`. But service rows are matched against provider estimates:
  `Stock::Estimator#calculate_delivery_rates` calls
  `delivery_method.offers_service?(estimate)` for **every** provider, and
  `offers_service?` is `services.empty? || service_for(estimate).present?`. An
  `Internal` estimate carries `carrier: nil, service_level: nil`, so a
  calculator-priced method that holds a service row with a real courier matches
  nothing and is **silently dropped from checkout**. A method therefore cannot
  carry a courier unless its rate provider emits quotes that name one. That is
  the whole reason this provider exists.
- **The provider is a thin annotation over the calculator, and must stay that
  way (2026-09-10).** `uses_calculator?` returns true, so the Estimator consults
  the calculator for the amount, applies no service-row markup, and keeps the
  pricing form visible in the admin UI. `requires_address?` returns true, which
  `rate_provider_must_ship` requires of a method whose fulfillment provider ships.
  If anyone later gives this provider its own arithmetic or a Cainiao call, they
  have changed what it is and the decision must be revisited.
- **Quoting freight from Cainiao is not possible on this product line
  (2026-09-10).** `cainiao.waybill.ii.get` takes a `cp_code` and returns a
  waybill number; it returns no price. Cainiao's only published rate cards
  (`cainiao.pickup.tieredpricing.*`) belong to a different product line —
  商家寄件 / 菜鸟裹裹, where Cainiao supplies the capacity and therefore sets the
  price — and they are a *table* keyed on the sending city, the destination
  province and a daily-order-volume tier, not a per-order quote.
- **The two product lines are not interchangeable (2026-09-10).** 电子面单
  (this plan) is for a merchant with their own courier accounts; 商家寄件/裹裹 is
  for a merchant buying Cainiao capacity. Different endpoints, different pricing,
  different onboarding. A future `spree_cainiao_pickup` gem could serve the
  second line; nothing here should be bent to half-serve it.

### The pricing model Spree does not have

- **Spree cannot express Chinese domestic courier pricing, and this gem does not
  fix that (2026-09-10).** The six shipped shipping calculators are
  `FlatRate`, `FlexiRate`, `PerItem`, `PriceSack`, `FlatPercentItemTotal` and
  `DigitalDelivery`. `FlatRate`'s weight preferences are suppression thresholds,
  not brackets. None of them is 首重 + 续重 — a first-weight price plus a
  continued-weight price per unit, by destination zone — which is how every
  Chinese courier prices a domestic parcel. A merchant on this gem can therefore
  only approximate their contract today, and a basket whose weight crosses a
  bracket boundary will be priced wrong.
- **The fix belongs in core, not in this gem (2026-09-10).** A
  `Calculator::Shipping::WeightBracket` is the right layer: pricing is a
  calculator's job, it is useful to every Chinese merchant with every courier
  rather than to Cainiao alone, and a registered calculator gets its
  configuration form in the dashboard for free — the admin UI renders calculator
  preference schemas generically, which a `DeliveryRateProvider` cannot do
  because providers have no preference schema and their pricing form is hidden.
  Per-zone rates need no nested configuration: one delivery method per
  destination `Spree::DeliveryZone`, each with that zone's first/continued
  prices, which is how merchants already think about 一区/二区/三区.
  **The calculator is planned separately and is not a blocker**:
  `6.1-weight-bracket-shipping-calculator.md` owns it, it depends on nothing in
  this plan, and it can land first. Until it lands, this gem prices with whatever
  calculator the merchant has — the rate provider is the seam either way, so
  nothing here changes when it arrives.
- **Cainiao is authoritative for the waybill, never for the money
  (2026-09-10).** The gem never writes `fulfillment.cost`, never touches an order
  total, and records nothing on the label except what the merchant paid the
  courier. `ShippingLabel#cost` stays what the 6.0 plan already says it is:
  merchant accounting, admin-only, never shopper money.

### The waybill itself

- **A district is never required (2026-09-10).** The waybill's address object
  takes `province` (required, a Chinese name, under 20 characters), `city`,
  `district`, `town` (all optional) and `detail` (required). Nothing carries an
  administrative division code. Spree's address model is therefore sufficient as
  it stands: province, city and the free-text detail are all the waybill needs,
  and no core address column is added. A district the merchant wants printed goes
  in the detail line, where a Chinese address already carries it.
- **The province name comes from a table the gem owns (2026-09-10).** Spree
  stores `state_code` — an ISO subdivision code such as `BJ` — and
  `Spree::Address#state_validate` clears `state_name` whenever a code is present,
  so a merchant's typed "北京" is not retained. `state.name` resolves through ISO
  reference data and answers in English, which Cainiao will not accept. The gem
  therefore ships a static map of the 34 province-level codes to their Chinese
  names. A lookup table in the gem, not a core concern.
- **The map retires with the division gem (2026-09-18).** `6.1-administrative-division-gem.md`
  owns one four-level tree for the whole set, so `lib/spree_cainiao/province_names.rb`
  is deleted from the layout below and `AddressParams` reads
  `Spree::AdministrativeDivision` by code instead of a gem-local hash. The one thing
  the tree cannot do alone is cross from an ISO subdivision code to a bureau code,
  because a Chinese address carries `state_code = BJ` and not `110000`; that 34-row
  crosswalk ships as one of the division gem's versioned mapping files, beside the
  Tencent and Amap ones, which keeps vendor codes out of storage. Nothing else in
  this plan changes.
- **The carrier is read from the selected rate, not from configuration
  (2026-09-10).** The method offers one rate per service row; the customer
  chooses one; `purchase_label` reads
  `fulfillment.selected_delivery_rate.carrier` and `.service_level`. This is
  exactly how the EasyPost reference provider resolves the carrier it buys from,
  and it is core's own doctrine — "the selected rate carries the carrier service
  the customer chose — the delivery method no longer pins one". It also means
  the courier is an order-level fact that survives the merchant editing the
  method afterwards.
- **The waybill is a cloud-print payload, not a document (2026-09-10).** The
  response's `print_data` is a JSON document the Cainiao cloud-print client
  renders from a template, carrying its own RSA signature — not a PDF, PNG or
  ZPL. `Spree::ShippingLabel#format` is validated against those three and
  `allow_blank: true`. The payload is stored as a **private-storage attachment**
  under a `json` format rather than in the label's `metadata`, so it never
  appears in a label or order response. This is a settled prerequisite with a
  named change list — see "Core Prerequisites"; the reasoning is in "Where the
  print payload lives" below. Rendering a PDF ourselves is explicitly not
  attempted.
- **Printing is a download in the first version (2026-09-10).** The merchant
  prints by downloading the stored payload and opening it in the Cainiao
  cloud-print client. Pull-mode printing (the client polls a URL we serve) and
  network-printer binding (`cainiao.waybill.cloudprint.netprint.bind`) are
  deliberately later: they are different products with different setup, and
  neither is needed to prove the integration works.
- **Privacy waybills (隐私面单) are out of scope for the first version, and the
  exclusion is documented rather than silent (2026-09-10).** Cainiao can mask the
  recipient's name and phone on the printed waybill, with real endpoints
  (`cainiao.waybill.privacy.subscription.get`,
  `cainiao.waybill.privacy.seller.order.get`) and a `need_encrypt` request
  parameter. Under China's Personal Information Protection Law that is a
  compliance question, not a nicety. It is nonetheless deferred, because the
  first version has to prove the waybill and tracking flow before it earns a
  compliance surface — and because the masking Cainiao offers is purchased and
  configured on the merchant's own account, so a merchant enrolled in it is
  already covered by their contract for the waybill Cainiao prints. **The
  consequence is stated wherever it is relevant**: the developer doc names the
  exclusion, and the Open Question below keeps it visible so it is answered
  before the first merchant goes live rather than after.

### Tracking

- **Tracking has two channels, and the pull channel is the authority
  (2026-09-10).** Cainiao pushes scan updates to a callback URL, but it also
  exposes `cainiao.waybill.logisticstrace.get`, which returns the complete node
  array for a waybill on demand. A push-only design loses whatever the push lost;
  the query endpoint lets a reconciliation job re-read every open parcel and
  settle the difference. Both channels feed the same workflow, so a missed push
  costs a delayed scan rather than a lost one.
- **The gem normalizes; core stores (2026-09-10).** Cainiao returns a
  machine-readable `action` beside a human `description` — `GOT` beside `已揽收`
  — which is exactly the split `Spree::DeliveryEvent` wants. The gem maps
  `action` onto `Spree::Delivery::STATUSES` and passes `description` through
  untouched, because that is the line a Chinese buyer reads. Core learns no
  Chinese and no Cainiao vocabulary. The mapping table is closed for values the
  gem knows and everything else becomes `unknown`, never a dropped scan.

### Credentials and placement

- **Secrets are `:password` preferences, settings are `:hash` (2026-09-10).**
  The app key, app secret and push verification secret are separate `:password`
  preferences. Per-courier settings live in one `:hash` preference, keyed by
  `cp_code` — the monthly settlement account number (月结卡号), the sending branch
  code (寄件网点编码) and the goods description default.
- **The encryption gap is stated, not hidden (2026-09-10).**
  `spree_integrations.preferences` is YAML-serialised in plaintext; `:password`
  preferences are masked *on output* only, and a value inside a `:hash`
  preference is not masked at all. The gem therefore keeps every secret in a
  `:password` preference and never in the hash. Whether core should encrypt
  integration preferences is a core decision and is not smuggled in here.
- **Operator-only methods (2026-09-10).** `DeliveryMethod#seller_methods_use_own_providers`
  refuses a non-default `rate_provider` or `fulfillment_provider` on a
  seller-owned method. A Cainiao method is therefore always the operator's, which
  matches the 6.0 rule that sellers may upload and track but never purchase or
  refund — those need the operator's carrier account.
- **No new tables, no route mounting (2026-09-10).** The gem adds models and an
  engine registration only. Carrier pushes land on core's existing
  `POST /api/v3/webhooks/fulfillments/:integration_id`; the gem participates by
  implementing `parse_webhook_event`.

## Core Prerequisites

Two core changes are prerequisites. Both are **settled decisions with a named
home**, not open questions, and neither is left for a reader to resolve. Neither
is implemented as part of writing this plan.

### A weight-bracket shipping calculator

Owned by `6.1-weight-bracket-shipping-calculator.md`, which depends on nothing in
this plan and can land first. Spree's six shipping calculators cannot express
首重 + 续重, which is how every Chinese courier prices a domestic parcel, so
without it a merchant approximates their contract and a basket crossing a bracket
boundary is priced wrong. The calculator is the right layer: pricing is a
calculator's job, it is useful to every store that ships by weight rather than to
Cainiao alone, and a registered calculator gets its configuration form in the
dashboard for free — the admin API already returns each calculator's preference
schema and the dashboard renders it generically, which a `DeliveryRateProvider`
cannot do because providers have no preference schema and their pricing form is
hidden by design.

This plan needs that arithmetic to price correctly, **but not to be built**: the
rate provider delegates to whatever calculator the method carries, so when the
calculator lands nothing here changes.

### A `json` format on `ShippingLabel`

So the cloud-print payload has a lawful home in private storage rather than in a
JSON response. This is a change list, not a design question, and every site is
named:

| File | Change |
| --- | --- |
| `spree/core/app/models/spree/shipping_label.rb` | `FORMATS` gains `json`; `FILE_CONTENT_TYPES` gains `application/json` |
| `spree/core/app/workflows/spree/shipping_labels/record.rb` | `format_from_file` maps `application/json` to `json`, so a payload uploaded under another name is not recorded as a PDF; the `file_format` YARD comment lists the new value |
| `spree/api/spec/integration/spree/api/v3/admin/orders/labels_spec.rb` | the `file_format` rswag enum gains `json` |
| `docs/api-reference/*.yaml` | regenerated with `rswag:specs:swaggerize`, never edited by hand |

Nothing else keys off `FORMATS`. The dashboard has no label-format constant, and
`ShippingLabel#download_filename` falls back to `pdf` only when `format` is
blank, which cannot happen for a stored payload. The change is justified by the
provider contract itself — a provider whose carrier returns a printable payload
rather than a document — not by Cainiao, and any provider in that position needs
it.

## Design Details

### Gem layout

```
  lib/spree_cainiao/province_names.rb         ISO province code -> Chinese name
  app/models/spree_cainiao/
    integration.rb                            credentials, can_connect?, parse_webhook_event
    delivery_rate_provider.rb                 carrier/product annotation over the calculator
    fulfillment_provider.rb                   purchase_label / refund_label / tracking_url / create+cancel
    waybill.rb                                the cainiao.waybill.ii.get request and response
    waybill_cancellation.rb
    trace.rb                                  the logisticstrace.get response -> Scans
    tracker_event.rb                          action -> Spree::Delivery::STATUSES
  app/jobs/spree_cainiao/

  config/locales/en.yml, zh-CN.yml
  spree_cainiao.gemspec
  spec/                                       VCR recordings, secrets filtered
```

No `db/migrate/`. The gem is a provider under `spree/providers/`, named
`spree_cainiao` with module `SpreeCainiao`, matching `spree_easypost`.

Registration is the three-line `after_initialize` block every provider gem uses,
plus `Spree.tracking_carriers` entries for the couriers it supports (`sf`,
`zto`, `yto`, `sto`, `yunda`, `ems`, …), which is what gives a hand-entered
waybill number a badge and a tracking page. Core's sixteen entries are all
Western and European.

### The rate provider

```ruby
class DeliveryRateProvider < Spree::DeliveryRateProvider::Base
  def self.integration_class = 'SpreeCainiao::Integration'
  def self.requires_address? = true
  def self.uses_calculator? = true          # the calculator still sets the price

  # The services the merchant has configured for this method, so the admin
  # picker offers them instead of asking for free-form identifiers.
  def self.service_catalog(integration) ... end

  def estimates(package)
    cost = delivery_method.calculator.compute(package)
    return [] if cost.nil?                  # nil still suppresses the method

    delivery_method.services.map do |row|
      Estimate.new(cost: cost, carrier: row.carrier, service_level: row.service)
    end
  end
end
```

One estimate per configured service row, each carrying the courier and product
that row names, all at the calculator's price. The Estimator then applies the
existing filtering, VAT gross-up, tax resolution and sorting unchanged, and
`service_for(estimate)` resolves the row whose `label` becomes the rate's display
name — so "顺丰标快" reaches the storefront without core knowing any Chinese.

`service_catalog` returns `ServiceCatalog.listing` built from the integration's
`carrier_accounts`, because the merchant's contracted products are the honest
source: Cainiao's product-listing endpoint (`cainiao.waybill.ii.product`) can be
consulted once credentials exist, and either way the picker must not invent a
product the account cannot buy.

### The fulfillment provider

`generates_labels?` is true. `create_fulfillment` returns `{}` and
`cancel_fulfillment` returns `true` — dispatch *is* the waybill, and both must be
implemented because the base class raises `NotImplementedError` for each.

#### Issuing a waybill — `purchase_label(owner)`

Called by `Spree::ShippingLabels::Purchase`, which already refuses a second
purchase on an owner holding an active label, so the gem keeps no idempotency
bookkeeping of its own.

| Cainiao field | Source |
| --- | --- |
| `cp_code` | `fulfillment.selected_delivery_rate.carrier` |
| `product_code` | `fulfillment.selected_delivery_rate.service_level` |
| `sender` | the stock location's address, via `AddressParams` |
| `recipient` | the fulfillment's address, via `AddressParams` |
| `template_url` | `carrier_accounts[cp_code]['template_url']` or the default |
| `user_id` | `preferred_merchant_id` |
| `customer_code` | `carrier_accounts[cp_code]['customer_code']` — 月结卡号 |
| `shipping_branch_code` | `carrier_accounts[cp_code]['shipping_branch_code']` |
| `trade_order_info_dtos[].object_id` | the fulfillment's `number` |
| `trade_order_info_dtos[].order_info.trade_order_list` | **the fulfillment's `number`, not the order's** — see below |
| `trade_order_info_dtos[].package_info.weight` | `fulfillment.item_weight`, converted to grams |
| `trade_order_info_dtos[].package_info.items[]` | the fulfillment manifest |
| `trade_order_info_dtos[].package_info.goods_description` | `carrier_accounts[cp_code]['goods_description']`, falling back to a generic description |

**The order identifier is the fulfillment number, deliberately.** Cainiao's
parameter notes warn: "为避免出现冲突，请按电商平台真实订单号传入，**请避免使用
同个订单号重复取号**". An order split into three parcels is three waybills, and
passing the order's own number to all three is exactly the repeated-number case
the note warns about. The fulfillment number is unique per parcel and is still a
stable identifier we can join back on.

**`goods_description` is mandatory for some couriers.** The parameter notes say
it is required for 顺丰 ("顺丰取号必须传此参数"). The same pass over the parameter
table must produce a list of per-courier mandatory fields, because a missing one
surfaces as an unspecific rejection rather than as "you did not set X".

The response becomes a `Spree::LabelPurchase`:

| `LabelPurchase` | Source |
| --- | --- |
| `external_id` | the request's `object_id` |
| `carrier` | `cp_code` lowercased, so it matches a `Spree.tracking_carriers` key |
| `service` | `product_code` |
| `tracking_number` | `parent_waybill_code` when present, else `waybill_code` |
| `cost` | the merchant's own postage, if known; otherwise zero |
| `currency` | the store's currency |
| `format` | `json` |
| `file_url` | nil — the payload is returned inline and stored by the purchase workflow |
| `metadata` | `waybill_code`, `cp_code`, `real_cp_code`, `parent_waybill_code`, `template_url` — all small, no payload |

Preferring `parent_waybill_code` follows Cainiao's own instruction that in a
sub-parent consignment it is the number carrying the logistics detail and the one
to ship and query with. The printed child number is kept in metadata, which also
preserves the 6.0 plan's separation: the label records what was printed, the
delivery records what is tracked.

`AddressParams` maps `province` from the province-name table, `city` from
`address.city`, `detail` from `address1` and `address2` joined, and `name` and
`mobile` from the address. A blank province refuses the purchase with a message
naming the field, because Cainiao's rejection for a missing province is not
specific.

#### Where the print payload lives

The payload is a JSON document containing the recipient's name, phone and full
address. Three candidate homes were considered:

| Home | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| `ShippingLabel#metadata` | **Rejected.** `Admin::ShippingLabelSerializer` serializes `metadata` whole, and labels are embedded in order responses — so a full print payload, RSA signature included, would ride along in every label list and every order that embeds one. `metadata` is a generic column, not an artifact store, and the neighbouring serializer's own comment sets the house convention: "The file is streamed through `download_url`, never linked to storage." |
| Re-fetch on demand via `cainiao.waybill.ii.query.by.waybillcode` | **Rejected as the primary path.** It would avoid storing anything, but it breaks the 6.0 plan's stated goal that a merchant reprints "without the carrier account being connected" — and reprinting is exactly when an integration is most likely to be misconfigured. |
| A private-storage attachment, under a `json` format | **Chosen.** It matches the existing label-file convention exactly: private storage, content-type validated, streamed through `download_url`, never a public URL. Depends on the `json` format prerequisite below. |

Storing it as `text/plain` instead is not a workaround: content-type validation
reads the *sniffed* type, and a JSON body may sniff as `application/json`, which
the validator would reject. The exact change list is in "Core Prerequisites".

#### Cancelling a waybill — `refund_label(shipping_label)`

`cainiao.waybill.ii.cancel` withdraws a waybill number that was issued but not
used. This is not the same act as refunding postage, and the gem must not pretend
otherwise: the method returns `'refunded'` only when Cainiao confirms the number
was released before use, and `false` otherwise, which leaves the label's status
alone and tells the merchant to settle the postage with the courier directly.
Overstating a cancellation as a refund would make `ShippingLabel#cost` — the
merchant's own accounting — wrong.

### Integration: `SpreeCainiao::Integration`

| Preference | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `app_key` | `:password` | |
| `app_secret` | `:password` | |
| `push_secret` | `:password` | verifies inbound pushes |
| `merchant_id` | `:string` | `user_id` on the waybill request — the merchant id behind the waybill account, which differs from the shop id when one account serves several shops |
| `default_template_url` | `:string` | the cloud-print template |
| `carrier_accounts` | `:hash` | keyed by `cp_code`: `customer_code` (月结卡号), `shipping_branch_code` (寄件网点编码), `template_url`, `goods_description` |

`integration_group` is `'shipping'`, matching the EasyPost gem's gallery placement.

`can_connect?` calls `cainiao.waybill.ii.search` — "查询面单服务订购及面单使用
情况". It is the right probe for two reasons: it is an authenticated read, so it
proves the credentials, and it reports which couriers the account has actually
contracted, which is exactly what a merchant needs to see. It must rescue
broadly: a DNS failure or a timeout has to surface as a clean activation error
with the carrier's own message, never a 500.

`parse_webhook_event(raw_post, headers)` verifies the push signature against
`push_secret` (raising `Spree::Integration::WebhookSignatureError` when it is
missing or wrong, which the controller answers with 401), reads the payload,
resolves the delivery by waybill number within the store, and returns the
`Deliveries::UpdateTracking` keyword hash — `tracking_code`, `tracking_status`,
`scans:` — or `nil` for a push that is not a tracking update. The controller
already splats the returned hash, so no controller change is needed.

### Merchant configuration

```
SpreeCainiao::Integration            app key, app secret, push secret, merchant id,
                                     per-courier accounts keyed by cp_code
        +
Spree::DeliveryMethod                one per courier, e.g. "顺丰"
  rate_provider        = 'SpreeCainiao::DeliveryRateProvider'
  fulfillment_provider = 'SpreeCainiao::FulfillmentProvider'
  calculator           = the store's own price rule (see the pricing gap above)
  services             = one row per product the merchant sells:
                         SF / STANDARD_EXPRESS / "顺丰标快"
                         SF / ECONOMY          / "顺丰特惠"
        +
Spree::DeliveryMethodRule            weight / volume / item total eligibility
Spree::DeliveryZone                  destination zone — and, for weight-bracket
                                     pricing, one method per zone
Spree::PackageType                   carton dimensions and weight (store on metric, kg/cm)
```

A delivery method is created through the existing admin API in one `PATCH`, which
already permits `rate_provider`, `fulfillment_provider`,
`calculator_type`, `calculator_preferences` and
`services: [:id, :carrier, :service, :label, :markup_flat, :markup_percent, :position]`.
**No new admin surface is needed**, and that is the point of the service-row
design: the courier binding is a first-class, validated, serialized field rather
than something buried in `metadata`, which the Admin API neither accepts nor
returns.

Because `DeliveryMethod#fulfillment_provider` has no inclusion validation and
falls back to `Manual` when the constant does not resolve, the gem's README must
say plainly: install the gem first, then set the provider strings, and confirm
the method shows the Cainiao provider before going live. A wrong string fails
silently rather than loudly.

### Tracking

Three surfaces, in order of trust:

1. **Pull** — `cainiao.waybill.logisticstrace.get` returns
   `logistics_detail_dto_list`, each entry carrying `action`, `description` and
   `time` and nothing else. `SpreeCainiao::Trace` turns each into a
   `Spree::Deliveries::Scan` and the newest `action` into `tracking_status`.
   A reconciliation job walks deliveries that are not yet delivered and calls
   `Deliveries::UpdateTracking` — the same workflow the push uses, so the
   fingerprint rules and the monotonicity guard apply to both.
2. **Push** — Cainiao's tracking push, verified and normalized by
   `parse_webhook_event`. Fast, and the primary path in normal operation.
3. **Hosted page** — `cainiao.waybill.ii.logisticsdetail.url.get` returns an
   authorised H5 page for a waybill. This is what
   `FulfillmentProvider#tracking_url(delivery)` returns, which
   `Spree::Delivery#resolved_tracking_url` already prefers over the carrier
   template. Its documented restriction is that the waybill number must have been
   issued by the same app key, which is always true here.

**There is no scan id.** The documented node carries only `action`,
`description` and `time`, so `Spree::DeliveryEvent`'s fingerprint falls back to
core's digest over `occurred_at`, `status`, `description` and `location`. The gem
supplies no `external_id`.

**Subscribing.** Cainiao pushes only for waybills it has been told to watch.
`SpreeCainiao::SubscribeTrackingJob` registers the callback after a waybill is
issued and re-registers on failure with backoff. It is enqueued from
`purchase_label` and must never raise into the purchase: a merchant who cannot
subscribe still has a valid waybill, and the reconciliation job is the safety net.

**Reconciling.** `SpreeCainiao::ReconcileTrackingJob` is the correctness
backstop, and it needs a real design rather than "walk the open parcels":

- **Cursor batches, not a full scan.** It walks deliveries that are not
  `delivered` in `created_at` order using `ActiveJob::Continuable` with the last
  seen id as its cursor — the pattern `Spree::Imports::ProcessJob` and
  `ProductTypes::ApplyToProductsJob` already use — so a store with thousands of
  parcels does not load them all, and a deploy mid-run resumes instead of
  restarting.
- **Age priority.** Each run handles the oldest unbounded window first, so a
  parcel whose push channel died is noticed early rather than after the queue.
- **A skip window.** A delivery whose `updated_at` is inside the last few minutes
  is skipped: the push almost certainly just updated it, and re-reading it spends
  quota to learn nothing.
- **A quota budget.** The run stops after a configured number of carrier calls
  per pass, so a backlog degrades into more frequent runs rather than into a
  rate-limit ban.
- **A stopping condition.** A parcel that reaches a terminal status
  (`delivered`, `return_to_sender`, `failure`) leaves the working set.

Both jobs enqueue on `Spree.queues.default`. A host that wants a dedicated queue
adds one the same way the engine adds `taxons`, and the gem reads it when present.

### Status mapping

`SpreeCainiao::TrackerEvent::STATUS_MAP` maps Cainiao's `action` codes onto
`Spree::Delivery::STATUSES`. `GOT` is the one documented in full — it means the
parcel has been collected — and the transit, delivery, signed, return and failure
families are named in the node reference. Anything unmapped becomes `unknown`
rather than being dropped, matching the EasyPost mapper's rule that a merchant
looking at a parcel should see that something was reported even when we cannot
name it. The complete action list still needs to be collected from the push
documentation; until then the map is partial by design, not by accident.

### Errors

- A waybill refusal raises `Spree::Core::LabelPurchaseRefused` carrying Cainiao's
  own message, so the merchant reads "该地址不可达" or "单号不足" rather than a
  generic failure. Cainiao's documented error codes (`WAYBILL_CODE_NON_EXISTS`,
  `CP_CODE_NOT_SUPPORTED`, `WAYBILL_NOT_MATCH_CURRENT_APPKY`, and the account
  balance family) are translated into sentences that name what to fix.
- A network failure is reported with `Rails.error.report` and answered with
  `nil`, which the explicit buy-label step surfaces as a 422 and the one-click
  fulfil degrades to "no label yet" — the existing contract, unchanged. The
  catch-all must not swallow `LabelPurchaseRefused`, which is a deliberate refusal
  and a `StandardError`.
- A rating failure cannot arise: the rate provider makes no call. A calculator
  that returns nil still suppresses the method, unchanged.
- `parse_webhook_event` never raises for a payload it does not recognise; an
  unrecognised push is acknowledged so Cainiao does not retry something that will
  never succeed.

## Migration Path

**Step 0 is a Cainiao open-platform developer account with enterprise
qualification.** Without it, nothing below can be verified: the signature scheme
cannot be proven against a real request, VCR cannot record real responses, the
`action` vocabulary cannot be collected, and the sandbox question (is there one?)
is unanswerable. This is a procurement task, not an engineering one, and it gates
the whole gem.

1. **Gem skeleton** — gemspec, engine, registration, `PROVIDER_NAME`,
   `tracking_carriers` entries, locales, CI wiring modeled on the EasyPost gem.
2. **Integration** — preferences, `can_connect?` against
   `cainiao.waybill.ii.search`, client and signature confirmed against real
   requests, with VCR recordings and the app key, secret and settlement numbers
   filtered.
3. **Rate provider and delivery-method setup** — the annotation provider,
   `service_catalog`, and a documented admin walkthrough for creating a courier
   method with its service rows.
4. **Waybill** — `AddressParams`, the province-name table, the per-carrier
   mandatory-field list, the request builder, `purchase_label`,
   `create_fulfillment`, `cancel_fulfillment`, `refund_label`, and the
   `Spree::LabelPurchase` mapping. Request-level specs plus one VCR end-to-end
   purchase.
5. **Tracking** — `TrackerEvent`, the `action` map, `parse_webhook_event`, the
   push request spec (wrong signature, unsigned, unconfigured secret, unknown
   waybill, cross-store waybill all covered, mirroring the EasyPost spec), then
   `SubscribeTrackingJob` and `ReconcileTrackingJob`.
6. **Tracking URL and print** — `logisticsdetail.url.get` cached on the label's
   metadata rather than fetched per read, since it is called while serializing an
   order; and the print download.
7. **Docs** — a developer doc under `docs/developer/` covering installation, the
   one-method-per-courier setup, the pricing approximation and its limits, the
   fact that the label is printed through the Cainiao client rather than a
   generic PDF viewer, and the operator-only rule.

Step 4 additionally waits on the `ShippingLabel` `json` format change; step 3
does not need it, and the pricing calculator is an independent prerequisite that
can land first. Both are change lists rather than open questions — see "Core
Prerequisites".

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Nothing in the gem may write order money.** Not `fulfillment.cost`, not a
  delivery rate, not an adjustment.
- **The rate provider must not gain arithmetic or a Cainiao call.** It delegates
  to the calculator; if that changes, it is a different design and needs a
  decision.
- **Do not put a service row on a calculator-priced method without a provider
  that emits matching estimates.** `offers_service?` runs for every provider, so
  a row that matches nothing silently removes the method from checkout. This is
  the trap this design exists to avoid.
- **Every secret is a `:password` preference.** A secret must never be placed in
  the `:hash` preference, because hash preferences are neither encrypted nor
  masked.
- **The print payload never goes in `metadata`.** It is a private-storage
  attachment; `metadata` carries ids and flags only.
- **The order identifier on a waybill is the fulfillment's number**, never the
  order's, so that a split order does not repeat an order number across waybills.
- **The gem never marks a fulfillment delivered, and never writes
  `delivery.status` directly.** It reports scans; the workflows and the rollup
  decide.
- **The reconciliation job must stay batched, cursored and budgeted.** A version
  that walks every open parcel per run is a quota leak, not a backstop.
- **Do not claim the pricing story is solved.** Until a weight-bracket calculator
  exists, a merchant's freight is approximated, and a basket crossing a bracket
  boundary is priced wrong. The README says so.

## Open Questions

- **A Cainiao developer account is required, and may require enterprise
  qualification.** This gates everything, including whether a sandbox exists.
  Until it is settled the gem cannot be built beyond its skeleton.
- **The push documentation could not be read.** `openapi.express.cainiao.com`
  returns `403 NonCnameForbidden` (an unbound OSS host), `open.cainiao.com` is a
  JavaScript application that returns an empty shell to a direct fetch, and the
  Wayback Machine has no capture. The parameter shapes in this plan come from the
  TOP-lineage mirrors on `api.alidayu.com` and `doc.alidayu.com`, which are
  labelled "只适用于TOP接口" and may differ from the current platform in signing,
  parameter names or the push payload. The three things most likely to differ are
  the signing scheme, the push payload's field names, and the `action`
  vocabulary — and each of them is a one-file change because they are isolated in
  `Signature`, `Integration` and `TrackerEvent`. They must be confirmed before
  step 4.
- **The two core prerequisites are tracked in "Core Prerequisites", not here.**
  Both are settled — a change list for the `json` label format, and a separate
  plan for the weight-bracket calculator — so neither is an open question. They
  are listed in one place so a reader cannot mistake the pricing gap for
  something this plan already covers.
- **The complete `action` vocabulary is not collected.** The mapping table needs
  every action code Cainiao emits, which lives in the push documentation.
- **The complete `cp_code` list is not collected.** Examples confirm `SF`,
  `ZTO`, `YTO`, `STO`, `POSTB`. `alibaba.ascp.logistics.cp.get` may enumerate
  them; otherwise the list is the merchant's contracted couriers, which
  `cainiao.waybill.ii.search` already reports.
- **Per-courier mandatory fields are not collected.** `goods_description` is
  required for 顺丰 and is unlikely to be the only such field. A pass over the
  parameter table per courier is part of step 4.
- **Privacy waybills (隐私面单): out of scope for the first version, revisited
  before the first merchant goes live.** The decision is recorded under "The
  waybill itself" and the developer doc names the exclusion, so it is documented
  rather than silent. What is genuinely unresolved is the trigger: whether a
  merchant enrolled in Cainiao's privacy waybills needs anything from us beyond
  the `need_encrypt` parameter, or whether their own subscription covers the
  printed waybill entirely. That is answerable only with an account, and it is
  the reason this stays here as a question while the scope decision does not.
- **Application type: self-use or ISV.** Several waybill endpoints are marked as
  requiring authorisation and the mirrored samples pass a session key. Whether a
  merchant's own integration runs as a self-developed application (no session) or
  requires an OAuth grant changes `can_connect?`, token refresh and the admin
  setup copy. It must be settled before step 2.
- **Sub-parent consignments (子母件) and multi-piece shipments.** Cainiao returns
  a `parent_waybill_code` and supports `multi_packages_shipment`. This plan
  prefers the parent number as the tracking number and records the printed child
  in metadata, but does not model several labels under one consignment — the 6.0
  plan explicitly did not either. Recommended: out of scope for the first
  version.
- **Pull-mode printing and network printers.** `cainiao.waybill.cloudprint.netprint.bind`
  and `.verifycode` suggest a merchant who wants the cloud-print client to poll a
  URL, or to bind a network printer, rather than downloading a payload. Later.
- **Address reachability and transit prediction.**
  `cainiao.waybill.address.reachable.query` and
  `cainiao.data.logistics.delivery.aging.predict` belong to the quoting side,
  which this gem does not own. Noted so a later plan does not rediscover them.
- **Whether core should encrypt integration preferences.** Raised, not answered.
  Until it is, a Cainiao app secret sits in the database in plaintext, as every
  other integration's secrets do.
