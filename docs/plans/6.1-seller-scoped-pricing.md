## Summary

The client shows **one goods at up to three different prices**, reached from three kinds of page: 区域 (an area price, keyed to a warehouse and a delivery address, reached from a campaign zone), 现场推广/分销 (a promoter's price) and 线下 (an offline activity's price). All three send the same `siteId` header every request carries (`static/config/api.js:42`), the same field names come back (`price`, `specList[].specPrice`, `allowBuy`, `status`), and the page skeleton is the same component — so this is **three price contexts for one goods, not three goods** (`pagesA/goods-details/{area,on-site}`, `pagesA/components/common/on-site/format/format.js:79-86`).

That gives the plan two axes, and the audit of the rest of the set makes them separable:

- **The seller axis is already solved by the catalogue.** `fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17 entries) rules that a seller holds **offers** — `Spree::Variant` rows carrying its `seller_id` on the operator's master products — and ends with "prices are the seller's and stock is in the seller's warehouses". `Spree::Price` belongs to a variant (`price.rb:27`), so a seller's price is **that offer variant's own base price**. No `seller_id` on `spree_prices`, no seller axis in `Spree::Pricing::Context`, no new price rule — which matters, because the context and its cache key carry store, variant, currency, country, market, channel, user and quantity and **no seller at all** (`spree/core/lib/spree/core/pricing/context.rb:19-31`), and adding one would be a core change to the hottest read path in the platform.
- **The context axis is what the three pages differ in, and it is a channel question.** The walk consults a catalog-attached list before a rule-matched one (`pricing_provider/internal.rb:47-54`), a channel already owns a `default_catalog`, and core's own `ChannelRule` doc says the rule is grandfathered and "the forward path for channel pricing is the channel's default catalog" (`price_rules/channel_rule.rb:3-5`). `CatalogAssignment` records that Channel and Market "were assignable in the 6.0 pre-release but never read … a market catalog waits for the regional-assortment design" (`catalog_assignment.rb:6-9`) — which is this plan, arriving.

What the plan must not do is the thing the repo has refused twice: **put a merchant-specific amount where it does not belong** — the points plan refused a points-priced row because a price row must carry a currency (`price.rb:53`), and the Omnibus work tracks **base prices only**, because "price list prices are internal segmentation" (`5.4-6.0-eu-legal-compliance.md:75`). The ruling lands a seller's price on the offer variant's **base** price (`price_list_id IS NULL`, `price.rb:63`), which is the category Omnibus tracks; a channel-scoped price sits on a list and is segmentation — so the two axes land on opposite sides of that line, and the Constraint below says which one a report must read.

The shape that follows is unusually cheap: the seller axis needs no schema, the context axis is a channel core already prices through, and the context is carried by the request — so **the product read the client already calls needs no parameter, and this plan adds one route in total** (which seller holds an offer for a product).

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **A per-seller price is the offer variant's base price, and nothing new carries it** (ruled 2026-09-18). The offer *is* the seller's participation in the catalogue, so a seller's price is `price_list_id IS NULL` on its own variant — no column on `spree_prices`, no `seller_id` on `spree_price_lists`, no new rule kind, and the pricing context stays as it is.
- **The three contexts are channels, not a second seller axis** (ruled 2026-09-18). Each of area, popularize and offline resolves a channel, and the channel's default catalog carries the price list — the forward path core's own `ChannelRule` comment names. A context is then modelled once, reuses the audience machinery, and lands in the price provenance the line already records.
- **The context is request scope, resolved by core's own channel resolution — and the product read does not change at all** (ruled 2026-09-18). The channel is what `Spree::Api::V3::ChannelResolution` already resolves at the request boundary — the publishable key's binding first, then the `X-Spree-Channel` header (code or `ch_…` id, scoped to the store), then `current_store.default_channel` — and it is already written into `Spree::Current.channel` (`channel_resolution.rb:40-42,80-81`). So this plan **builds no resolution of its own**: the rewritten client sends the header for the context it is in (区域 / 现场推广 / 线下), or a key is bound to that channel, and everything downstream prices through it. Nothing is added to the products read, because `Spree::Pricing::Context#initialize` already falls back to it — `@channel = channel || Spree::Current.channel` (`context.rb:25`) — and `Spree::Current.channel` also feeds `catalogs_for` and the global pricing context (`current.rb`). The consequence is the point: the price on the read, the price the cart writes on the line and the price the checkout charges are **one context by construction**, not three that have to be kept in step. **Accepted trade-off:** the context belongs to the request, so the popularize case — whose detail call carries no identifier of its own — depends on the header the rewritten client sends or, failing that, the customer's binding; a signed-out visitor whose client sends neither sees the default channel's price. And two contexts cannot be compared inside one request.
- **The provenance a report needs already exists.** `spree_line_items.price_list_id` and `price_source` are written on the cart path (`carts/price_items.rb:97`) and copied to the order; a channel-scoped price is recoverable from them because the list names the channel. **What a seller's own price leaves behind is neither** — the ruling makes it the offer variant's base price, so `price_list_id` is nil and `price_source` is nil too (`price_resolution.rb:86-88` stamps nothing on the internal path), and the seller is recoverable from the variant rather than from the line.
- **Who funds a discount is this plan's to answer, and the precedent is expensive.** A price-list price leaves **no `Discount` row** — the walk returns a `Spree::Price` (`pricing_provider/internal.rb:36-38`) — so a reduction the seller did not agree to cannot be read off a discount and must be computed and snapshotted when the transfer is created, exactly as the member subsidy is (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “Commission follows the actual selling price”), and the five coupled core edits that ruling cost). **The cheap answer is that the channel's owner funds it**: an area, promoter or offline price is set by the party who runs that channel. This plan states the funding per context in Design Details, and names the offline case as the one it cannot settle on its own — Open Question 1 — rather than assuming the operator absorbs it.
- **The seller scope is read, never resolved.** `resolve_seller` is request scope (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “A mini-program "site" is a seller”)) and the architecture plan forbids a gem resolving it for itself (`:164`). This plan takes the seller from the request context and fails loudly if a price needs one and none is present — and now takes the context the same way.
- **No seller outside its service area is filtered out of allocation** — the routing plan's own constraint (`:572-579`), which this plan inherits: the area context narrows *which price is shown*, and never becomes a second eligibility mechanism beside `DeliveryZone`.
- **A missing price is an answer, not an exception.** The walk already returns a placeholder `Spree::Price` with a nil amount when nothing matches (`internal.rb:206-251`), so a goods with no price in this context renders as unpurchasable rather than erroring.

## Design Details

### The two axes

```ruby
# The seller axis needs no schema at all: the offer is a variant,
# and its base price row is the seller's price.
Spree::Variant.for_seller(seller).map(&:price)      # variant.rb:211-221

# The context axis is a channel, whose catalog owns the price list,
# and it is assigned for the request the way the seller is.
Spree::Current.channel = resolved_context_channel    # area / scene / offline
Spree::Channel          # default_catalog → its price_list → automatic or fixed
```

- **The seller comes from the request context, never from the record.** `Spree::Variant#resolved_seller` is `product&.seller || seller` (`variant.rb:425-427`) — product-first inference, and exactly what a price scoped to a *resolved* seller must not use; the architecture plan states the same rule ("do not let it infer the seller from an object it happens to hold", `6.1-miniprogram-extension-architecture.md:164`). The plan reads the routing plan's resolved seller and fails loudly when a context needs one and none is present. `Products::SelectBuyBox` has no seller parameter, and constraining it is "a design requirement rather than a tuning detail" (`fork-decisions.md` (2026-09-17, “Master-product catalog mode with seller offers is committed ”)).
- **The context resolves to a channel**, and the channel's catalog supplies the price. An `automatic` list inside a catalog derives on read (`6.0-price-list-automatic-pricing.md:27-33`), which is what a percentage-based area or offline price wants; a context whose price varies per goods uses explicit rows, and the pricing-strategy vocabulary already has the word for that (`catalog.rb:216-220`).
- **The three contexts keep the client's own `cartType` names on the wire** — `area`, `scene`, `offline` (`components/j-format/j-format.js:29`, `pagesA/components/common/on-site/format/format.js:13`) — and map to three channels the operator creates, not to three Ruby classes, because a fourth context is a row, not a release.

### What a context carries

The client reveals the inputs each context needs, and they are the plan's configuration surface:

- **Area** needs a warehouse (`shopId`, a `StockLocation` with that `seller_id`) and resolves a division from the administrative tree; its stock is the warehouse's, and the area read carries **both** `specStock` and `shopStock` — the explicit switch on `cartType` is the on-site pair's (`pagesA/components/common/on-site/format/format.js:115`).
- **Offline** needs a campaign zone (`specialId`) and an activity (`offlineId`), and prices goods the shop stocks.
- **Popularize** needs only the goods, and prices it for a channel whose promoter arrives as `onSitePuid` on the **submit** (`static/api/orderApi.js:31`), not on any read — the promoter's identity is the distribution plan's table, and this plan only states that the price shown does not depend on *which* promoter it is (the client's payload proves it: the detail call sends `{ id }` and nothing else).

### Price provenance and the report

`price_list_id` and `price_source` are written when the cart prices a line (`carts/price_items.rb:97`) and travel to the order. A channel-scoped price is therefore attributable **because its list is the channel's** — an operator can answer "what did the offline channel sell at" from the order lines without a new column. **The one case that loses the trail is the seller's own price**, and the plan says so rather than leaving it to be discovered: the ruling makes it the offer variant's base price, so the seller is recoverable only from the variant, never from the line.

### Funding, per context

Stated for each, because the member ruling showed what happens when it is not:

- **Area**: the seller's own regional price — the seller funds it, and nothing needs a subsidy.
- **Offline**: the operator runs the activity, so the operator funds it, and the seller's payout is made whole through the same `subsidy` transfer kind the membership ruling established (five coupled core edits, already carried by `6.1-membership-tiers-and-rights.md`) — **this plan consumes that capability, it does not re-open it**.
- **Popularize**: the promoter's channel, and the commission the distribution plan already models is where the margin comes from; no separate subsidy.

### What this plan does not build

No new price rule kind (the channel's default catalog is the forward path and `ChannelRule` is explicitly grandfathered); no `seller_id` on `spree_prices` or `spree_price_lists`; no seller axis in `Spree::Pricing::Context`; no second eligibility mechanism beside `DeliveryZone` — the routing plan's constraint (`:572-579`) already forbids the last one, and the first three are the cheap answers this plan chooses deliberately; and **no read of its own**, because the context rides the request and the product read already prices through `Spree::Current.channel`.

**A bundle needs no rule of its own here.** `6.1-product-bundles.md:158` hands this plan the question of how a bundle's component prices are scoped, and the answer is the same axis one level down: a bundle is one seller's composition, so its components are that seller's own offer variants and each carries its own base price. Nothing bundle-shaped is added.

## Store API Surface

| Method and path | Replaces |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/v3/store/products/:id` — **as it stands** | the three detail variants, because the context is request scope: the same read prices through whichever channel the request resolved |
| `GET /api/v3/store/products/:id/purchase` (or the preview the gaps plan owns) — **as it stands** | the three buy variants — price, stock and limits follow the same request-scoped channel |
| `GET /api/v3/store/products/:id/sellers` | `goods/getSiteIdByGoodsId` — which sellers hold an offer for this product |

**Only the third row is a route.** The two product rows add nothing: the read is core's, unchanged, and the context is resolved **by machinery that already ships** — `Spree::Api::V3::ChannelResolution` writes `Spree::Current.channel` from the key, the `X-Spree-Channel` header, or the store's default (`channel_resolution.rb:40-42,80-81`), and `Spree::Pricing::Context` reads it (`context.rb:25`). What the plan owes is the *naming*: which channel each context's pages ask for, and a client that sends it. Where a context genuinely cannot be resolved — no header, no key binding — the default channel answers, which is a wrong price rather than a missing one, so the client's rewrite is the guarantee here, not the server's.

**One route for seven endpoints.** The client's variants differ in *which context is asked for*, not in what is answered — and the context now travels with the request rather than as a parameter, so the read it already calls is the one that answers it.

## Migration Path

1. **The naming and the client contract** — three channels the operator creates, and the client sending `X-Spree-Channel` for the context it is in. There is no server-side assignment to build: `ChannelResolution` already writes `Spree::Current.channel` and the pricing context already reads it, so this step is the naming plus the client's half.
2. **The context model**: channels for the three contexts, and the catalog/list wiring that gives each one a price (configuration plus a read).
3. **The seller read**: `GET .../sellers` from the offer variants, and the confirmation against `resolve_seller` when a coordinate is available.
4. **The stock switch** (`shopStock` vs `specStock`) and the time-boxed price the area context carries — the timer itself is `6.1-flash-sales.md`'s shape, so this step consumes whatever that plan settles rather than inventing a second one.
5. **The buy-side limits**, and the funding wiring for the offline context — the only funding that needs any, since the area price is the seller's own and the popularize price rides the distribution commission (Design Details).
6. **The provenance answer**: the report that reads which price a line carries (`price_list_id`/`price_source`, and the variant when the list is nil) — where the last Constraint lands.
7. **The admin**: a channel's catalog and price list are already admin surfaces; what this plan adds is the naming that makes a channel read as 区域 / 现场推广 / 线下.
8. Regenerate types, Zod, OpenAPI and SDK tests.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not add a seller column to any price table.** The seller's price is its offer's base price.
- **Do not add a `context` parameter to the product read, and do not shadow it with a second route.** The context is request scope, and the read that prices it already exists.
- **Do not add a seller axis to `Spree::Pricing::Context`** or its cache key.
- **Do not build a second eligibility mechanism.** The area context narrows a price; `DeliveryZone` and the routing plan own eligibility.
- **Do not invent a fourth context in code.** A context is configuration; a new one is a row — and the seckill is deliberately not one, because `6.1-flash-sales.md` prices it as its own discount rather than as a channel.
- **Do not re-open the subsidy capability.** The offline context consumes the `subsidy` kind the membership plan carries.
- **Do not let a gem resolve the seller.** It comes from the request; if it is absent, a context that needs one fails loudly.
- **Do not let a price list's price be mistaken for the base price in reporting** — the Omnibus rule tracks base prices only (`5.4-6.0-eu-legal-compliance.md:75`), and any report this plan feeds must say which it is reading.

## Open Questions

Four of this plan's five questions are settled and sit in **Key Decisions**: three on 2026-09-18 — the seller axis is the offer variant's base price, the three contexts are channels, and `goods/getEstimate` belongs to the price preview (`6.1-store-api-miniprogram-gaps.md`) rather than here, since its payload carries no site, shop, address or special and that plan already calls the cartless preview its highest-value single addition — **and the fourth the same day: the context is request scope, so the product read is untouched.**

One is genuinely deferred: **who funds the offline context's price.** This plan assumes the operator does, through the `subsidy` kind the membership plan already carries; the alternative is that an offline activity's price is the seller's own concession, which changes nothing mechanically and everything about what the ledger says. What unblocks it: the operator's answer on who runs 线下 activities.
