## Summary

Spree 6.0 ships **no review model at all**, so this is a new domain rather than a
mapping. The word does appear in core and never means this: it is the marketplace's
listing review — a seller's submission awaiting an operator's decision
(`product.rb:48-53,218`, `seller.rb:34-39`) — a seller requirement's operator review
(`seller_requirements/operator_review.rb`), and a payout provider's review
(`payout_provider/base.rb`). Nothing rates a variant and nothing a customer wrote is
stored.

One order produces **two reviews that are not the same shape**, and the client
treats them as two blocks of one payload:

- **商品评价** — one per line item, on the seller's offer: a 1–5 star rating, a
  100-character body, up to 9 photos, an anonymity choice, and impression tags
  (`orderGoodsEvaluateVoList`, `pagesF/order/appraise/appraise.wxml:52-124`).
- **配送/服务评价** — one per order, on the delivery: 满意 or 不满意, a good or bad
  tag list, and a 100-character body (`orderDeliveryEvaluateVo`, `:10-51`).

They are written from one screen and submitted through two endpoints —
`order/orderEvaluate` carries the product reviews with the delivery review inline,
and `order/orderDeliveryEvaluate` carries the delivery review alone
(`components/order/evaluate/evaluate.js:170`).

And this gem owns the one earning channel the points plan deliberately does not:
**评价积分**. The points plan's ruling is that a gem earning points on something
that is not an order writes a subscriber and calls the ledger service
(`fork-decisions.md`, 2026-09-17), and a review is not a purchase, so both the amount
and the trigger live here.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **The subject is the seller's offer, not the product.** A review points at the
  variant, because the client's key is `skuSaleSiteId` — the offer — and the
  product's figure is an aggregate over its offers. `skuSaleSiteIds` is a
  comma-joined string of them, built by appending and trimming
  (`pagesA/goods-details/normal/normal.js:630`,
  `pagesA/components/common/on-site/format/format.js:117`): the product page holds
  the whole joined list and asks for one rating over it
  (`pagesA/static/behaviors/goodsDetailsBehavior.js:78-92`), while the write form
  holds **one per line item** (`appraise.js:236`). The product never receives a
  review directly.
- **A review is written against an order, and the order is the proof of purchase.**
  `order/getOrderEvaluateInfo` takes an `orderId` and answers what on that order is
  reviewable, and both submits name the order. So the write is nested under the
  order in the API, the way `returns` and `claims` already are
  (`spree/api/config/routes.rb:78-88`).
- **Both kinds are one table with a `kind`, not two tables.** They differ in
  subject and in rating range, not in structure: both carry a customer, an order, a
  rating, a body, tags and a status. The client sends them in one payload and hangs
  both off one order, so splitting them would duplicate a created_at-to-status
  lifecycle to gain nothing.
- **An impression selection may carry its own label.** The client lets a customer
  type a tag the vocabulary does not have — max 6 characters for a product
  (`components/order/custom-tag`, sent as `userImpressionName`) and max 100 for the
  delivery (`userEvalTagName`, `components/order/evaluate/evaluate.wxml:42-45`).
  The client models it as `{ type: 'default' | 'custom', checked, id, label }`
  (`appraise.js:295-300`, `evaluate.js:158-166`), so the join row is
  `impression_id` **or** a free-text `label`, and not a plain many-to-many.
- **Only the anonymity choice is stored on the review; every other decoration is
  computed at read time.** 匿名用户 is the customer's decision at write time and
  cannot be re-derived. 已注销 (`logOut`), the SVIP badge and the repeat-buyer badge
  (`isBuyAgain`) are read from the customer, the membership grant and the order
  history (`pagesA/comment/comment.wxml:36-40`) — freezing them would make a review
  wrong the moment the account is deleted or the customer's tier changes. The
  nickname is returned raw and masked by the client at 12 characters
  (`static/utils/utils.js:245-248`), so the server masks nothing.
- **An automatic good review is a row with a source, not a counter column.** The
  list folds them into a sentence — "已折叠{{defaultGoodNumStr}}条对您帮助不大的评价"
  (`comment.wxml:61`) — and excludes them from its records by passing
  `defaultContent: false` on every request (`comment.js:82-87`). A count nobody can
  drill into is a number the operator cannot correct, so they are rows the list
  filters out.
- **The reward cannot depend on what the review says.** The client states the
  amount **before the review exists**: `integralNum` rides the order payload
  (`static/behaviors/orderListBehavior.js:66`, read at `pages/order/order.js:188`)
  and the prompt renders it as "得{{N}}积分" beside 立即评价
  (`components/order/evaluate-medal/evaluate-medal.wxml:13`). So 评价积分 is a flat
  amount per order, credited through the points ledger service, and reversed by a
  ledger row rather than an edit.
- **No helpful votes are built.** The inventory's 评价体系 annotation names them —
  "订单/商品/配送评价 + 标签 + **有用票**" (`endpoints-spree-mapping.md:714`) — and
  **no endpoint carries one.** There is no vote control anywhere in the review
  pages, and no `有用` string in the client. Recorded here so a later reader does
  not think a feature was dropped.

## Design Details

### Models

```ruby
create_table :spree_reviews do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.references :customer, index: true          # null only for an automatic review
  t.references :order, index: true             # the proof of purchase
  t.references :line_item, index: true         # which line it came from; null for delivery
  t.references :variant, index: true           # the seller's offer; null for delivery
  t.string   :kind, null: false                # product / delivery
  t.string   :source, null: false              # customer / automatic
  t.string   :status, null: false              # published / withheld
  t.integer  :rating, null: false, default: 0  # 1-5 for a product review, 1-2 for delivery
  t.text     :body
  t.boolean  :anonymous, default: false, null: false
  t.datetime :published_at
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

- `Spree::Review` inherits `Spree.base_class`, includes `Spree::SingleStoreResource`
  and `Spree::Metadata`, uses `has_status` and `acts_as_paranoid`, and publishes
  lifecycle events. `rating` and `anonymous` are integer and boolean, so they carry
  defaults; `kind`, `source` and `status` are strings with no default, per the
  migration rules. `rating`'s default is `0` and means "not rated": the creating
  workflow always sets a real value, and the model validates inclusion in `1..5` for
  a product review and `1..2` for a delivery one, so `0` can never be saved.
- Uniqueness needs **two partial indexes, not one**: a product review is unique on
  `(order_id, line_item_id)` and a delivery review on `(order_id)` alone. A single
  index over both would not restrain the delivery review at all, because its
  `line_item_id` is null and nulls are distinct in a unique index. The model
  restrains the same pair with `scope: spree_base_uniqueness_scope`. The client
  cannot offer a second review of the same line either: once `orderEvaluate` is
  truthy the form renders read-only (`appraise.wxml:79-122`).
- `Spree::Review` carries `has_many_attached :images` with the public storage
  service, capped at **9** per review to match the uploader
  (`appraise.wxml:105`). The precedent is `Spree::ClaimLineItem`, which already
  attaches customer-supplied images (`spree/core/app/models/spree/claim_line_item.rb:14`).
  The upload path itself — signing, the bucket, the returned URL — is the
  platform-services plan's, not this one's.

```ruby
create_table :spree_review_impressions do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.string  :name, null: false
  t.string  :kind, null: false                 # product / delivery
  t.string  :polarity                          # good / bad; delivery only
  t.integer :position, null: false, default: 0
  t.string  :status, null: false
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

```ruby
create_table :spree_review_impression_selections do |t|
  t.references :review, index: true
  t.references :impression, index: true        # null when the customer typed it
  t.string :label                              # the typed text, or nil
  t.timestamps
end
```

- The `label` column is the whole reason this is not a join table with two foreign
  keys. A custom impression has no row in the vocabulary and must survive the
  vocabulary changing around it.
- The **delivery** tags are the same family with `kind: delivery` and a `polarity`,
  because the client's own endpoint returns two lists, good and bad
  (`appraise.js:146-178`), and the popup picks one by the rating
  (`evaluate.js:123`). One vocabulary, three client endpoints.
- Counts are read with a grouped count over the selections, indexed on
  `(impression_id)` and on the review's variant. The product page block is the
  postcard case for `Rails.cache`: it is the same figure for every visitor.

```ruby
create_table :spree_review_questions do |t|
  t.references :store, index: true
  t.string  :name, null: false                 # the question, e.g. 您对我的服务满意吗？
  t.string  :good_answer, null: false          # e.g. 满意
  t.string  :bad_answer, null: false           # e.g. 不满意
  t.integer :position, null: false, default: 0
  t.string  :status, null: false
  t.datetime :deleted_at
  t.timestamps
end
```

The order payload's `answerEvaluateVo` is a **row**, not a constant: it carries an
`id`, the question text and both button labels, and the submit echoes
`answerEvaluateId` back (`appraise.js:452-476`). That is operator-editable copy
with identity, and hardcoding "您对我的服务满意吗？" plus 满意/不满意 is the same
defect as a hardcoded tier list — the client already ships the default as a
fallback rather than as the only value (`components/order/evaluate/evaluate.js:150-156`).

### What each read returns

**The aggregate** (`trade_evaluate/getRecommendEvaluateNew`) answers with
`favorableRate`, `goodScore`, `recommendNumStr`, `tradeEvaluateImpressionListVoList`
and `emptyTradeEvaluate` (`pagesA/components/goods-details/area-comment/area-comment.wxml:8-22`),
and the product page renders the rate as a percentage with one decimal
(`goodsDetailsBehavior.js:78-92`) and the score as half stars. The list endpoint
returns the same rate and score plus `defaultGoodNum` and `defaultGoodNumStr`.

`favorableRate` is a ratio the client multiplies by 100 and truncates to four
characters, so it must be a fraction and not a percentage — sending it
pre-multiplied would render 4500%.

**A review row** answers `nickName` or `anonymous`, `userScore`, `evaluationTime`,
`evaluationContent` (rich text — the client parses HTML with `j-parse`), `picPath`,
`impressionNames`, `isSvip`, `isBuyAgain` and `logOut`
(`comment.wxml:29-57`).

**The write form** (`order/getOrderEvaluateInfo`) answers the order's reviewable
line items with their impression vocabulary, the delivery block, the question, and
`hasAnswerEval`. The delivery block is absent for third-party delivery —
`orderDeliveryEvaluateVo.thirdDelivery` hides the whole rider section
(`appraise.wxml:10`), because the rider is not the merchant's.

### Automatic good reviews

The client folds them rather than hiding them, and counts them into the aggregate,
so they are rows: `source: 'automatic'`, `status: 'published'`, no customer and no
body. A job creates one per completed order still unreviewed after an operator-set
grace period, and the per-line product reviews it creates carry the line's variant.
It must be idempotent and must use `ActiveJob::Continuable`, per `AGENTS.md`, since
it iterates orders.

This is the one place the plan creates content nobody wrote, so it states the
tradeoff: the favourable rate rises for orders that were never reviewed, which is
what the client's own "已折叠N条对您帮助不大的评价" admits. An operator can withhold
them like any other review, and the grace period is a preference, so the behaviour
can be turned off by setting it longer than any order lives.

### 评价积分

Credited through the points plan's ledger service, from a subscriber on this gem's
own event, with three properties the client fixes:

- **A flat amount per order**, because the client states it before the review
  exists. It is a `Spree::Store` preference here, and the value the order payload
  carries is that preference — so the number the customer is promised is the number
  the ledger will write.
- **Once per order.** `integralNum` rides the order, not the review, and the form
  goes read-only after the first submit, so a second submission cannot pay twice.
  The subscriber checks for an existing ledger entry for the order before crediting.
- **Reversed by a ledger row, never an edit.** Withholding a review, or a customer
  or operator deleting one, writes the reversal, matching the points plan's
  append-only rule.

The reward is not a function of the body or the photos, so "填写高质量的评价, 即可获得
奖励" (`appraise.js:106`) is a prompt rather than a term: the amount does not vary
with quality. Whether a refunded order keeps a review's reward is left open below.

### What a customer cannot do

There is no report control, no vote, no reply and no edit after submitting. The
client's read-only rendering of an already-reviewed order is the whole enforcement
(`appraise.wxml:79-122`), so the model refuses the edit rather than trusting the
screen. Withholding is the operator's, through the admin.

## Store API Surface

| Method and path | Replaces |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/v3/store/reviews` | `trade_evaluate/page` — the collection, filtered by offer, impression id and rating, paginated |
| `GET /api/v3/store/reviews/summary` | `trade_evaluate/getRecommendEvaluateNew` — the product page block |
| `GET /api/v3/store/review_impressions` | `trade_evaluate_impression/getSkuEvaluateList`, `.../getSkuImpress` and `orderDeliveryEvalTag/getDeliveryEvalTagByType` — one vocabulary, `kind` and `polarity` as filters, `with_counts` for the list |
| `GET /api/v3/store/orders/:order_id/review` | `order/getOrderEvaluateInfo` — what on this order is reviewable and what was already said |
| `POST /api/v3/store/orders/:order_id/reviews` | `order/orderEvaluate` and `order/orderDeliveryEvaluate` — `kind` selects which; a `product` create takes the line items and may carry the delivery block inline, a `delivery` create takes the delivery block alone |

Five resources for eight client endpoints, on the same reasoning the points plan
used for nine into five: the vocabulary's three callers differ in filters, not in
shape, and the two submits differ in `kind`. `reviews/summary` is a nested
controller rather than an action on the collection, which is what `AGENTS.md` asks
for instead of a custom action. The write nests under the order because both the
order-scoped lookup and the create need one, and core already nests `returns`,
`claims` and `tax_identifier` there (`spree/api/config/routes.rb:78-88`).

**The order payload gains three fields**, and this is a core-API change rather than
a gem-local one: the order list reads `orderEvaluate`, `orderDeliveryEvaluate` and
`integralNum` off every order row (`orderListBehavior.js:66`) to decide whether to
show 去评价 or 已评价 (`static/utils/order.js:193,200`) and whether to show the
reward badge. The gem registers its own order serializer through
`Spree.api.order_serializer` rather than patching core's.

Admin gets the vocabulary and the questions as resources, and a withhold action
that writes a status through a workflow.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not rate the product; rate the offer.** A review points at the variant. A
  product-level rating column would have to be reconciled with the aggregate every
  time a seller's offer is added or withdrawn, which is the defect the master
  catalog model was built to avoid.
- **Do not freeze the decorations.** 已注销, the SVIP badge and the repeat-buyer
  badge are read at render time from the customer, the membership grant and the
  order history. Storing them makes every review a snapshot of the day it was
  written.
- **Do not store a review count or an average on the product.** The client's own
  figure spans every offer of the product, and the set of offers changes. Read it,
  and cache it.
- **Do not let the reward vary with the review's content.** The amount is stated on
  the order before the review exists, so scoring the body would make the promise on
  the order a lie.
- **Do not pay the reward twice.** It is once per order, guarded by the ledger
  entry, not by the review row — a withholding and a re-publish must not mint a
  second credit.
- **Do not reverse a reward by editing a ledger row.** Refunds and withholding
  write a reversal, the same rule the points plan carries.
- **Do not build helpful votes, replies or a report control.** No endpoint carries
  them and no screen offers them. The inventory's 有用票 annotation is the only
  place they appear.
- **Do not put the review write anywhere but under the order.** It needs the order
  as proof of purchase, and the order is what the customer can address by token or
  session.
- **Do not read a review out of `order/*` on the strength of the prefix.** Three
  endpoints in this set are filed under §订单 and belong here. Grading by domain
  heading misfiled them, the same way it misfiled the membership plan's 16
  endpoints under 优惠券与促销 and the points plan's `vip/getUserLevelRecord`
  under §会员.
- **Wire the gem in three places or its specs never run:** the starter
  `server/Gemfile` path block, `.github/workflows/tests.yml`, and
  `scripts/test/rspec`.

## Open Questions

1. **What makes a review favourable?** `favorableRate` is rendered as a percentage
   beside the average score, and the client does not say where the line is. A fixed
   4-and-above threshold is simple and wrong for a delivery review rated 1 or 2. An
   operator-set threshold is a preference. The automatic good reviews count in this
   figure, so the choice moves the displayed rate on every product.
2. **Does a refund take the reward back?** The points plan reverses the points an
   order earned when the order is refunded, through `consume_return`. A review's
   reward is not earned on the order's money, so this plan's default is that it
   follows the **review's** state rather than the order's — but a customer who is
   refunded in full and keeps both the review and the points is a case worth ruling.
3. **Is there a window in which a review is editable?** The client offers none, and
   the model refuses the edit. Most review systems allow a short correction window.
   Nothing in the restored source asks for one, so the default is that there is
   none.
